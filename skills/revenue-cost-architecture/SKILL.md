# Revenue & Cost Architecture

## Purpose

Design the revenue logic and cost structure of a venture's business model. This skill covers revenue stream identification, pricing model selection, unit economics calculation, and cost structure analysis. It ensures the business model has a coherent theory of value capture, not just value creation.

## When to Use

Use this skill after the value proposition is defined and the customer segment is identified. Use it whenever the model needs a revenue logic, pricing thesis, or cost structure. It is essential before any financial projection work.

## Process

### Step 1: Revenue Streams

Identify all revenue streams and classify each by type:

| Revenue Stream Type | Description | Example |
|---------------------|-------------|--------|
| **Asset sale** | One-time sale of a physical or digital product | A chair, a software license |
| **Usage fee** | Customer pays per use | Hotel room, cell phone minutes |
| **Subscription** | Recurring fee for ongoing access | Netflix, SaaS |
| **Renting/Leasing** | Temporary exclusive access | Car rental, equipment lease |
| **Licensing** | Fee for using protected IP | Patent royalties, brand licensing |
| **Brokerage** | Fee for intermediating two sides | Real estate commission, Uber |
| **Advertising** | Fee for delivering audience attention | Google Ads, social media |

For each stream, specify:
- **Payer:** Who pays?
- **Price point:** How much, in what unit?
- **Frequency:** One-time, recurring (daily/weekly/monthly/annual), per-use?
- **Expected revenue at maturity:** Estimate at 3-5 years based on reasonable penetration assumptions.

### Step 2: Pricing Logic

Select a pricing mechanism and justify it:

- **Cost-plus:** Price = cost + target margin. Simple but ignores willingness to pay.
- **Value-based:** Price = a fraction of the value created for the customer. Maximizes capture but requires knowing customer value.
- **Competition-based:** Price = aligned with or slightly below competitors. Safe but commoditizing.
- **Dynamic:** Price changes based on demand, time, or customer segment. Complex but optimizes yield.
- **Freemium:** Free basic tier, paid premium tier. Good for adoption, requires conversion math.
- **Pay-what-you-want:** Customer chooses price. Rarely viable outside certain contexts.

**Pricing test:** "If you doubled the price, would customers still buy? If you halved it, would they buy twice as much?" Answers reveal elasticity and headroom.

### Step 3: Unit Economics

Calculate the unit-level metrics. Define the unit of analysis (one transaction, one customer, one subscription year):

- **Average Revenue Per Unit (ARPU):** Total revenue / number of units.
- **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) per unit:** Direct costs to deliver one unit.
- **Gross Margin per unit:** ARPU - COGS. As a percentage: (ARPU - COGS) / ARPU × 100.
- **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):** Total sales & marketing costs / number of new customers acquired.
- **Lifetime Value (LTV):** ARPU × average customer lifespan (in periods) × retention rate adjustment.
- **LTV:CAC ratio:** Target > 3:1 for healthy models.
- **Payback period:** Months (or years) to recover CAC from gross margin.

### Step 4: Cost Structure Analysis

Identify the venture's cost structure type:

- **Cost-driven:** Minimize costs wherever possible. Lean cost structure, low margin, high volume.
- **Value-driven:** Focus on value creation. Premium cost structure, high margin, lower volume.

List the top 5 cost categories and classify each as fixed or variable. Determine whether the business benefits from economies of scale (cost per unit declines with volume) or economies of scope (cost per unit declines with variety).

### Step 5: Output

Produce a structured revenue and cost architecture summary:

- **Revenue Streams Table:** Type, payer, price point, frequency, expected revenue at maturity.
- **Pricing Rationale:** Selected mechanism and justification (2-3 sentences).
- **Unit Economics:** ARPU, COGS/unit, Gross Margin%, CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC ratio, Payback period.
- **Cost Structure:** Type (cost-driven vs value-driven), top 5 cost categories with fixed/variable classification.
- **Assumptions:** List all key assumptions behind revenue projections and cost estimates (e.g., conversion rate, churn rate, production volume).
- **Sensitivity:** What happens if ARPU is 20% lower, COGS 20% higher, or CAC 50% higher? State whether the model survives.
