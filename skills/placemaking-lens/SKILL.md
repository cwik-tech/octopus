# Placemaking Lens

## Purpose

Stephen Ross built Hudson Yards, Time Warner Center, and the Related portfolio by refusing to treat buildings as the product. The product is the place — the composition of uses, the public realm, the programming, and the partnerships that create a destination people want to be in. This skill forces the crew to apply the placemaking lens to every deal and identify where the place thesis is strong, weak, or absent.

## Protocol

### Step 1: Write the Place Thesis

In one paragraph, describe what this site becomes as a place in year 15. Walk a visitor through it: what do they see, where do they enter, where do they linger, what are they doing, who are they with, what draws them back? If you cannot write this paragraph, the deal has no place thesis — only a building thesis.

### Step 2: Map the Mix Reinforcement

For each use on the site (residential, office, retail, hotel, cultural, civic, parking, open space), state:

- The square footage
- The customer profile
- The time-of-day pattern (when this use is busy vs. quiet)
- How it feeds the other uses (office workers feed lunch retail; residents feed evening retail; cultural anchors feed weekend hotel demand)

Compute a rough reinforcement score: does the mix keep the place active across days, nights, and weekends, or are there dead hours? A place with dead hours is a collection of buildings.

### Step 3: Name the Anchor Strategy

List the anchor tenants the place requires — the commitments that make the rest of the uses viable. For each:

- Named candidate (not "a technology tenant" — a specific company or institution)
- Current stage of conversation (speculation, early interest, term sheet, signed)
- What they need from you and what you need from them
- What happens to the place thesis if they walk

Without named anchors in real conversation, the thesis is aspiration. With them, it becomes a plan.

### Step 4: Commit the Public Realm Budget

State the public realm investment as a percentage of total project budget. The target is 10% or more; anything less should be explicitly justified. Break it down:

- Streets, sidewalks, plazas, parks
- Lighting, furniture, paving, signage
- Art, water features, landscape
- Programming reserves (events, activations, management)
- Long-term maintenance endowment

Public space without a maintenance endowment degrades within a decade. Budget the endowment.

### Step 5: Define the Programming Plan

Programming is what happens in the public realm after opening day. State:

- The first 12 months of programming (events, activations, partnerships)
- Who runs programming (in-house, partner, BID-like entity)
- The annual programming budget as a line item, not an afterthought
- The measurement: foot traffic, dwell time, repeat visits, press, social attention

A place without programming is a place that opens beautifully and empties by year two.

### Step 6: Map the Public-Partnership Relationships

Identify every public-sector party with leverage over the project:

- Planning department
- Zoning board or commission
- Transportation agency (if transit or streets are involved)
- Economic development office
- Community boards and civic associations
- Environmental and historic review bodies

For each, state the current relationship status and who on your team owns it. Public partnerships are built over years of showing up, not negotiated in the week before a hearing.

### Step 7: Run the Architecture Test

The building that defines a neighborhood for 50 years is not the building that saved 3% on construction. Ask:

- Who is the architect, and is their body of work consistent with the ambition of this place?
- Where in the budget is the architecture being protected from value engineering, and where is it exposed?
- What is the one detail that will be photographed for the next 30 years, and what does it cost?

If there is no photographed detail, there is no architectural thesis.

### Step 8: Write the Placemaking Verdict

> "Place thesis: [year-15 paragraph]. Mix reinforcement: [score and dead hours]. Anchor strategy: [named, stage]. Public realm budget: [X% of total, Y for endowment]. Programming plan: [owner, budget, measurement]. Public partnerships: [status and owner]. Architecture: [signature detail]. [Pursue at full ambition / narrow scope / walk]."

## When to Apply

Every development, redevelopment, mixed-use acquisition, and large-scale repositioning. Also any acquisition where the upside story depends on the surrounding area changing character.

## When Not to Apply

Small, single-use, operating-asset trades where the place is already fully formed and the deal is a financial bet on the existing cash flow. Even then, note that the place's trajectory over the hold period is a variable, not a constant.
