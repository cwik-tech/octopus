# Twenty-Year Land Plan

## Purpose

Donald Bren has held much of Irvine Ranch for more than fifty years, shaping it through master plans that integrate land use, infrastructure, schools, open space, and phasing. The result is a portfolio whose value is inseparable from the place it created. This skill forces the crew to ask whether a deal is being underwritten on a short horizon when a long-horizon plan would produce a structurally better outcome — and to sketch that plan explicitly before making the decision.

## Protocol

### Step 1: Declare the Intended Horizon

State, in writing, the intended hold period:

- Short (under 5 years) — merchant build or trade
- Medium (5–10 years) — classic fund hold
- Long (10–20 years) — permanent capital strategy
- Generational (20+ years) — dynastic, family, or sovereign-style hold

Many deals are structured short because the capital is short, not because the opportunity is short. Name the distinction.

### Step 2: Sketch the Year-20 Vision

If this deal were held for 20 years, describe in one paragraph what the asset, the land, and the surrounding area would look like. Not a financial model — a place. Where are the buildings? What uses? What open space? What connection to transit, schools, civic life? What is the relationship to the next parcel over, and who owns that parcel?

If you cannot describe it, you do not have a 20-year plan — you have a bet on exit cap rates.

### Step 3: Identify the Adjacent Land and the Long Control Strategy

List the adjacent parcels whose fate will materially affect your parcel over 20 years. For each:

- Current owner and likely disposition
- Current zoning and upzoning probability
- What happens to your asset if the adjacent land develops badly

Then state: is your strategy to control the adjacent land over time (assemblage), to influence its use through entitlement and civic relationships, or to accept that you cannot control it? All three are legitimate; pretending you do not need a strategy is not.

### Step 4: Map the Public Infrastructure Dependencies

List the public infrastructure on which 20-year value depends: roads, transit, water, sewer, schools, public safety, parks. For each:

- Current state
- Planned or proposed improvements with realistic timelines
- Who pays (public, private, public-private, exaction)
- What your project contributes

If any dependency is weak and without a plan, 20-year value is at risk and nobody has been designated to protect it.

### Step 5: Design the Phasing Plan

State how the parcel develops over 20 years in phases, with:

- What gets built first and why
- What the second-phase trigger is (absorption, market condition, infrastructure delivery)
- What remains undeveloped and why
- How each phase leaves optionality for the next

Phasing is the core of long-horizon value creation. A plan that builds everything day one is not a 20-year plan — it is a merchant plan with a long tail.

### Step 6: Name the Stewardship Obligations

Long holds require stewardship — of the physical asset, of the community, of the public trust. State:

- Who maintains the asset to institutional standards over decades
- What the reinvestment reserve looks like (per-square-foot annual set-aside)
- What the community obligations are (schools, affordability, open space) and who enforces them
- What the governance structure is if ownership changes within the family or institution

Stewardship is not soft; it is the condition under which long-term value is actually realized.

### Step 7: Write the Land-Plan Verdict

> "Intended horizon: [X]. Year-20 vision: [one paragraph]. Adjacent land strategy: [control / influence / accept]. Public infrastructure dependencies: [list and gaps]. Phasing plan: [phases]. Stewardship structure: [named]. [Proceed as generational hold / proceed with shorter horizon and accept the compromises / walk]."

## When to Apply

Every development, entitlement, land acquisition, and master-planned community deal. Also stabilized assets where the land itself is the dominant value component.

## When Not to Apply

Pure operating asset trades where the land is functionally incidental (credit-tenant lease on a stabilized building, for example). Even there, note that the long-horizon question should be asked once per decade to check whether the character of the asset has changed.

## Integration With Other Skills

This skill addresses generational survivability and placemaking. Combine with Ross's placemaking lens (the physical and programmatic vision), Zell's supply pipeline check (whether the medium-term market supports the phasing), and Drucker's enterprise audit (stewardship is an operating capability).
