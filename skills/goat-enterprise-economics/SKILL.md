# Goat Enterprise Economics

## Purpose

Convert commercial meat-goat production records and market assumptions into transparent economic decisions. Focus on contribution margin, cash needs, risk, and the biological drivers that determine profitability.

## Method

1. **Define the decision.** State the decision to be made: retain versus sell, breed more does, purchase replacements, change feed program, alter kidding season, invest in infrastructure, or select a marketing channel.
2. **Set the unit of analysis.** Use the unit that best exposes the decision: per exposed doe, per doe kidding, per kid weaned, per kid marketed, per pound sold, per group, or whole enterprise.
3. **Build the production bridge.** Trace exposed does to successful breeding, kids born, kids weaned, kids marketed, sale weight, price received, and revenue. Do not allow a favorable price to mask weak survival or growth.
4. **Separate costs.** Distinguish variable costs from fixed or overhead costs and cash costs from noncash costs. Identify costs that actually change under the alternative being considered.
5. **Compare channels on net return.** Compare expected price, sale weight and uniformity requirements, commissions, transport, shrink, sorting, timing, buyer reliability, payment terms, volume needs, risk of rejected animals, labor, and relationship value.
6. **Calculate break-even and sensitivity.** Test the assumptions most likely to move the outcome: sale price, pounds sold, kid survival, gain, feed cost, forage replacement cost, labor, and mortality or cull rate.
7. **Make a staged recommendation.** Distinguish low-cost measurement improvements from changes requiring capital, land, or operating risk.

## Core Calculations

Use transparent formulas and label assumptions.

- **Kids marketed per exposed doe** = kids marketed ÷ does exposed.
- **Pounds marketed per exposed doe** = total live pounds sold ÷ does exposed.
- **Average daily gain** = (ending weight − starting weight) ÷ days between weights.
- **Revenue per exposed doe** = total goat-sale revenue ÷ does exposed.
- **Variable cost per kid marketed** = attributable variable costs ÷ kids marketed.
- **Feed cost per pound of gain** = attributable feed cost ÷ pounds gained.
- **Contribution margin** = revenue − variable costs.
- **Contribution margin per exposed doe** = total contribution margin ÷ does exposed.
- **Break-even price per pound** = (variable costs + allocated costs, if included − other revenue) ÷ pounds expected to sell.
- **Break-even pounds sold** = costs to recover ÷ expected net price per pound, adjusted for relevant per-head variable costs.

State whether land, unpaid labor, depreciation, interest, facilities, and management are included. A partial budget answers incremental-change questions; a full enterprise budget answers whole-business viability questions.

## Minimum Enterprise Records

| Category | Fields |
|---|---|
| Inventory | beginning and ending counts by class, purchases, sales, deaths, culls |
| Reproduction | does exposed, breeding groups, kids born, kids weaned, losses |
| Growth | weights, dates, days on feed or forage program, sale weight |
| Revenue | buyer, date, head count, live weight, price basis, gross revenue, deductions, net proceeds |
| Feed and forage | purchased feed quantities and cost, forage tests, pasture costs, hay inventory, waste observations |
| Health and labor | veterinary and health expenses by group, hired labor, estimated owner labor separately |
| Capital and overhead | repairs, utilities, equipment, depreciation, interest, insurance, facilities, land costs |

## Sensitivity Rules

- Use at least a base, downside, and upside case for material decisions.
- Change one assumption at a time first, then combine plausible downside assumptions.
- Do not use a single spot price as an annual forecast. Use dated, cited market information when current price data matters.
- Explicitly identify nonfinancial constraints: welfare, labor, pasture carrying capacity, facilities, and buyer requirements.

## Output Format

Return:

1. decision and economic unit;
2. assumptions, dates, and information gaps;
3. production bridge and cost classification;
4. option comparison with net-return logic;
5. break-even and sensitivity table;
6. recommendation, downside risks, and next record to collect; and
7. citations for current prices, programs, or regulatory requirements when material.
