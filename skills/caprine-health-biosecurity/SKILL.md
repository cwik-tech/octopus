# Caprine Health and Biosecurity

## Purpose

Help a meat-goat operation reduce preventable health and biosecurity risk through management, records, veterinary partnership, and clear escalation pathways. This skill is educational and does not diagnose, prescribe, or replace veterinary care.

## Safety Boundary

- Do not diagnose disease or state that a particular condition is present.
- Do not prescribe drugs, doses, withdrawal intervals, treatment duration, or extra-label use.
- Do not recommend product use without current label, regulatory, and veterinarian review where applicable.
- Direct the operator to their veterinarian for animal-specific decisions, diagnostics, vaccination protocols, parasite-treatment decisions, and all emergencies.

## Immediate Veterinary Escalation Triggers

Advise urgent contact with a veterinarian or emergency service for difficult or prolonged labor, severe respiratory distress, collapse, seizures, inability to stand, severe pain, uncontrolled bleeding, inability to nurse in a newborn, sudden neurologic signs, suspected toxic exposure, rapid dehydration, multiple acute illnesses, unexplained deaths, or a rapidly spreading outbreak. Follow local animal-health authority instructions for reportable-disease concerns.

## Method

1. **Clarify the event and urgency.** Determine whether this is prevention planning, an individual concern, a cluster, a mortality event, a new-arrival issue, or a market/shipping issue. Apply escalation triggers first.
2. **Describe exposure pathways.** Map animal movement, purchase sources, shared equipment, visitors, fence-line contact, transport, feed and water, manure, wildlife, dogs or predators, and movement between age groups.
3. **Review preventive foundations.** Assess clean water, feed storage, ventilation, stocking density, bedding and manure management, weather protection, handling stress, nutrition consistency, observation routines, and records.
4. **Design quarantine and introduction controls.** Establish a physically separate area where feasible, dedicated tools and footwear, last-in-workflow handling, observation and record procedures, and a veterinarian-led arrival assessment plan.
5. **Design parasite-risk management.** Use a program that combines veterinary-guided diagnostics and treatment decisions with grazing management, body-condition and performance monitoring, contamination reduction, and individual records. Avoid calendar-only or one-size-fits-all advice.
6. **Plan vaccination discussions.** Identify herd goals, local disease risks, buyer or show requirements, kidding schedule, and current label/regulatory conditions; formulate questions for the herd veterinarian rather than selecting products or schedules independently.
7. **Contain potential outbreaks.** Pause nonessential movements, separate affected groups when safe, preserve accurate observations and timelines, intensify hygiene, contact a veterinarian, and follow veterinary or authority instructions before resuming normal movement.
8. **Review and learn.** Use case, mortality, cull, treatment, and exposure records to identify recurring patterns and revise prevention controls.

## Biosecurity Controls

| Risk point | Control | Record |
|---|---|---|
| Purchased or returning animals | Quarantine and veterinarian-led entry plan | source, arrival date, observations, clearance decision |
| Visitors and service providers | Limit access; clean footwear and equipment procedures | visitor date, purpose, areas accessed |
| Shared equipment | Clean and separate where possible; work healthy groups first | equipment movement and sanitation log |
| Sick or vulnerable animals | Safe separation and observation process | animal ID, signs observed, time, veterinarian contact |
| Transport | Clean transport, source/destination controls, movement records | date, carrier, animals, destination |
| Feed and water | Protect from contamination and monitor supply | source, maintenance, concerns |

## Parasite-Control Planning Questions

Use these questions to structure a veterinarian-supported program:

- What diagnostics and sampling plan best fits this farm and season?
- Which groups are most vulnerable based on age, production stage, performance, and grazing exposure?
- What grazing, forage-height, stocking, and movement practices raise or reduce exposure?
- What records identify animals with repeated poor performance or recurring concern?
- What current local resistance patterns, labels, and veterinary guidance apply?
- How will efficacy and program outcomes be evaluated rather than assumed?

## Record Fields

- animal or group ID, date, age class, location or paddock, observations, onset timing, body condition or weight trend, exposure history, actions directed by veterinarian, diagnostics, outcome, mortality or cull reason, and veterinarian or authority communication.

## Output Format

Return a concise prevention and response plan with:

1. urgency assessment and veterinary escalation triggers;
2. likely exposure pathways stated as possibilities, not diagnoses;
3. immediate containment and welfare actions;
4. prevention controls and owner assignments;
5. records to capture; and
6. current authoritative sources when local rules, disease alerts, labels, or products matter.
