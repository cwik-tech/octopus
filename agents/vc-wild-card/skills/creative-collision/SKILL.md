# Creative Collision Skill

Identical in spirit to the consulting Crazy Man's creative-ideation skill, but adapted for investment evaluation. The goal is to produce perspectives no structured agent would generate.

## When to Use

Selectively — when the committee is converging too fast, when the deal thesis feels too comfortable, or when the sector is crowded and conventional thinking produces conventional conclusions.

---

## Collision Protocol

### Technique 1 — The Inversion Spiral

Take the investment thesis and invert it. Then invert the inversion. Go 3-4 levels deep.

**Example:**
- Thesis: "This company will win because they're first to market."
- Inversion 1: "What if being first is actually a disadvantage — they're educating the market for free and a fast follower will capitalize."
- Inversion 2: "What if the fast follower advantage is a myth in this specific market — what if customer loyalty forms early and is irreversible."
- Inversion 3: "What if customer loyalty is irreversible but it locks in a suboptimal solution — and the real winner offers migration tools to break the lock-in."

Each inversion surfaces a different strategic reality.

### Technique 2 — Wrong Domain Import

Pick a domain that has nothing to do with startups or investing. Extract a mechanism and force-fit it onto the deal.

**Templates:**
- "What would a biologist see when looking at this company's growth pattern?" (Parasitic growth? Symbiotic? Cancerous? Healthy tissue formation?)
- "What would a military strategist say about this company's competitive positioning?" (Guerrilla warfare? Siege? Flanking? Overextended supply lines?)
- "What would an epidemiologist think about the product's adoption pattern?" (Superspreader dynamics? Quarantine effects? Herd immunity?)

### Technique 3 — The Scale Warp

Take the company and imagine it at 1000x scale or 0.001x scale:

- **1000x:** "If this company had 10 million customers instead of 10,000, what would break first? What would become trivially easy?"
- **0.001x:** "If this company had exactly 10 customers, what would the business look like? Would it be profitable? Would those 10 customers be ecstatic?"

Extreme scale reveals structural properties invisible at current scale.

### Technique 4 — The Time Scramble

- "What if this company existed in 2005? What tools would they use? What market would they serve?"
- "What if we're evaluating this company in 2035? Is this technology ancient or prescient?"
- "What if this problem solved itself in 3 years — what made it go away?"

### Technique 5 — The Uncomfortable Combination

Take two unrelated aspects of the deal and combine them in a way nobody would:

- "What if the biggest risk is that the product works TOO well — customers solve their problem and leave?"
- "What if we're the wrong investor — what if this company needs a corporate strategic investor, not a financial one?"
- "What if the founder's weakness is actually the company's strength — what if being non-technical forces them to hire better engineers?"

---

## Output Format

Deliver as a barrage of sparks, not a structured analysis:

- **3+ collision ideas** with what you combined and what emerged
- **2+ inversions** with what you flipped and what appeared
- **2+ imports** with source domain and transferred mechanism
- **The One That Scares You** — the idea that feels different, more dangerous, more resonant
- **Closing provocation** — a question or statement designed to prevent comfortable convergence
