# Extended Collision Techniques

## Purpose

Additional creative collision, inversion, and import techniques beyond those in the SKILL.md. Use when the standard five techniques haven't produced sufficiently provocative insights.

---

## Advanced Inversion Techniques

### The Founder Inversion

Take the thesis about the team and completely invert it:
- "The founder's biggest strength is their technical depth" → "What if technical depth is actually a liability — they're building for engineers, not for customers?"
- "The team is first-time founders, which is a risk" → "What if first-time founders have an advantage here — they don't know what's 'impossible' so they try things experienced founders wouldn't?"
- "The founder's previous exit was modest ($20M)" → "What if a modest exit taught them more about capital efficiency than a billion-dollar exit would have?"

### The Market Inversion

Take the market thesis and flip it:
- "The market is growing 30% annually" → "What if the market growth is actually slowing and we're looking at the last generation of lagging adopters, not the first generation of mainstream adopters?"
- "The market is too small for VC" → "What if this 'small' market is actually the seed of something much larger — like AWS starting as internal infrastructure?"
- "This market is commoditizing" → "What if commoditization of the base layer creates massive value in the layer above — and this company occupies that layer?"

### The Product Inversion

- "The product is too simple" → "What if simplicity is the product? What if every competitor's complexity is their weakness?"
- "The product is too complex" → "What if complexity creates a moat — no one else is willing to solve this problem because it's too hard?"
- "The product isn't differentiated" → "What if the lack of differentiation IS the differentiation — a standard, interoperable solution in a world of proprietary lock-in?"

## Extended Domain Import Techniques

### Import: Evolutionary Biology

| Biological Concept | VC Application |
|---|---|
| **Adaptive radiation** | After a platform shift (extinction event), many new species (startups) emerge to fill empty niches. Are we in such a period? |
| **Parasitism** | Is this company's business model parasitic on a host platform? What happens when the host develops immunity (API restrictions, competing features)? |
| **Symbiosis** | Is the company in a symbiotic relationship with its ecosystem? True symbiosis is the strongest competitive position. |
| **Punctuated equilibrium** | Markets don't evolve gradually — they have long periods of stability punctuated by rapid change. Is this company positioned for the next punctuation? |
| **Red Queen hypothesis** | In a competitive market, you have to keep running just to stay in place. Is this company evolving faster than its competitors, or just running to survive? |

### Import: Physics

| Physics Concept | VC Application |
|---|---|
| **Phase transition** | Markets can undergo sudden phase transitions (liquid → solid). What would a market phase transition look like here — and is this company positioned for it or against it? |
| **Entropy** | Systems tend toward disorder. Every company fights entropy (complexity, technical debt, organizational dysfunction). Does this company have the energy to fight entropy at scale? |
| **Critical mass** | What's the critical mass for this network/marketplace/community? How close are they? What happens if they don't reach it? |
| **Resonance** | Is this company's growth resonating with an external frequency (market trend, behavioral shift)? Resonance amplifies small forces into large effects. |

### Import: Game Theory

| Game Theory Concept | VC Application |
|---|---|
| **Prisoner's dilemma** | Are the company and its competitors in a prisoner's dilemma? (Both would benefit from cooperation but incentives drive competition.) Will price wars destroy value for everyone? |
| **Chicken game** | Two competitors both investing heavily — who blinks first? What's the company's appetite for a war of attrition? |
| **Coordination game** | Does the market need coordination (standards, interoperability)? Is this company the Schelling point — the natural coordination point? |
| **Signaling** | What signals is the company sending to the market? What signals are competitors sending? Are these signals credible? |

### Import: Urban Planning

| Urban Planning Concept | VC Application |
|---|---|
| **Induced demand** | Building highways creates more traffic, not less. What if this product creates more demand for the problem it solves? (Support tools that create dependency.) |
| **Gentrification** | The startup moves into an underserved neighborhood (market), improves it, then gets priced out by larger players who follow. Is this company the gentrifier or the beneficiary? |
| **Zoning** | What are the invisible "zoning laws" of this market? Regulations, norms, platform rules that constrain where you can build? |
| **Public goods** | Is this company creating a public good (open-source, free tier) that benefits everyone but is hard to monetize? |

## Provocative Combination Techniques

### The "What If We're Wrong About Everything?" Test

List the 5 most fundamental assumptions in the deal thesis. Then assume ALL of them are wrong simultaneously. What business would survive that? Is there a kernel of value that persists even when everything else fails?

### The Competitor Empathy Exercise

Pretend you're the CEO of the company's biggest competitor. You just heard about this startup. What do you do? What would scare you? What would make you laugh? The competitor's reaction reveals more about the startup's threat level than our own analysis.

### The Customer Defection Scenario

Imagine the startup's best customer gets a call from a competitor offering 50% lower price, 80% of the features, and free migration. Does the customer switch? If yes, the moat is shallow regardless of what we call it. If no, why not? That "why not" is the real moat.

### The "Obituary" Exercise

Write the startup's obituary — it's 2029 and the company failed. What went wrong? What was the cause of death? This exercise forces the committee to articulate failure modes they're unconsciously avoiding.

### The Fund Anti-Portfolio Exercise

Imagine it's 5 years from now and this company is a $10B outcome. We passed. We're writing the anti-portfolio entry. What did we miss? What bias caused us to say no? This exercise counteracts the natural conservatism of committee deliberation.

## Output Amplification

When using these techniques, look for:

1. **The uncomfortable insight** — the idea that makes the committee squirm. That's usually where the signal is.
2. **The "obvious" thing nobody said** — if the import or inversion reveals something that seems obvious in retrospect, it was probably being avoided.
3. **The contradiction** — when two techniques produce opposite conclusions, the truth is usually in the tension between them.
4. **The new question** — the best collision output isn't an answer but a question nobody had thought to ask.
