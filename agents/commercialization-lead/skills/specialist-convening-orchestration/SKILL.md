# Specialist Convening and Orchestration

## Purpose

Bring the right specialists into conversation at the right moment. Make visible what each specialist is assuming about what the other specialists will deliver. Force explicit reconciliation when domains conflict.

## When to Use

Use this skill when:
- Assembling the core commercialization team.
- Specialists are working in silos and not communicating dependencies.
- One domain makes a decision that affects another domain (but they haven't talked).
- Assumptions are implicit instead of explicit ("Regulatory will be fine with this" — have you asked?)
- Hidden dependencies emerge during planning.

## Specialist Roles and Their Key Concerns

### Technical Lead

**Owns:** Product design, technical feasibility, prototyping, scale-up engineering.

**Assumptions often make about others:**
- "Regulatory will be okay with this approach." (Have they confirmed with regulatory?)
- "Manufacturing can handle this design." (Have they confirmed with manufacturing?)
- "Design is locked when regulatory needs it." (When exactly does regulatory need it?)

**Questions to surface:**
- What technical constraints will regulatory impose? (Sterilization method? Shelf life? Safety margin?)
- What manufacturing constraints will limit design choices? (Material sourcing? Process capability?)
- When must the design be locked to allow manufacturing and regulatory to proceed in parallel?

### Regulatory Lead

**Owns:** Regulatory pathway, compliance strategy, dossier preparation, regulatory approval.

**Assumptions they often make about others:**
- "We'll figure out manufacturing data requirements after we meet with regulatory." (Manufacturing needs to start now to have data ready.)
- "Clinical trial will show safety/efficacy in time for our approval deadline." (Is the trial timeline aligned with regulatory timeline?)
- "Design is final." (But technical team says it may evolve during trials.)

**Questions to surface:**
- What does regulatory need from manufacturing to approve? (Sterilization validation? Biocompatibility data? Manufacturing controls?) When do they need it?
- What does regulatory need from clinical trials? (Phase I, II, III? Sample size? Indications?) How long will that take?
- If regulatory classification changes (e.g., Class II → Class III), how does that affect the overall timeline?

### Manufacturing Lead

**Owns:** Manufacturing process design, scale-up, process validation, cost modeling, supply chain.

**Assumptions they often make about others:**
- "Design will be locked by [date]." (Is that realistic given technical and regulatory needs?)
- "Regulatory will approve our process design." (Have they engaged regulatory early?)
- "Suppliers can deliver components by [date]." (What if a supplier delays?)

**Questions to surface:**
- What manufacturing data does regulatory require? When must that data be generated?
- What design changes does manufacturing foresee once they see the prototype in production context?
- What is the risk profile of the chosen suppliers? What is the Plan B if a supplier fails?

### Market Lead

**Owns:** Market sizing, customer validation, go-to-market strategy, market timing.

**Assumptions they often make about others:**
- "Product will launch by Q3." (Will regulatory and manufacturing actually deliver by then?)
- "Customers will adopt immediately." (What is the actual procurement timeline?)
- "Market window will be open indefinitely." (What are the closure conditions?)

**Questions to surface:**
- What is the customer buying cycle? Does it align with the planned launch date?
- What regulatory claims does the customer need to see before they will buy?
- If launch slips by 6 months, does the market opportunity disappear?

### Legal/Compliance Lead

**Owns:** IP strategy, liability structure, insurance, post-market compliance.

**Assumptions they often make about others:**
- "We'll address liability structure after the product is designed." (It affects the business model and should be settled early.)
- "No IP conflicts." (Has freedom-to-operate been validated?)
- "Regulatory will not require post-market surveillance." (They almost always do.)

**Questions to surface:**
- Does the chosen manufacturing method have IP clearance? Are there license agreements needed?
- What insurance is required, and when must it be in place?
- What legal entity structure is optimal for this commercialization path?

## Convening Protocol

### Step 1: Preparation

Before calling the specialists together, map what each one is assuming about the others:

**Template:**
- Technical assumes: "Regulatory will approve [design choice] by [date]."
- Regulatory assumes: "Clinical data will be ready by [date]."
- Manufacturing assumes: "Design will be locked by [date]."
- Market assumes: "Product will launch by [date]."

### Step 2: Surfacing Assumptions

Bring all specialists to the table. Go through the assumption map explicitly:

"Technical team, you're assuming regulatory will approve [X] by [date]. Regulatory lead, is that realistic?"
"Regulatory, you're assuming design will be locked by [date]. Technical lead, can you commit to that?"

Do not accept vague answers. Require specific dates, conditions, and owners.

### Step 3: Identifying Conflicts

Listen for conflicts like:
- "I assumed we'd have regulatory feedback by Q2." vs. "Regulatory review won't happen until Q3."
- "We need manufacturing data by [date]" vs. "Manufacturing pilot won't be ready until [later date]."
- "Market window closes in 12 months" vs. "Regulatory approval takes 18 months."

Write these down. Do not move forward until they are resolved.

### Step 4: Resolution

For each conflict, force a decision:

**Option A:** Change the timeline. (If regulatory takes longer, all downstream activities slip.)
**Option B:** Change the sequence. (Can we get preliminary regulatory input while design is still evolving?)
**Option C:** Change the scope. (Do we reduce the initial product scope to meet market timing?)
**Option D:** Accept the risk. (We know [X] is fragile, but we're proceeding anyway. Who owns the risk?)

Every unresolved conflict becomes a named risk and an assigned owner for mitigation.

### Step 5: Documentation

After the convening, document:
1. **Agreed assumptions:** What each domain is committing to.
2. **Unresolved conflicts:** Decisions deferred and owners responsible for resolving them.
3. **Gate criteria:** What must be true at each phase gate (each specialist must sign off).
4. **Communication cadence:** How often specialists meet, how they escalate dependencies.

## Red Flags in Specialist Communication

- **One specialist is absent:** If manufacturing is not in the room during regulatory discussions, decisions will have to be made twice.
- **Assumptions are implicit:** "We'll just make it work" is not an assumption — it is avoidance.
- **Timelines lack owners:** "Regulatory approval will happen by Q3" — who at regulatory is committing to that? (Nobody, usually.)
- **Dependencies are not named:** "We need design locked before manufacturing can proceed" — when exactly? What is the definition of "locked"?
- **Conflicts are avoided:** When two specialists disagree on timeline, do not smooth it over. Name it, analyze it, decide on it.

## Output

Delivery a specialist convening summary with:

1. **Core Team Roster:** Each specialist role, owner name, domain, and primary accountability.
2. **Assumption Map:** What each domain is assuming about the others, with owners and validation dates.
3. **Unresolved Conflicts:** Disagreements between specialists, the options for resolution, and the decision pending.
4. **Gate Sign-Off Requirements:** What each specialist must sign off on at each phase gate.
5. **Communication Cadence:** Meeting frequency, escalation path, decision velocity.
6. **Hidden Expertise Gaps:** What domains are under-represented or missing from the core team?

## Follow-Up Actions

After convening:

1. **Schedule specialist reviews:** Every phase gate should include specialist sign-off. Calendar these explicitly.
2. **Escalate unresolved conflicts:** If a conflict cannot be resolved by the specialists, escalate to leadership with options and recommendations.
3. **Monitor assumptions:** As the commercialization progresses, validate key assumptions. If one breaks, trigger a re-plan with all specialists.
4. **Maintain transparent communication:** Share roadmap changes with all specialists immediately. Do not let silos reform.
