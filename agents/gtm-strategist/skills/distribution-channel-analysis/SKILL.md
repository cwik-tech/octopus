# Distribution Channel Analysis

## Purpose

Transform channel options into a ranked, actionable decision: which 2–3 channels should you pursue in year one, and what are the realistic economics and timelines for each?

This skill evaluates channels across three dimensions:

1. **Activation speed:** How long before you can acquire your first customer through this channel?
2. **Margin sustainability:** What economics does the channel require, and at what volume do those economics work?
3. **Volume potential and customer quality:** Can this channel deliver the customers you need, and will they become reference cases?

Output a ranked list of channels with clear go/no-go recommendations, not a generic market analysis.

## Framework

### Step 1: Identify Candidate Channels

For your product and customer base, list all realistic paths to a customer:

- **Direct sales:** You contact and sell directly to end customers (architects, GCs, facility managers, procurement). Pros: highest margin, direct feedback. Cons: longest sales cycle, highest resource load.
- **Partnerships:** Design firms, architects, or consultants recommend or resell your product. Pros: credibility transfer, warm introductions. Cons: partner incentive alignment, margin concession.
- **Distributors:** Third-party wholesalers who buy from you and resell. Pros: fast channel activation, high volume potential. Cons: low margin, loss of customer relationship, may require large inventory commitments.
- **Contractors/builders:** GCs or specialty contractors who integrate your product into their offering. Pros: bundled solutions appeal to end customers. Cons: need contractor buy-in, product often becomes a cost-line item.
- **Design/specification agents:** Reps or agents who carry your product and sell to their existing customer base. Pros: leverage existing relationships. Cons: split margin, alignment challenges.
- **Hybrid:** Combination of direct sales to key accounts + distributor or partner channel for lower-priority segments.

### Step 2: Screen Channels by Activation Speed

For each candidate channel, estimate time to first customer acquisition:

**Direct sales:** 3–6 months (you have existing architect/GC relationships or can build them quickly) or 12+ months (you must build credibility and relationships from zero).

**Partnerships:** 3–9 months if partners already work in your space and see fit. 12+ months if you must convince them the market exists.

**Distributors:** 2–4 months if you have distributor relationships; 6–12 months if cold outreach.

**Contractors:** 6–12 months to sign; 12+ months to first project pipeline.

**Agents/reps:** 2–6 months if they carry similar products; 9+ months if they must develop expertise.

**Red flag:** If all channels have 12+ month activation timelines, you have a credibility problem, not a channel problem. Revisit positioning or pilot-customer strategy.

### Step 3: Model Unit Economics by Channel

For each channel, estimate the margin and volume you'll need to justify investment:

**Direct sales:**
- Gross margin per customer (price − manufacturing cost)
- Sales cost per customer (your sales effort, travel, proposals)
- Payback: months to recover sales cost
- Volume needed: how many customers/year to justify a dedicated sales person?

**Partnerships:**
- Your gross margin after partner concession (e.g., 40% partner margin = 60% of gross margin to you)
- Partner incentive (volume rebate, co-op marketing, demo unit, training)
- Volume: how much volume do partners need to commit to make it worth their time?

**Distributors:**
- Distributor margin (typically 30–50%, varies by industry)
- Your net margin (50–70% of gross, depending on distributor take)
- Minimum order commitment from distributor (what's their inventory carry cost?)
- Volume potential (how many SKUs can a distributor move in year one?)

**Contractors:**
- Your margin on bundled pricing (will you discount to get bundled projects?)
- Contractor incentive (volume discount, spec support, design help)
- Average project size and frequency (how many projects per contractor per year?)

**Agents/reps:**
- Commission structure (typically 15–30% of gross margin)
- Territory and existing customer overlap (are they already selling to your target customer?)
- Volume ramp (how many customers will they realistically add in year one?)

**Acid test:** Does the margin and volume math work at year-one scale? If a channel requires 100+ customers in year one to pencil out, it's not a year-one channel.

### Step 4: Assess Customer Quality and Reference Potential

Not all customers are equal. Rank channels by likelihood of generating reference cases:

- **Direct sales to architects/designers:** Highest reference value (they influence many projects). Medium activation speed. High margin.
- **Pilot customer in your target segment:** Highest reference value (exactly your customer). Slowest activation (you must recruit and co-develop). Highest margin.
- **Distributors or large contractors:** Fast activation and volume. Medium reference value (you don't own customer relationship). Lower margin.
- **Design firms or consultants:** High reference value (they influence customer decisions). Medium-to-slow activation. Margin varies.

**Question:** Are you optimizing for reference momentum or for early revenue? Year-one channels should do both, but if you must choose, reference > revenue for later-stage success.

### Step 5: Rank and Recommend

Create a decision matrix:

| Channel | Activation (mo) | Margin | Year-1 Volume | Reference Value | Go/No-Go |
|---------|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct architects | 4–6 | High | 10–20 | High | **GO** |
| Design firm partnership | 6–9 | Medium-high | 15–30 | High | **GO** |
| Distributor | 3–4 | Medium-low | 30–50 | Medium | **CONDITIONAL** |
| Contractors | 9–12 | Medium | 5–15 | Medium-low | **2024+** |
| Agents/reps | 6–8 | Medium | 20–40 | Medium | **GO** |

**Recommendation:** Pursue 2–3 GO channels in parallel, starting in month 1. (Do not pursue a single channel; concentration risk.) Delay CONDITIONAL or 2024+ channels until you have proof of concept.

## Common Mistakes

**Assuming TAM = addressable channels:** Just because a market is large doesn't mean you can reach it. Filter candidate channels by reachability, not market size.

**Falling in love with high-margin channels that take forever to activate:** Direct sales to tier-1 architects is high-margin but might take 18 months. Pair it with a faster channel.

**Ignoring partner incentive misalignment:** A distributor who makes more money on competitor products won't push yours. Honest about competitive positioning before signing.

**Confusing volume potential with actual volume:** "Distributors can move 200 units/year" is a market size claim, not a commitment. What will *this* distributor actually order in month one?

**Optimizing only for revenue, not reference:** A distributor channel can deliver revenue but won't generate reference cases that attract other customers. Balance both.

## Questions to Resolve Before Recommending

1. **For each candidate channel: Do you have an entry point?** (Existing relationship, warm introduction, or credible outreach plan.) If not, add 6+ months to activation timeline.
2. **What is your target customer's actual buying process?** Does an architect specify and the GC order? Or does the GC find products and the architect approve? That determines channel priority.
3. **What discount/margin concession would a partner require to be excited about your product?** Be honest about this upfront; it constrains which channels are sustainable.
4. **Which channel can you activate with <5 people and <$50K?** Year-one constraint. High-touch channels that require large sales teams or distributor inventory aren't year-one channels.
5. **What would a customer from each channel tell a second customer about you?** If the answer is "nothing memorable," the channel isn't generating reference momentum.

## Output

Deliver a decision table ranking 2–3 GO channels with:
- Time to first customer (months)
- Unit economics (customer acquisition cost, margin per customer, payback period)
- Year-1 volume target
- Reference/momentum value (high/medium/low)
- Key success metric (customer type, geographic focus, volume ramp)
- Go/no-go recommendation
- Key execution risk for each channel
