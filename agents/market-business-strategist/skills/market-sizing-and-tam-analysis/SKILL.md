# Market Sizing & TAM Analysis

## Purpose

Quantify addressable market for modular interior walls by identifying customer segments, estimating segment size, and validating customer willingness-to-pay through direct discovery. Build TAM from bottom-up (customer count + installation frequency + budget) rather than top-down decomposition of global construction spend.

## When to Use

Use this skill:
- At the start of market validation, before competitive analysis
- When the addressable market size is uncertain or contested
- Before committing capital to manufacturing or regulatory effort
- When customer segment clarity is weak ("anyone who needs walls" is not a segment)

## Workflow

### Step 1: Segment the Market

Define addressable customer segments by decision-making structure, budget cycle, and installation frequency:

**Art & History Museums**
- Decision-maker: Facilities director, curator
- Budget cycle: Annual capital planning or grant-funded projects
- Installation frequency: 2–4 years per installation
- Typical project size: Gallery reconfiguration, temporary exhibit infrastructure
- Budget per project: $50k–$500k

**Corporate Office**
- Decision-maker: Facilities, real estate, sometimes department head
- Budget cycle: Quarterly or semi-annual reconfiguration
- Installation frequency: 1–3 times per year per facility
- Typical project size: Team expansion, open-plan reconfiguration, private booth creation
- Budget per project: $20k–$200k

**Hospitality (Hotel, Restaurant)**
- Decision-maker: General manager, corporate real estate
- Budget cycle: Annual renovation or rapid reconfiguration
- Installation frequency: 1–2 times per year
- Typical project size: Function space reconfiguration, room conversion
- Budget per project: $30k–$300k

**Government / Institutional**
- Decision-maker: Facilities, procurement
- Budget cycle: Annual capital planning, sometimes multi-year
- Installation frequency: 1–2 times per year
- Typical project size: Office reconfiguration, temporary partitioning
- Budget per project: $25k–$150k

For each segment, estimate:
- Number of addressable facilities (use Dun & Bradstreet, Bureau of Labor Statistics, or industry databases)
- Installation frequency per facility per year
- Average budget per installation
- TAM = (# facilities) × (installations/year) × (avg budget)

### Step 2: Validate with Customer Discovery

Conduct 10–15 customer interviews per segment (5 for early validation, 10–15 to confirm). For each interview:

**Discovery Protocol:**

1. **Current solution**: "How do you currently create temporary or semi-permanent walls in [space type]?"
   - Document: incumbent product/service, cost, installation time, pain points

2. **Installation frequency**: "How often in a typical year do you reconfigure or add walls?"
   - Document: number per year, notice required, budget timing

3. **Willingness-to-pay**: "What's the budget for a typical [project type] project? At what price point would modular walls become attractive vs. your current solution?"
   - Document: stated budget, price ceiling, ROI calculation (speed, reusability, flexibility)

4. **Switching cost**: "What would it take for you to switch from [incumbent] to a new solution?"
   - Document: switching barriers (training, compatibility, down-time, organizational approval)

5. **Buying process**: "Walk me through how this decision gets made. Who approves? What's the timeline?"
   - Document: decision-makers, approval layers, budget cycle, lead time

**Evidence Standard**: A segment is "validated" when:
- At least 5 customers confirm they perform this function today
- At least 3 customers state a price ceiling equal to or above your target price
- At least 2 customers state they would switch if the product solved a specific pain point (speed, flexibility, cost)

### Step 3: Calculate TAM by Segment

**Example: Corporate Office Segment**

- Addressable facilities (US): 600k offices with 50+ employees = 600,000 × 0.4 (subset with reconfiguration needs) = 240,000
- Installation frequency: 1.5 per facility per year
- Average budget: $75,000 (based on discovery interviews)
- **TAM = 240,000 × 1.5 × $75,000 = $27 billion**

**Example: Museum Segment**

- Addressable institutions (US): 35,000 museums
- Subset using temporary exhibit or reconfiguration infrastructure: 15,000
- Installation frequency: 2 per museum per year
- Average budget: $100,000 (based on discovery interviews)
- **TAM = 15,000 × 2 × $100,000 = $3 billion**

**Total Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)**:
- Sum TAMs across all segments: $27B + $3B + ... = **$35B+** (aggregate)

**Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM) — Year 1–3**:
- Assume 0.5–2% market penetration across segments within 3 years (based on similar product adoption curves)
- SOM = SAM × 0.01 = **$350M–$700M** (conservative, realistic for new entrant)

### Step 4: Confidence Range & Risk Factors

State TAM with confidence intervals:

- **High confidence** (±20%): Customer-validated segments with 10+ interviews and confirmed budget cycles
- **Medium confidence** (±50%): Segments with 5–7 interviews but incomplete budget data
- **Low confidence** (±100%+): Segments with <5 interviews or highly variable budgets

**Risk factors that reduce TAM:**

1. **Incumbent switching resistance**: Customers may prefer incremental improvements to incumbent (faster drywall installation) over new category
2. **Regulatory barriers**: Some segments (hospitals, government) have prescriptive material/fire codes that lock in incumbent solutions
3. **Installation complexity**: If modular walls require specialist training or custom engineering, SAM shrinks to facilities with in-house expertise or on-site contractors
4. **Economic downturn**: Capital-project budgets compress; installation frequency drops

## Red Lines

- Never accept TAM based solely on top-down market decomposition. Always validate with customer interviews.
- Never count customers who express interest but have no current budget or decision authority.
- Never assume installation frequency without specific customer confirmation.
- Never inflate TAM by including "nice-to-have" segments; focus on customers who are actively solving this problem today.
