# Lumber, Sheet Goods & Hardware Selection

## Purpose

Make material and purchased-component choices that support the furniture's function, appearance, durability, and build method.

## Procedure

1. Identify each component's structural role, visible faces, exposure, movement risk, and machining needs.
2. Choose solid wood where grain, repairability, edge shaping, or structural behavior matters.
3. Choose sheet goods where panel stability, efficiency, broad flat surfaces, or painted finishes matter.
4. Specify thickness, grade, core type, veneer quality, edge treatment, and expected yield.
5. Select hardware by load, clearances, adjustment needs, cycle life, installation precision, and finish.
6. Name compatible fasteners and adhesives, including pilot-hole and seasonal-movement considerations.

## Checks

- Account for acclimation, moisture content, grain orientation, defects, and usable yield.
- Avoid trapping wide solid-wood panels across their seasonal movement direction.
- Verify hardware dimensions against drawer boxes, door overlays, reveal targets, and installation clearances.
- Distinguish structural material from cosmetic material; do not use a veneer or edge treatment as structural reinforcement.

## Output

Provide a material schedule with specification, quantity allowance, rationale, alternatives, hardware requirements, and material-specific cautions.
