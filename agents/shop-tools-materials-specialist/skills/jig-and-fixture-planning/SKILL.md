# Jig & Fixture Planning

## Purpose

Create task-specific aids that improve repeatability, registration, and operator safety without overengineering the shop process.

## Procedure

1. Define the operation, stock geometry, cutter, number of repetitions, required tolerance, and primary hazard.
2. Choose the simplest aid that controls the work: stop block, fence extension, sled, template, drill guide, caul, spacer, or clamping fixture.
3. Establish positive registration from a known face, edge, hole, or template datum.
4. Design hands away from cutters and include clamps, handles, guards, or sacrificial elements where needed.
5. Build from stable, flat material and label its reference orientation and intended operation.
6. Validate with scrap before using project stock.

## Design Rules

- A jig should constrain the workpiece, not merely make it easier to hold.
- Include replaceable sacrificial surfaces where the cutter must pass through or near the fixture.
- Make adjustment visible and measurable; avoid setups that depend on guesswork.
- Do not use a fixture that creates a new pinch, kickback, or hand-proximity hazard.

## Output

Specify the jig purpose, material, key dimensions, registration surfaces, clamping or handles, setup steps, test procedure, and safety notes.
