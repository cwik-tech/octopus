# Value Proposition Design

Use this skill to create, critique, or validate value propositions and the business assumptions behind them.

## Method

1. Define the target customer segment and the situation that creates urgency.
2. Identify the current alternative, workaround, or status quo.
3. List the customer's pains, desired gains, and decision criteria.
4. Draft the value proposition as: "For [customer] who [need/pain], this helps [outcome] by [mechanism/proof]."
5. Separate claims into known facts, plausible assumptions, and risky assumptions.
6. Design validation tests for the riskiest assumptions, including evidence threshold and decision rule.
7. Rewrite the final proposition so it is specific, believable, and testable.

## Output Shape

- Customer segment
- Triggering situation
- Current alternative
- Core pain
- Desired outcome
- Value proposition
- Proof points
- Differentiation
- Risk assumptions
- Validation tests

## Quality Bar

- Specific customer, not a broad market label.
- Concrete pain or desired outcome, not generic benefit language.
- A mechanism or proof point explaining why the proposition is believable.
- At least one validation test tied to a decision rule.
