# Gap Classification — Reference

Extended examples of the four gap types with resolution strategies. Use during Step 5 of the Brief Scoping protocol.

---

## Hard Gaps (BLOCKING)

A hard gap blocks the brief. The Writer cannot produce a credible section without this information. Do not proceed until resolved.

### Examples

**Missing core data:**
"The brief asks for a Market Opportunity section. No market size data, growth rate, or competitive landscape information was provided in the inputs."
→ Resolution: Request market research, client data, or authorize use of public sources (specify which)

**Unidentified audience:**
"The document is described as 'for the leadership team' but the company has three leadership tiers with different information needs."
→ Resolution: Confirm the primary reader and their decision context before proceeding

**Ambiguous purpose:**
"The inputs describe both a proposal to win new business AND an assessment of current operations. These require different structures and tones."
→ Resolution: Confirm which document type this is

**Contradictory inputs:**
"The client brief says 'recommend Option A.' The data provided shows Option B has stronger economics. Cannot write a credible recommendation for Option A."
→ Resolution: Surface the contradiction and get direction before drafting

**Missing financial data for financial section:**
"Template requires ROI analysis. No cost data, revenue projections, or investment figures were provided."
→ Resolution: Request the numbers or remove the financial section from scope

---

## Soft Gaps (NON-BLOCKING)

A soft gap weakens the document but doesn't prevent drafting. Flag it, note where it impacts quality, and proceed.

### Examples

**Missing benchmark data:**
"The retention rate (78%) is presented without industry context. The section will be written but the comparative claim ('strong retention') cannot be substantiated."
→ Flag: "Retention benchmark will be described as 'above industry average' — confirm whether you want to find the specific benchmark"

**Thin competitive landscape:**
"Only two competitors were mentioned in the inputs. The competitive analysis section will be limited in scope."
→ Flag: "Competitive analysis will cover the two named competitors only. Recommend expanding before finalizing."

**No direct client quotes:**
"The customer research section references 'positive customer feedback' but no specific quotes were provided."
→ Flag: "Customer sentiment will be described in aggregate without specific examples. Insert quotes in revision if available."

**Outdated data:**
"The financial figures in the inputs are from Q1. The document will be delivered in Q4."
→ Flag: "Financial section uses Q1 data. Confirm whether updated figures are available."

---

## Assumption Gaps (FLAG)

Information the user takes for granted that the reader may not share. These must be surfaced in the brief so the Writer can decide whether to explain them or leave them assumed.

### Examples

**Internal acronyms:**
"Inputs reference 'the NPS initiative' and 'the Q3 transformation.' Reader may not know what these are."
→ Flag: "Define or briefly explain the NPS initiative and Q3 transformation in the relevant sections, or confirm the reader already knows"

**Prior decision context:**
"The recommendation builds on 'the decision made in the January board meeting.' Reader may not know what was decided."
→ Flag: "Add 1-2 sentence context on the January board decision, or confirm reader has that context"

**Shared understanding of the problem:**
"Inputs assume the reader already believes the current approach is broken. If the reader is skeptical of this premise, the recommendation loses its foundation."
→ Flag: "The Situation section needs to build the case for why change is needed, not assume it"

**Industry knowledge:**
"Inputs use regulatory and technical terminology (e.g., 'CAC payback period,' 'Series B metrics') without explanation."
→ Flag: "Define technical terms on first use, or confirm reader sophistication level"

---

## Data Gaps (QUANTIFY)

Numbers, metrics, or evidence referenced but not provided. Flag each one specifically — vague flags are useless.

### Examples

**Vague claims needing numbers:**
- "Strong growth" → What was the growth rate? Over what period? Compared to what?
- "Significant cost savings" → How much? Over what time horizon?
- "High customer satisfaction" → NPS? CSAT score? Retention rate? Time period?
- "The market is large" → What is the total addressable market (TAM)? Source?
- "Competitive pricing" → What is the price? What are competitors charging?

**Missing comparisons:**
- Revenue figure without prior period → "Q3 revenue was $4.2M" needs "vs. $3.1M in Q3 prior year"
- Headcount without context → "The team is 45 people" needs "up from 28 in 2024" or "vs. 80 at Competitor X"

**Referenced but absent:**
- "See attached financial model" with no attachment
- "As per the customer survey" with no survey data
- "Our analysis shows" without the analysis inputs

**Resolution format:**
For each data gap, specify:
1. The exact figure needed
2. The section it affects
3. Where it might be found (who to ask, what to look for)
4. What to do if unavailable (use placeholder, estimate with caveat, omit the point)
