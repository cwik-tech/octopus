# Capital Market Cycle Indicators

## Purpose

Indicators for determining where we are in the venture capital market cycle. The cycle position affects deal evaluation, valuation benchmarks, and follow-on risk assessment.

---

## Cycle Phases

### Phase 1 — Risk-On (Frothy)

**Characteristics:**
- Median pre-money valuations 50-100%+ above historical averages
- Rounds oversubscribed, closing in <2 weeks
- Non-traditional investors (hedge funds, mutual funds, corporates) entering early-stage
- "Tourist capital" — investors entering sectors they don't understand
- Compressed diligence timelines (days, not weeks)
- FOMO (Fear of Missing Out) as primary investment driver
- New investor entrants (first-time fund managers, angel syndicates scaling up)
- Narrative-driven investing (themes > metrics)

**Historical examples:** 2020-2021 (ZIRP (Zero Interest Rate Policy) era), 1999-2000 (dot-com bubble)

**Investment implications:**
- Apply 30-50% valuation discount to account for market inflation
- Focus on unit economics over growth rate (growth is easy when capital is free)
- Be more selective — the cost of overpaying is highest at cycle peaks
- Check for "capital-dependent growth" — companies that only grow because they're burning investor capital
- Expect future rounds to come at lower multiples (the correction)

### Phase 2 — Neutral

**Characteristics:**
- Valuations in line with 5-year historical averages
- Normal deal flow, competitive but not frenzied
- Diligence timelines of 4-8 weeks (standard)
- Mix of institutional and individual investors at appropriate stages
- Balanced supply/demand for quality deals
- Metrics matter — investors demanding evidence before investing

**Investment implications:**
- Standard evaluation frameworks apply
- Benchmarks are useful as-is (no adjustment needed)
- Follow-on environment is predictable

### Phase 3 — Risk-Off (Downturn)

**Characteristics:**
- Median valuations 30-50% below recent peaks
- Deals struggling to close, extended timelines (3-6+ months)
- Bridge rounds and extensions replacing new rounds
- Investor retreat — non-traditional investors exit, many funds pause deployment
- "Flight to quality" — capital concentrates in top-decile companies
- Flat rounds and down rounds become common
- Layoffs across portfolio companies

**Historical examples:** 2022-2023 (post-ZIRP correction), 2008-2009 (financial crisis), 2001-2002 (post-dot-com)

**Investment implications:**
- Opportunity for better valuations and terms
- But: follow-on risk is significantly higher (companies may not be able to raise next round)
- Focus on companies with runway >24 months or path to profitability
- Expect lower exit valuations and longer hold periods
- Counter-cyclical investing can produce best vintages — but requires conviction and patience

## Quantitative Indicators

### Venture Capital Activity Metrics

| Indicator | Risk-On | Neutral | Risk-Off | Data Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total VC invested (quarterly) | >$70B US | $30-$70B US | <$30B US | PitchBook, NVCA (National Venture Capital Association) |
| Median pre-money (Series A) | >$40M | $15-$40M | <$15M | PitchBook |
| Median time to close | <4 weeks | 4-8 weeks | >8 weeks | Market observation |
| Down round frequency | <5% of rounds | 5-15% | >15% | PitchBook |
| Bridge round frequency | <10% of follow-ons | 10-25% | >25% | PitchBook |
| New fund formation | >500 new funds/year | 200-500 | <200 | PitchBook |

### Public Market Signals

| Indicator | Risk-On | Neutral | Risk-Off |
|---|---|---|---|
| SaaS public company multiples (BVP (Bessemer Venture Partners) Cloud Index) | EV/Revenue >15x median | 8-15x | <8x |
| IPO market | Active, multiple tech IPOs | Selective, occasional IPOs | Frozen, no tech IPOs |
| NASDAQ trend | Rising >20% YTD | Flat to +20% | Declining |
| Interest rates (Fed Funds) | Low (<2%) or decreasing | Stable | High (>4%) or increasing |
| VIX (Volatility Index) | <15 | 15-25 | >25 (sustained) |

## Cycle Timing and Investment Strategy

### Counter-Cyclical Investing

The best venture fund vintages historically come from deploying during downturns:

| Fund Vintage | Market Phase | Outcome Tendency |
|---|---|---|
| Deployed at peak (frothy) | Risk-On | Below-average returns (overpaid for everything) |
| Deployed in neutral | Neutral | Average returns |
| Deployed in downturn | Risk-Off | Above-average returns (better prices, less competition, stronger companies survive) |
| Deployed in recovery | Early Risk-On | Strong returns (good prices + improving exit environment) |

### Adjusting Deal Evaluation by Cycle

| Evaluation Element | Risk-On Adjustment | Risk-Off Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Valuation | Apply 30-50% discount to comparables | Comparables may be too low — use 5-year averages |
| Growth targets | Discount growth (capital-fueled growth inflates numbers) | Accept lower growth (environment is harder) |
| Follow-on capacity | Model follow-on at higher valuations | Model follow-on at flat or down round valuations |
| Runway requirements | Require 18+ months (markets can shift quickly) | Require 24+ months (fundraising will be harder) |
| Exit timeline | Add 1-2 years to exit projections | Add 2-3 years |
| Burn rate tolerance | Lower — companies should be more efficient | Lowest — survival is paramount |
