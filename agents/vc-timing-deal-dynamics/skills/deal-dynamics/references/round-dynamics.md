# Round Dynamics

## Purpose

How to read round composition, investor behavior, and process signals to extract information about a deal beyond what the pitch deck reveals.

---

## Reading the Round Process

### Speed Signals

| Round Speed | Interpretation | Investor Action |
|---|---|---|
| Closing in <1 week | Either exceptional company or FOMO-driven | Caution — ensure diligence isn't being sacrificed for speed |
| Closing in 1-3 weeks | High demand, competitive process | Move quickly but don't skip diligence steps |
| 4-8 weeks | Normal, healthy process | Standard evaluation timeline |
| 2-4 months | Moderate difficulty raising | Investigate why — thesis fit? Market concern? Valuation mismatch? |
| >4 months | Struggling to close | Strong negative signal — multiple investors have evaluated and passed |

### The "Why Now" of Fundraising

| Trigger | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Hit key milestone (revenue, product, customer) | Positive — raising from strength |
| Running out of runway (<6 months) | Neutral to negative — may accept unfavorable terms |
| Competitor raised a large round | Defensive — may need to match competitor's resources |
| Market window (AI hype, sector momentum) | Opportunistic — may be raising because they can, not because they should |
| Strategic event (partnership, regulatory change) | Context-dependent — evaluate the event independently |

## Reading Investor Behavior

### Existing Investor Signals

| Behavior | Signal | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Leading the round (taking pro-rata or more) | Very positive — maximum conviction from informed insiders | Strongest positive |
| Participating pro-rata | Positive — maintaining position, steady conviction | Strong positive |
| Participating below pro-rata | Mixed — still involved but reduced conviction | Moderate concern |
| Not participating but supporting the raise | Negative — they have inside information and are declining to invest more | Significant concern |
| Actively selling secondary | Very negative — trying to exit | Strong negative |
| No comment / hard to reach | Concerning — avoiding the question | Worth investigating |

### New Investor Signals

| Behavior | Signal |
|---|---|
| Top-tier VC leading with board seat | Strong validation — they've done diligence and are committing time |
| Multiple top-tier VCs competing | Very strong validation — but may drive up price |
| Mid-tier VC leading | Moderate validation — check if top-tier passed |
| No institutional lead (angel/individual round at later stage) | Negative — why didn't any institutional investor want to lead? |
| Corporate strategic leading | Mixed — strategic validation but potential conflicts (information access, exit limitations) |

## Round Structure Analysis

### Healthy Round Structures

| Structure | When It's Healthy |
|---|---|
| One lead + 2-3 followers | Standard, clean. Lead provides governance, followers provide capital. |
| Two co-leads | Acceptable if both are active and roles are clear. |
| One lead + strategic investor | Good if strategic provides non-capital value (distribution, partnerships). |
| Inside round (existing investors only) | Healthy if company is performing well and insiders are increasing conviction. Concerning if no new outside investor would invest. |

### Concerning Round Structures

| Structure | Why It's Concerning |
|---|---|
| Party round (many small checks, no lead) | No one has enough conviction to lead. No governance. No one helps the company. |
| Single large investor (no diversification) | Concentration risk — if that investor can't follow on, company is stranded. |
| Excessive SAFEs at different caps | Cap table becomes unpredictable. Conversion math may surprise founders. |
| Down round with aggressive terms | Existing shareholders may be significantly diluted. Check if founders are still motivated. |
| Bridge with conversion at next round | Kicks the valuation question down the road. Company may be weaker by then. |

## Competitive Dynamics in Fundraising

### When You're Competing for Allocation

**Our advantages to emphasize:**
- Specific value-add relevant to this company (introductions, expertise, portfolio synergies)
- Speed of decision-making (can we commit faster than competitors?)
- Clean, founder-friendly terms (differentiate on structure, not just valuation)
- Reputation and track record with similar companies
- Specific post-investment plan (100-day plan)

**Our disadvantages to assess honestly:**
- Are we outbid on valuation? (Don't win on price alone — winner's curse)
- Do we lack sector expertise the company needs?
- Is our fund size appropriate for the check size needed?
- Do we have a competitor in our portfolio that creates a conflict?

### When We're the Only Term Sheet

**Investigate deeply:**
- Why hasn't anyone else offered terms? Ask the founder directly.
- Were there other term sheets that fell through? Why?
- Did other investors evaluate and pass? What were their reasons?
- Is the company raising in a difficult market, or is something specific to this deal?

**Possible explanations (positive):**
- We have unique sector expertise that others lack
- The deal is too small / too large for most funds
- The company is raising from a position of strength and chose us first

**Possible explanations (negative):**
- Other investors found issues in diligence we haven't yet
- The company has been fundraising for months without success
- The valuation expectations are unrealistic and others declined

## Co-Investor Due Diligence

### Before investing alongside another fund, verify:

1. **Do they have capital for follow-on?** A co-investor who can't follow on in future rounds leaves the company stranded.
2. **What's their investment thesis?** Is it aligned with ours, or are they investing for different reasons?
3. **What's their reputation with founders?** Combative board members or passive investors both hurt companies.
4. **Will they compete with us for board influence?** Governance clarity matters.
5. **What's their fund lifecycle?** A fund near the end of its investment period may not participate in follow-on rounds.
