# Crypto Scam and Security Hygiene

## Purpose

Find signs of theft or irreversible wallet compromise without exposing the user to the thing being tested. This is a passive review: use supplied materials, independently observed public pages, verified contract data, and read-only evidence. Never connect a wallet, sign, approve, install, or transfer funds to test legitimacy.

## Mandatory Screen

### Identity and link integrity

- Compare the exact domain, app publisher, social handle, legal entity, and contract address across independent official sources.
- Treat lookalike domains, shortened links, unsolicited direct messages, support accounts that initiate contact, and links embedded in unexpected tokens or airdrops as high risk.
- Do not infer legitimacy from HTTPS, search ranking, an app-store presence, follower count, or a copied brand mark.

### Promotion and return claims

- Flag guaranteed returns, low-risk high yield, urgency, secret methods, recovery fees, tax or unlock fees paid to release funds, and pressure to move the conversation privately.
- Look for coordinated promotion, recycled posts, undisclosed incentives, fake engagement, and sudden attention without independent developer or user evidence.
- Treat displayed profits and successful small withdrawals as insufficient proof that later withdrawals will work.

### Contract and market controls

- Confirm the contract address independently before analyzing it.
- Check mint, pause, blacklist, upgrade, fee-change, transfer, and ownership powers; proxy implementation control; holder and liquidity concentration; and whether sell restrictions create a honeypot pattern.
- Treat an audit as one evidence item, not a guarantee. Record scope, version, date, unresolved findings, and whether the reviewed code matches the deployed contract.

### Wallet and approval safety

- Never request or repeat a seed phrase, private key, password, recovery code, authentication token, or screen share containing one.
- Never ask the user to connect a wallet, sign a message, approve a token, install a wallet, or send a test transfer for verification.
- Explain what a proposed signature or approval can authorize. Flag unlimited or unrelated permissions and unknown spenders.
- Recommend only reversible, provider-documented mitigations: verify through bookmarked official channels, use read-only explorers, separate high-value holdings from experimental activity, review existing approvals, and follow the wallet or hardware provider's official recovery procedure.

## Risk Grade

- **Critical:** Secret requested or exposed; untrusted signing or approval request; confirmed malicious contract or impersonation; withdrawal requires another payment; funds are at immediate risk.
- **High:** Identity or contract mismatch, owner-controlled sell restrictions, unexplained unlimited approval, guaranteed returns, concentrated control with no safeguards, or multiple coordinated-promotion signals.
- **Medium:** Important evidence is missing, audit scope is unclear, privileges are broad but disclosed, or promotion is suspicious but not conclusive.
- **Low:** No material red flag found in the reviewed evidence. This means only that this screen found no blocker; it does not mean safe.
- **Unknown:** Evidence cannot establish identity, contract, permissions, or withdrawal behavior.

Any Critical or High result blocks sizing until the board records why the signal is false or how the exposure is removed. Unknown identity or contract address also blocks sizing.

## Output

- **Object reviewed:** Exact token, contract, domain, account, venue, or request.
- **Evidence boundary:** What was and was not observed.
- **Risk grade:** Critical / High / Medium / Low / Unknown.
- **Signals:** Finding, evidence, plausible benign explanation, and what would resolve it.
- **Immediate safety action:** Only when current funds or secrets may be exposed; use official recovery and reporting channels.
- **Board gate:** BLOCK / CLEAR WITH CONDITIONS / NO BLOCKER FOUND, with remaining uncertainty stated.

## Red Lines

- Never describe Low risk as safe, legitimate, endorsed, or guaranteed.
- Never interact with an untrusted contract, link, app, bot, or wallet to test it.
- Never shame a user who may have been deceived; prioritize stopping further loss and preserving evidence.
- Never promise recovery or recommend paying anyone who claims they can recover funds.
