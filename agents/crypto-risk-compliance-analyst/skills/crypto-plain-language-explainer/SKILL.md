# Crypto Plain-Language Explainer

## Purpose

The board's reasoning is only useful if the user can follow it. This skill preserves the substance and the uncertainty while removing the vocabulary barrier. It never simplifies a risk away; if the honest answer is "this could go to zero," the plain-language version says exactly that.

## Method

1. **Scan** the draft for every term a newcomer would not know. Typical offenders: TVL, FDV, float, unlock, slashing, sequencer, oracle, impermanent loss, restaking, MEV, L2, rollup, bridge, wrapped asset, basis, funding rate, drawdown, sizing, risk budget.
2. **Decide** for each term: define inline, replace with plain wording, or move to can-skip.
3. **Rewrite** using the sentence rules below.
4. **Split** the final output into two labelled blocks: **Need to know** and **Can skip for now**.
5. **Check** that no risk, downside, or uncertainty statement was softened in the rewrite.

## Sentence rules

- Define a term the first time it appears, in the same sentence, in under fifteen words.
- Prefer the concrete over the abstract: "the team can release 20% more tokens in March" beats "significant unlock overhang."
- One idea per sentence. If a sentence has "which" or "whereas," split it.
- Numbers keep their dates: "as of 12 August 2026" stays attached.
- Never replace a total-loss warning with "volatile" or "high risk." Say "you could lose all of it."

## Analogy bank

Use these when they fit; do not force them.

| Concept | Analogy |
| --- | --- |
| Private key | A signing key: anyone who gets it may move the funds, while losing every recovery path may lock you out |
| Exchange custody | A company controls the keys, so access depends on both its systems and your legal rights against it |
| Proof of stake | Validators post a bond; some protocols penalize specified faults, while rewards may combine fees and new issuance |
| Smart contract | A vending machine — it does exactly what it was built to do, including the bugs |
| Upgradeable contract | A vending machine whose owner can rewire it overnight |
| L2 | An express lane that settles up with the main highway's toll booth periodically |
| Bridge | A border crossing with extra guards or machinery that can fail between the two ledgers |
| Token unlock | Early employees' stock options all vesting the same week |
| FDV vs market cap | The price of the whole building versus the price of the floors currently for sale |
| Impermanent loss | A pool rebalances as relative prices change, so its return can lag simply holding the assets |
| Yield from emissions | A store paying you in its own coupons that it prints freely |
| Invalidation trigger | The tripwire you set before entering so you do not argue with yourself later |
| Risk budget | The amount you have already decided you can afford to set on fire this year |

## Need-to-know versus can-skip

**Need to know** contains only what changes the user's decision or their understanding of the downside. Usually two to four sentences:
- What the board decided and the single biggest reason.
- The worst realistic outcome.
- The one event that would mean the board was wrong.
- Any action the user needs to take or condition they need to watch.

**Can skip for now** contains the mechanics, the secondary risks, the full evidence trail, and the debate. Label it clearly and keep it, because the user may come back to it.

## Quality check before returning

- Would a smart adult with no crypto background understand the need-to-know block without looking anything up?
- Is every downside stated at least as bluntly as in the original?
- Did any date or source fall off a number?
- Did any contrarian objection disappear in the rewrite?
