# Occasion Tone-Fit Review

An occasional poem is judged by a room, not a reader. This review tests every tonal choice against the people who will actually hear it.

## Inputs

- The locked occasion brief: occasion, tone target, delivery mode, reader
- The interviewer's audience notes and sensitivity flags (topics to avoid, estrangements, illness, recent losses, religious or cultural considerations, children present)
- The draft poem

## Procedure

### 1. Build the room

List three to six audience archetypes from the brief. Always include:

- The honoree or the honoree's closest person
- The most emotionally vulnerable attendee
- The most distant attendee, such as a colleague or acquaintance who lacks the inside context
- Any group named in the sensitivity flags

### 2. Tone map

Label each stanza with its dominant tone: tender, playful, teasing, solemn, reverent, celebratory, wistful, or grieving. Compare the sequence to the tone target in the brief.

### 3. Front-Row Test (every tonal move)

For each joke, tease, reference to loss, religious reference, or mention of a third party, ask how each archetype hears it. Flag the line if any archetype would wince, feel excluded, or feel exposed.

Typical failures by occasion:

- **Eulogy / memorial:**
  - Humor that mocks the deceased's flaws rather than celebrating their quirks
  - Jokes about the manner of death
  - Certainty about an afterlife the family may not share
  - Rushing to consolation before grief is acknowledged
- **Wedding:**
  - References to exes or past relationships
  - Teasing about infidelity, money, or in-laws
  - Inside jokes that exclude most of the guests
  - Addressing only one partner
- **Birthday (milestone):**
  - Age and decline jokes aimed at someone sensitive about health
  - Mentioning absent or deceased family without a clear hinge
- **Retirement:**
  - Implying the person was pushed out or is now irrelevant
  - Jokes at the expense of colleagues who are present
- **Toast:**
  - Anything that forces the reader to perform emotion they may not feel
  - Material unsuitable for children or elders who will be present

### 4. Hinge check

Every shift between registers needs a hinge line the audience can follow, for example humor to tenderness or grief to celebration. An abrupt shift reads as tone-deaf even when both halves are good. Flag missing hinges as WARNING.

### 5. Inside-joke ratio

An inside joke is acceptable if the wider room can tell it is affectionate without knowing the story. Flag inside jokes that require backstory to be read as kind.

### 6. Reader fit

Confirm the poem suits the person actually reading it. A grieving child should not be asked to deliver a stanza of jokes. A best man should not be asked to voice a parent's perspective.

## Severity rules

- **BLOCKING:**
  - Any line touching a flagged sensitivity
  - Humor at a memorial that could read as mocking
  - Anything that could embarrass a named attendee
  - A tone contrary to the locked tone target
- **WARNING:**
  - Missing hinges
  - Exclusionary inside jokes
  - Mild register drift
- **SUGGESTION:**
  - Optional softening or sharpening

## Output

Provide:

- The tone map
- The room archetypes used
- Each flagged line, quoted, with the affected archetype, severity, and a targeted fix

If a tone risk cannot be judged because the brief is silent on the audience, list the missing question for the interviewer instead of guessing. If fixing a tone issue would require changing the locked occasion brief, name the escalation to the lead.
