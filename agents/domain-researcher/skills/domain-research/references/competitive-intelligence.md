# Competitive Intelligence Framework

## Purpose

When researching a domain, understanding who the incumbents are is necessary but insufficient. This reference extends domain research with structured competitive intelligence: understanding what competitors are doing, why, and where the landscape is heading.

## Competitive Intelligence Protocol

### Step 1: Identify the Competitive Set (5 minutes)

Map competitors into tiers:

- **Direct competitors:** Same product category, same target customer. Fight for the same budget line.
- **Adjacent competitors:** Different product category, overlapping customer need. Could pivot into your space.
- **Substitute competitors:** Different approach entirely, same underlying problem solved. Often missed.

For each tier, identify 3-5 players. Don't stop at the obvious ones — search for emerging players, funded startups, and open-source alternatives.

### Step 2: Decode Positioning and Strategy (10 minutes per competitor)

For each major competitor, extract:

- **Value proposition:** What do they claim to do for whom? (Read their homepage, not their about page.)
- **Pricing model:** How do they charge? (Free tier, per-seat, usage-based, enterprise-only.) Pricing model reveals strategy.
- **Target segment:** Who are they actually selling to? (Look at case studies, testimonials, sales language.)
- **Recent moves:** Product launches, acquisitions, partnerships, pivots, leadership changes, layoffs, funding rounds in the last 12 months.
- **Technology bets:** What platform, infrastructure, or technology choices have they committed to? These are hard to reverse and reveal long-term strategy.
- **Hiring signals:** What roles are they hiring for? Heavy engineering hiring = building. Heavy sales hiring = scaling. Heavy support hiring = retention problem.

### Step 3: Map Competitive Dynamics (10 minutes)

Analyze the relationships between competitors:

- **Market concentration:** Is this a winner-take-all market, an oligopoly, or fragmented? This determines competitive intensity.
- **Differentiation axes:** On what dimensions do competitors differ? (Price, features, vertical focus, integration depth, brand trust, geographic coverage.)
- **Switching costs:** How hard is it for a customer to move between competitors? High switching costs = defensible positions. Low = price wars.
- **Network effects:** Does the product become more valuable with more users? If yes, first-mover advantage is significant.
- **Convergence patterns:** Are competitors becoming more similar over time? Feature parity signals commoditization.
- **Divergence patterns:** Are competitors specializing into niches? This signals market maturation and opportunity.

### Step 4: Identify Whitespace and Vulnerability (5 minutes)

Based on Steps 1-3, surface:

- **Underserved segments:** Which customer needs are no competitor addressing well?
- **Overserved segments:** Where are competitors over-investing in features customers don't value?
- **Structural vulnerabilities:** Which competitors are fragile? (Single customer concentration, key-person risk, technical debt, pending regulation.)
- **Timing opportunities:** Where is the market shifting in a direction that benefits a new entrant or different approach?

## Source Hierarchy for Competitive Intelligence

| Source Type | Reliability | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| SEC filings, annual reports | High | Revenue, strategy, risk factors |
| Patent filings | High | Technology direction, R&D focus |
| Job postings | Medium-High | Organizational priorities, growth areas |
| Product changelogs | Medium-High | Feature development velocity and direction |
| Customer reviews (G2, Capterra) | Medium | Strengths, weaknesses, satisfaction patterns |
| Conference talks, blog posts | Medium | Strategic thinking, positioning intent |
| Press releases | Medium-Low | Announcements (discount for spin) |
| Social media, forums | Low-Medium | Sentiment, complaints, emerging issues |
| Vendor marketing materials | Low | Claims only — verify independently |

## Output: Competitive Landscape Summary

Integrate competitive intelligence into the domain brief as a new section:

- **Competitive set** (direct, adjacent, substitute — 3-5 per tier)
- **Positioning map** (2-3 key differentiation axes, where each player sits)
- **Recent strategic moves** (last 12 months, by competitor)
- **Market dynamics** (concentration, switching costs, network effects, convergence/divergence)
- **Whitespace and vulnerability** (underserved segments, fragile competitors, timing opportunities)
- **Confidence assessment** (what you're sure of vs. what's speculative)

## For AI Agents

- **Don't just list competitors.** Map their strategy, moves, and relationships. A list of names is not intelligence.
- **Watch for recency bias.** Recent funding or press coverage doesn't mean a competitor is strong. Check fundamentals.
- **Triangulate claims.** A competitor's marketing says one thing; customer reviews say another. The truth is usually in between.
- **Flag information gaps.** If you can't find pricing, financials, or recent moves for a key competitor, note it explicitly. Missing data is a signal.
- **Update regularly.** Competitive landscapes shift quarterly. Flag the brief's date and recommend refresh intervals.
