# Deck Template Systems

## Purpose

Provide reusable pitch deck structures while adapting them to the business, audience, stage, and decision context. Templates are scaffolds, not scripts. The goal is to create a deck that feels familiar enough to be easy to follow and specific enough to be persuasive.

## When To Use

Use this skill when:

- The user asks for a deck template.
- The user mentions Y Combinator, YC, investor decks, fundraising decks, demo day decks, sales decks, client decks, or proposal decks.
- The user wants a repeatable format for building pitch decks.
- The user needs to choose between multiple deck structures.

## Template Selection Rule

Choose the template based on the audience’s decision:

| Audience | Decision | Best Template |
|---------|----------|---------------|
| Investors | Take a meeting, invest, continue diligence | YC-style fundraising deck |
| Prospective clients | Buy, schedule proposal review, approve pilot | Client sales deck |
| Strategic partners | Explore partnership, commit resources | Partnership deck |
| Executives | Approve initiative, budget, or strategy | Executive proposal deck |
| Internal team | Align around plan | Internal strategy deck |

## YC-Style Fundraising Deck

Use for startup fundraising, especially pre-seed through Series A, when the goal is to make investors believe the company is solving a large, urgent problem with a capable team and credible early proof.

Recommended core structure:

1. **Title / One-liner** — Who you are and what you do.
2. **Problem** — The painful, specific problem.
3. **Customer / User** — Who has the problem and why they care.
4. **Solution** — What you built or are building.
5. **Why Now** — The timing shift that makes this opportunity possible.
6. **Market** — Size and shape of the opportunity, preferably bottom-up.
7. **Product** — How the product works, with screenshot or workflow if available.
8. **Traction / Validation** — Revenue, users, pilots, retention, LOIs, pipeline, or strong qualitative validation.
9. **Business Model** — How the company makes money.
10. **Competition / Differentiation** — Why this can win.
11. **Go-to-Market** — How the company acquires customers repeatably.
12. **Team** — Why this team can execute.
13. **Ask / Use of Funds** — How much is being raised, why, and what milestones it unlocks.

Optional appendix slides:

- Financial model.
- Cohort retention.
- Customer case studies.
- Product roadmap.
- Regulatory path.
- Technical architecture.
- Detailed competitive matrix.
- Market sizing backup.

## Client Sales Deck

Use when selling a product, service, consulting engagement, or pilot to a client.

Recommended structure:

1. **Title / Client-specific promise** — Make it clear this is for them.
2. **Client context** — Show you understand their situation.
3. **Problem / pain** — Name the costly issue.
4. **Cost of inaction** — Show why delay matters.
5. **Proposed solution** — Introduce the offer.
6. **How it works** — Explain the process, product, or engagement model.
7. **Expected outcomes** — Tie the offer to business results.
8. **Proof / credibility** — Case studies, results, experience, testimonials, or relevant examples.
9. **Implementation plan** — Timeline, phases, responsibilities.
10. **Investment / pricing** — Cost, options, or commercial model if appropriate.
11. **Why us** — Differentiation and trust.
12. **Next step** — Meeting, pilot, contract, workshop, or approval.

## Partnership Deck

Use when asking another organization to collaborate, distribute, co-build, sponsor, or provide access.

Recommended structure:

1. **Shared opportunity** — Why both parties should care.
2. **Market or ecosystem shift** — Why the timing is right.
3. **Your asset** — What you bring.
4. **Their asset** — Why they are the right partner.
5. **Partnership concept** — What the collaboration looks like.
6. **Value to partner** — Commercial, strategic, brand, data, or customer value.
7. **Value to end customer** — Why the market benefits.
8. **Pilot plan** — Low-risk first step.
9. **Success metrics** — How both sides measure progress.
10. **Ask** — Decision, resources, access, or next meeting.

## Executive Proposal Deck

Use when asking internal or client executives to approve a plan, budget, or strategic initiative.

Recommended structure:

1. **Executive summary** — Recommendation in one slide.
2. **Decision required** — What approval is needed.
3. **Situation** — Current state.
4. **Problem or opportunity** — Why action is needed.
5. **Options considered** — Show alternatives.
6. **Recommendation** — Preferred path.
7. **Rationale** — Why this option wins.
8. **Impact** — Financial, operational, customer, or strategic value.
9. **Risks and mitigations** — Credible risk handling.
10. **Implementation roadmap** — Timeline and ownership.
11. **Resources required** — Budget, people, tools, decisions.
12. **Next steps** — Immediate action.

## Template Adaptation Rules

1. **Do not include a slide just because the template includes it.** Include it only if it advances the decision.
2. **Move proof earlier when the audience is skeptical.** If the audience doubts traction, show traction before deep product detail.
3. **Use appendix for detail.** Keep core deck focused; move deep analysis, backup data, and technical detail to appendix.
4. **Match the deck to delivery mode.** A live presentation can use lighter slides; a send-ahead deck needs more self-contained context.
5. **Make the ask explicit.** Every template must end with a concrete next step.

## Slide Content Guidelines

For each slide, specify:

- **Slide title:** A claim-based title.
- **Purpose:** Why the slide exists.
- **Core message:** The one thing the audience should take away.
- **Content:** Bullets, data, examples, or copy.
- **Visual direction:** Chart, diagram, screenshot, customer quote, process map, comparison table, or simple text.
- **Evidence need:** What proof is required.

## Common Template Mistakes

Avoid:

- Starting with product features before the audience understands the pain.
- Using a huge top-down TAM without explaining who will actually buy.
- Treating “no competitors” as a strength.
- Showing financial projections without acquisition or pricing logic.
- Ending with a vague “thank you” instead of a specific ask.
- Making every slide equally dense.
- Copying YC-style structure for a client sales deck where buyer pain and implementation matter more.

## Output Format

When using this skill, produce:

```markdown
## Recommended Deck Template

**Template:** [YC-style fundraising / client sales / partnership / executive proposal / custom hybrid]

**Why this template fits:** [Rationale]

## Slide Structure

| # | Slide | Purpose | Must Include | Optional |
|---|-------|---------|--------------|----------|
| 1 | ... | ... | ... | ... |

## Adaptations

- [How the template should be adjusted for this business or audience]

## Appendix Slides

- [Optional supporting slides]
```

## Quality Bar

A template recommendation is strong when:

- It matches the actual audience and decision.
- It provides a complete but not bloated slide sequence.
- It explains what each slide must accomplish.
- It adapts to the business stage and evidence available.
- It separates core deck from appendix.
