# Negotiation Levers

## Purpose

Guide for understanding what's negotiable in a venture term sheet, what's standard (don't waste political capital), and when to push back.

---

## Negotiation Priority Matrix

### Tier 1 — Worth Fighting For (High Impact, High Variability)

These terms have significant economic or governance impact and vary meaningfully between deals.

| Term | Why It Matters | Negotiation Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Valuation** | Determines ownership and dilution | Anchor with comparable transactions. Present multiple valuation methods. Negotiate pre-money vs. post-money framing. |
| **Liquidation preference** | Determines who gets paid at exit | Push for 1x non-participating as standard. Resist participating preferred or multiple preferences unless terms compensate elsewhere. |
| **Board composition** | Determines control over major decisions | Founders should maintain board control through Series A. Trade investor board seats for specific commitments (introductions, hiring help). |
| **Option pool size** | Affects effective valuation when included in pre-money | Right-size the pool to the actual 18-24 month hiring plan. Challenge inflated pool requests by presenting the hiring plan. |
| **Pro-rata rights** | Determines ability to maintain ownership | Ensure pro-rata for this fund. Resist super pro-rata from other investors unless the fund is providing exceptional value. |

### Tier 2 — Negotiate If Opportunity Arises (Moderate Impact)

| Term | Why It Matters | Negotiation Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Anti-dilution mechanism** | Affects how much existing shareholders are diluted in a down round | BBWA (Broad-Based Weighted Average) is standard. Push back on narrow-based or full ratchet. |
| **Protective provisions scope** | Determines investor veto power over company decisions | Accept standard structural protections. Push back on operational veto rights (hiring, spending thresholds, pricing decisions). |
| **Information rights** | Determines reporting burden | Monthly financials are standard. Weekly dashboards are excessive for early-stage. Annual audit is reasonable from Series A. |
| **Vesting acceleration** | Determines what happens to founder equity on acquisition or termination | Negotiate single-trigger (acceleration on acquisition) or double-trigger (acceleration on acquisition + termination) for founders. |
| **Drag-along threshold** | Determines who can force a sale | Higher threshold protects founders; lower threshold protects investors. 66-75% of all shares is a reasonable compromise. |

### Tier 3 — Accept Standard Terms (Low Impact or Low Variability)

| Term | Standard | Don't Waste Capital On |
|---|---|---|
| **Right of first refusal** | Company and/or investors have ROFR on secondary sales | This is standard and reasonable |
| **Co-sale rights** | Investors can sell alongside founders in secondary transactions | Standard investor protection |
| **Piggyback registration** | Investors can include shares in any IPO registration | Standard — no reason to fight |
| **Non-compete** | Founders agree not to compete during tenure | Standard and reasonable |
| **D&O (Directors and Officers) insurance** | Company obtains D&O insurance | Standard — protects everyone |

## Founder-Side Negotiation Strategies

### When the Founder Has Leverage (Multiple Term Sheets)

- **Play on valuation:** Use competing offers to push pre-money up.
- **Clean up structure:** Push for 1x non-participating, founder-friendly board, reasonable option pool.
- **Add value-add commitments:** Make investor value-add promises specific and measurable (e.g., "3 customer introductions within 60 days").
- **Negotiate timing:** Shorter exclusivity period, faster closing timeline.

### When the Founder Has Less Leverage (Single Term Sheet)

- **Focus on economics over governance.** A higher valuation with aggressive governance terms may be worse than a slightly lower valuation with clean terms.
- **Trade across categories.** Accept slightly lower valuation in exchange for cleaner structure (no participation, smaller option pool, founder-friendly board).
- **Push on value-add.** If the investor wants aggressive terms, demand specific post-investment support commitments.

## Investor-Side Negotiation Strategies

### Building Trust While Protecting Interests

- **Lead with founder-friendly defaults.** Offering clean 1x non-participating, standard BBWA, and a balanced board builds trust and wins competitive deals.
- **Explain, don't impose.** Founders who understand why a term exists are more likely to accept it. "We want protective provisions so we can help prevent catastrophic decisions" lands better than "this is standard."
- **Use side letters sparingly.** Side letters for specific commitments (board observer rights for smaller investors, specific information rights) are fine. Avoid accumulating complex side letter stacks.

### Red Lines (Terms That Should Not Be Compromised)

- **Information rights:** The fund must have adequate visibility into company performance.
- **Pro-rata rights:** Without pro-rata, the fund cannot maintain ownership through future rounds.
- **Standard protective provisions:** Veto over new equity issuance, sale of company, and major debt is non-negotiable.
- **Liquidation preference (at least 1x):** The fund must recover its investment before common shareholders in a downside scenario.

## Common Negotiation Mistakes

### Founder Mistakes
- Optimizing for valuation at the expense of terms (a $20M pre-money with participating preferred may be worse than $15M with clean terms)
- Not reading the full term sheet before signing
- Assuming all standard terms are fair (some "standard" terms are investor-favorable relics)
- Not having a lawyer experienced in venture deals

### Investor Mistakes
- Over-optimizing terms and losing the deal to a more founder-friendly competitor
- Insisting on aggressive governance that signals distrust
- Creating complex structures that make future rounds harder to negotiate
- Not aligning incentives — terms that demotivate founders hurt everyone
