# Occasion Form Selection

Choose the vessel before the words. This skill turns a locked occasion brief into two or three candidate forms, eliminates the ones that do not fit, and commits to one primary form and one fallback.

## Inputs Required

- Occasion type and emotional register (celebratory, tender, solemn, comic, mixed)
- Delivery mode (spoken live, recorded, printed program, framed keepsake, card, gift book)
- Reader and their comfort with performing verse
- Length or time budget
- Must-include details and sensitivity notes

If delivery mode or budget is missing, stop and flag it. Form cannot be chosen responsibly without both.

## Step 1: Place the Occasion on Two Axes

- **Weight:** light → warm → earnest → solemn
- **Delivery:** ear (spoken or recorded) → eye (printed, framed, or card)

Poems for the ear need short lines, strong end-stops, audible structure, and a clear ending. Poems for the eye can carry enjambment, visual stanza shape, subtler sound, and more density.

## Step 2: Start From the Default Matrix

| Occasion | Spoken (ear) | Keepsake (eye) |
|----------|--------------|----------------|
| Wedding toast | Rhymed quatrains (ballad or common meter), tetrameter couplets | Not applicable |
| Wedding ceremony reading | Short free verse, unrhymed or lightly rhymed; anaphora-driven list poem | Shakespearean or Petrarchan sonnet; free verse; epithalamium-style ode |
| Anniversary | Rhymed quatrains; one sonnet if the reader is confident | Sonnet; free verse; year-by-year list poem |
| Eulogy | Short elegy in free verse or loose unrhymed stanzas; elegiac quatrains with slant rhyme | Elegy; villanelle only if the refrain is cleared; memorial free verse |
| Birthday | Light verse in tetrameter couplets or limerick-style stanzas; comic quatrains | Acrostic on the name; light verse; card-sized quatrains |
| Retirement | Comic rhymed quatrains for roasts; blank-verse tribute for earnest send-offs | Framed sonnet or ode; career list poem |
| General toast | Rhymed couplets or quatrains ending on a raise-your-glass line | Not applicable |
| Gift poem | Short rhymed stanzas if read on delivery | Acrostic; sonnet; haiku sequence; free verse sized to the object |

Treat the matrix as a starting point, not a verdict.

## Step 3: Break the Default When

- **The family's register differs from the convention.** A funny family may want a eulogy with lightness. Allow gentle humor in free verse or loose quatrains, but never bouncy limerick meter.
- **The details resist rhyme.** Long names, technical careers, and foreign place names favor unrhymed or slant-rhymed forms.
- **The reader is anxious.** Choose the simplest audible structure, which is usually short rhymed quatrains or brief free-verse stanzas with repeated openings.
- **The budget is tiny.** Under 60 seconds or under 10 lines rules out sonnets and villanelles. Consider a single rhymed stanza, couplets, or a haiku sequence.
- **The requester asks for a specific form.** Honor it if it fits the budget and the reader. Otherwise name the conflict and offer the closest workable form.

## Step 4: Eliminate Candidates

Discard any candidate that:

1. Cannot complete itself within the budget.
2. Cannot hold the essential details without contortion.
3. Mismatches the occasion's weight, for example bouncy meter for grief or grand ode for a casual birthday.
4. Requires performance skill the reader lacks.
5. Repeats a line that the sensitivity notes have not cleared.

## Step 5: Commit

State the following:

- **Primary form** and the single deciding reason
- **Fallback form** and the condition that would trigger it
- **Rejected candidates** with a one-line reason each, so the lead can see the tradeoff

## Form Quick Reference

- **Ballad or common meter quatrain:** 8-6-8-6 syllables, rhymed ABCB. Singable, familiar, and toast-friendly.
- **Heroic couplet:** pentameter lines rhymed AA. Witty and ceremonial, but can feel stiff when spoken by amateurs.
- **Tetrameter couplet:** 8 syllables per line, rhymed AA. Light, quick, and good for birthdays and roasts.
- **Shakespearean sonnet:** 14 lines rhymed ABAB CDCD EFEF GG, with a turning couplet. Suits love and anniversaries.
- **Petrarchan sonnet:** an octave and a sestet with a turn at line 9. More contemplative.
- **Villanelle:** 19 lines with two refrains. Suits obsession and grief, and is risky if a refrain may wound.
- **Elegy:** a mode rather than a fixed form. It moves from lament through praise to consolation.
- **Ode:** an elevated address to a person or quality. Suits tributes.
- **Acrostic:** first letters spell a name. Charming as a gift, and weak when read aloud.
- **Free verse:** its shape comes from breath, image, and repetition. Suits intimacy.
- **List or anaphora poem:** repeated openings such as *I remember* or *For the years*. Highly audible and forgiving for new readers.
