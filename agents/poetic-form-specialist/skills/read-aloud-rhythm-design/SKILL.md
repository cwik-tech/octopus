# Read-Aloud Rhythm Design

A poem for an occasion lives or dies in delivery. This skill converts delivery mode into concrete rhythm and layout instructions.

## Spoken Delivery

### Timing Budget

- Comfortable spoken pace is 130 to 150 words per minute. Nervous readers speed up, so plan for roughly 140.
- Typical budgets:
  - Toast: 30 to 90 seconds, or 70 to 200 words
  - Ceremony reading: 1 to 2 minutes, or 140 to 280 words
  - Eulogy poem: 1 to 3 minutes, or 140 to 400 words
  - Birthday or retirement verse: 45 to 120 seconds
- Reserve time for laughter and applause, typically 3 to 5 seconds per planned laugh line.

### Breath Units

- One line should equal one breath for the actual reader.
- For non-performers, keep lines to 6 to 10 syllables. For confident readers, pentameter of about 10 syllables is fine.
- Prefer end-stopped lines. A listener cannot see enjambment, and inexperienced readers often pause at line ends regardless of the punctuation.
- Use enjambment only for a deliberate effect, and mark it for the reader.

### Audibility

- Listeners cannot reread, so make structure audible through rhyme, refrain, anaphora, or a clear stanza pattern.
- Put names, places, and punchlines at line ends, where the voice naturally lands.
- Avoid tongue-twister clusters and sibilant pileups, and avoid homophones that confuse the ear.

### Landing Line

- Every spoken occasional poem needs one landing line: the line the audience remembers and responds to.
- **Toast:** the final line cues the raise, so it should be short, rhymed if possible, and include the honoree's name.
- **Eulogy:** the consolation line comes near the end, followed by a quiet closing line rather than a flourish.
- **Wedding:** the final line faces forward to the future or to a blessing.
- **Birthday or retirement:** the final line lands the biggest laugh or the warmest turn.

### Pause and Laugh Points

- Mark stanza breaks as beats.
- Place laugh lines at stanza ends so laughter does not bury the next line.
- In grief pieces, place one deliberate pause before the turn toward consolation.

## Keepsake Delivery

### Medium Constraints

| Medium | Guidance |
|--------|----------|
| Greeting card | 4 to 12 lines, short lines, and a title only if the space allows |
| Framed print | 12 to 24 lines, visually balanced stanzas, and line lengths that center cleanly |
| Ceremony program | Under half a page, with a title and attribution line |
| Gift book or engraving | Very short, and every word must be permanent-worthy |

### Page Shape

- Eye poems may use enjambment, white space, and irregular stanzas for effect.
- Keep stanza lengths visually consistent unless the variation carries meaning.
- For acrostics, confirm that the spine letters are visible in the chosen typography.
- Specify title treatment and whether to include date, names, or dedication lines.

## Dual Delivery

If the poem is both read aloud and given as a keepsake, design for the ear first and then check the page. Audible structure reads fine on paper, but page-first structure often fails when spoken.

## Output Checklist

- Timing or space budget, converted to words and lines
- Breath unit rule for this reader
- Landing line position and function
- Pause and laugh point positions
- Layout notes for the keepsake, if one applies
