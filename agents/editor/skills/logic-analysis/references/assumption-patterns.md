# Assumption Patterns — Reference

Common hidden assumptions in business consulting documents by document type. Use during Step 3 of the Logic Analysis protocol.

---

## Strategy Documents

**Hidden assumption 1 — Stable competitive environment**
Documents often assume that competitors will not respond to the recommended action.
Test: "If we execute this strategy, what would our top three competitors do? Does the recommendation still hold?"

**Hidden assumption 2 — Internal capability is ready**
Recommendations assume the organisation can execute the proposed strategy without significant capability development.
Test: "What capabilities does this strategy require? Does the organisation currently have them? What's the gap?"

**Hidden assumption 3 — Market conditions persist**
Analysis uses current market conditions as the basis for a multi-year recommendation.
Test: "Over the timeframe of this recommendation, which market conditions could change? What happens to the recommendation if they do?"

**Hidden assumption 4 — Customer behaviour is predictable**
Projections assume customers will respond to changes as modelled.
Test: "What assumptions about customer behaviour drive the projected outcome? What evidence supports these assumptions?"

---

## Financial Documents

**Hidden assumption 1 — Revenue projections are achievable**
Growth projections are accepted as realistic without scrutiny.
Test: "What is the basis for these projections? What growth rate has been achieved historically? What would need to be true for this projection to be met?"

**Hidden assumption 2 — Cost structure is stable**
Cost projections assume no significant changes to input costs, headcount requirements, or operational complexity.
Test: "What are the largest cost drivers? Which are most likely to change over the projection period?"

**Hidden assumption 3 — One-time costs are truly one-time**
Implementation costs are modelled as non-recurring.
Test: "What ongoing costs might follow the initial investment? (Maintenance, support, training, licensing)"

**Hidden assumption 4 — Timing is as planned**
Benefits are projected to arrive on schedule.
Test: "What is the probability of on-time delivery? What happens to the Return on Investment (ROI) if delivery slips by 3, 6, or 12 months?"

---

## Operational Documents

**Hidden assumption 1 — Change management is frictionless**
Process change recommendations assume the organisation will adopt new ways of working smoothly.
Test: "What resistance to change is likely? From whom? What does adoption realistically look like?"

**Hidden assumption 2 — Systems integrations are straightforward**
Technology recommendations assume data and system integrations will work as planned.
Test: "What are the integration dependencies? What is the complexity and risk of each?"

**Hidden assumption 3 — Current process is understood accurately**
The assessment of the current state is assumed to be complete and accurate.
Test: "Was this process mapped from observation or from interviews with process owners? Are there edge cases or exceptions not captured?"

**Hidden assumption 4 — Head count and skills are available**
Operational plans assume the right people are available to execute.
Test: "Who specifically will implement this? Do they have the time and skills required? What are they currently working on that would be displaced?"

---

## Market Entry / Growth Documents

**Hidden assumption 1 — Total Addressable Market (TAM) is serviceable**
TAM figures are accepted at face value without assessing how much is realistically accessible.
Test: "Of the stated TAM, how much is genuinely accessible given the company's go-to-market capabilities, geographic reach, and product positioning? What is the Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)?"

**Hidden assumption 2 — Customer acquisition will scale**
Growth projections assume customer acquisition efficiency remains constant as volume increases.
Test: "What is the current customer acquisition cost (CAC)? At what point does the addressable audience become more expensive to reach? How does CAC typically evolve in this type of market?"

**Hidden assumption 3 — Competitive response will be slow**
Market entry plans assume incumbents will not respond aggressively in the short term.
Test: "What would the market leader do if this company enters? How quickly can they respond? Does the recommendation account for a competitive response?"

**Hidden assumption 4 — Product-market fit transfers across segments**
Success in one segment is assumed to transfer to new segments.
Test: "What evidence exists that this product will meet the needs of the new segment? What differences in the new segment might require product or positioning changes?"

## Assumption Classification Quick Reference

| Assumption type | Marker phrases | Risk level |
|---|---|---|
| Stable environment | "as the market continues to..." / "assuming current trends..." | High — always flag |
| Internal capability | "the team will..." / "we can leverage existing..." | High — often false |
| Linear scaling | "as we grow..." / "by expanding this approach..." | Medium — check assumptions |
| Customer behaviour | "customers will respond to..." / "demand will increase..." | High — needs evidence |
| Competitive non-response | (usually unstated — look for its absence) | High — always ask |
| Implementation timing | "within 6 months..." / "by Q3..." | Medium — check feasibility |
| Data accuracy | "based on our analysis..." / "the data shows..." | Medium — check source |
