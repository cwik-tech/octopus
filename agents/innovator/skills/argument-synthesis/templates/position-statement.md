# Position Statement Template

Use this structure when formalizing a position for board deliberation or responding to another agent's argument.

---

## Stance

State your position in one declarative sentence. No hedging.

*Example*: "We should divest from the European market within 12 months."

---

## Evidence

List 2–4 concrete, verifiable facts or data points. Include source and recency.

1. **[Evidence Item 1]**: [Specific metric or observation]. Source: [where you got this]. As of: [date].
2. **[Evidence Item 2]**: [Specific metric or observation]. Source: [where you got this]. As of: [date].
3. **[Evidence Item 3]**: [Specific metric or observation]. Source: [where you got this]. As of: [date].

*Example*:
1. Revenue declined 15% YoY ($2M to $1.7M). Source: Q4 financial reports.
2. Monthly churn accelerated to 8%, 2x company average. Source: customer analytics dashboard. As of: Feb 2026.
3. Customer acquisition cost (CAC) rose 40% while lifetime value (LTV) fell 25%. Source: marketing finance model. As of: Jan 2026.

---

## Reasoning

Explain *how* the evidence supports your stance. Name the causal mechanism or logical chain.

*Example*: "These three metrics together signal structural misalignment between our product and European customer needs, not a cyclical downturn. When CAC rises sharply while LTV falls, it means we're chasing unprofitable segments. The 15% revenue decline in a stable market indicates we're losing share to competitors, likely because our product-market fit has eroded."

---

## Key Assumption

Identify what *must* be true for your reasoning to hold. State it explicitly.

*Example*: "This assumes that the product-market fit problem is structural, not temporary. This assumption holds if the misalignment persists despite our recent product improvements; if the redesign suddenly reverses churn, the assumption fails."

---

## Confidence Level

State your confidence in this position as a percentage, and why that number.

*Example*: "65% confident. The evidence is strong on metrics (85% confident), but I'm less certain about whether the redesign could reverse the trend (40% confident). I'm holding at 65% overall because I've seen similar situations turn around with the right product changes."

---

## Strongest Counter-Argument

Before presenting, steel-man the opposing view. Restate it in its strongest form.

*Example*: "The counter-argument is that we've invested $4M in European infrastructure and brand-building, and the market is only beginning to adopt our category at scale. If we exit now, we forfeit the upside when the market matures. A strategic competitor could take our market share and dominate Europe in 3 years. We should be patient, improve the product, and hold our position."

---

## Conditions That Would Change This Position

Specify what evidence or outcomes would move you toward the counter-argument.

*Example*:
- If churn returns below 4% after the redesign launches (90-day period)
- If LTV/CAC ratio improves to 3:1 or better within 6 months
- If a major customer signals credible intent to expand usage significantly
- If we identify and fix a specific product gap that diagnostics show is the primary churn driver

---

## Next Steps (Optional)

If this position is adopted, what's the first decision or action?

*Example*: "Immediate: Notify the board. Week 1: Begin wind-down planning. Month 1: Inform major customers; develop transition support plan."
