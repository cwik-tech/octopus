# First Principles Fool — Experience Scratch Pad

### Session: 2026-04-01 — Deep research integration architecture for multi-agent pipeline template
- **What worked:** Running regress chains on the phrase "deep research" immediately exposed that it was a marketing label with no operational content — the Domain Researcher's findings confirmed this independently. The plain language translation ("agents should be able to search the internet") did significant work: it collapsed the apparent complexity of the architectural debate and revealed that the urgency was partly manufactured by jargon. Naming circular reasoning explicitly (especially the "provider interface should abstract providers → research needs provider abstraction" loop) forced the board to confront that they were debating interface placement without agreeing on what was being placed.
- **What didn't:** The undefined terms audit was thorough but may have been too broad — listing four undefined terms diluted attention. In hindsight, "deep research" was the load-bearing term; the others (integration layer, domain knowledge, works for any domain) were secondary. Concentrating fire on the single most consequential undefined term might have been more productive than cataloguing all four.
- **What noticed:** When a board converges on a debate (sync vs async, provider vs tool vs engine), the convergence itself is a signal — they are usually all answering a question one level above the real question. In this session, the real question ("why does research need to happen at all during runtime?") was never asked until my closing question surfaced it. The pattern: expert boards get locked into *how* debates before settling *whether* questions. The Fool's highest-value move is always to go one level up before engaging the *how*.
- **For next time:** After surfacing the "question nobody answered," push harder on it rather than leaving it as a rhetorical close. The question "why does this need to be solved at the infrastructure level?" had a real answer (fully automated pipelines without human-in-the-loop), but I named it as a possibility and moved on. A stronger play: refuse to let the board move forward until that answer is explicitly committed to as a product requirement. The Fool's power is in blocking progress on unexamined foundations — use it more aggressively at the close, not just at the open.
