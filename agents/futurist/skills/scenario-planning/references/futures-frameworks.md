# Futures Frameworks: Scanning Tools for Scenario Planning

## PESTEL Analysis

Systematic scan of macro-environmental forces. Use as input to Step 2 (Identify Driving Forces).

### The Six Categories

**Political:** Government stability, trade policy, taxation, regulation philosophy, geopolitical tensions, election cycles.
- Key question: "What political changes could reshape our operating environment?"

**Economic:** GDP growth, inflation, interest rates, exchange rates, unemployment, consumer confidence, capital availability, commodity prices.
- Key question: "What economic shifts would change demand for our offering or our cost structure?"

**Social:** Demographics, cultural values, lifestyle trends, education levels, health consciousness, work-from-home norms, generational shifts.
- Key question: "How are the people we serve (or employ) changing?"

**Technological:** Emerging technologies, adoption curves, R&D investment trends, digital infrastructure, automation potential, platform shifts.
- Key question: "What technology changes could disrupt our business model or create new opportunities?"

**Environmental:** Climate change impacts, resource scarcity, sustainability mandates, carbon pricing, circular economy trends, natural disaster frequency.
- Key question: "What environmental pressures will affect our operations, supply chain, or customer expectations?"

**Legal:** Data privacy laws, employment law, intellectual property, industry-specific regulation, compliance costs, litigation trends.
- Key question: "What legal changes are coming that we must prepare for?"

### Using PESTEL for Scenario Planning

1. Scan all six categories for relevant forces.
2. For each force, assess: Impact (High/Medium/Low) × Uncertainty (High/Medium/Low).
3. High Impact + High Uncertainty forces are candidates for scenario axes.
4. High Impact + Low Uncertainty forces are **predetermined elements** — build them into ALL scenarios as givens.

## STEEP Variant

Same as PESTEL but organized as: Social, Technological, Economic, Environmental, Political. Some practitioners prefer this grouping. Use whichever feels more natural for the domain.

## Three Horizons Framework

A tool for structuring thinking about different time scales of change.

### The Three Horizons

**Horizon 1: The Present System (0-2 years)**
- The dominant way things work today.
- Optimizing, defending, extending current approaches.
- Signals: revenue from existing products, established customer relationships, current regulatory compliance.
- Strategic focus: Efficiency, incremental improvement, protection.

**Horizon 2: Emerging Innovations (2-5 years)**
- New approaches gaining traction but not yet dominant.
- Experimenting, scaling, transitioning.
- Signals: growing startups, pilot programs, early adopters, venture investment.
- Strategic focus: Investment, positioning, capability building.

**Horizon 3: Future Possibilities (5-10+ years)**
- Weak signals, research breakthroughs, radical alternatives.
- Exploring, imagining, preparing.
- Signals: academic papers, patent filings, niche experiments, fringe communities.
- Strategic focus: Awareness, option creation, small bets.

### Application to Scenario Planning

- **Horizon 1 trends** are predetermined elements — build them into all scenarios.
- **Horizon 2 signals** are the most useful inputs for scenario uncertainties — their trajectory is unclear.
- **Horizon 3 wild cards** make excellent "black swan" elements within scenarios.

## Weak Signal Detection

Weak signals are early indicators of potential future changes. They're easy to dismiss individually but collectively may indicate a shift.

### Where to Find Weak Signals

| Source | What to Look For |
|---|---|
| Patent filings | Technology directions 3-5 years before products |
| Academic publications | Fundamental breakthroughs before commercial application |
| Startup funding | Where venture capital flows indicates perceived future demand |
| Regulatory proposals | Draft legislation signals where regulation is heading |
| Fringe communities | Early adopters and enthusiasts often signal mainstream trends 3-5 years early |
| Adjacent industries | What's happening in related domains that hasn't crossed over yet |
| Demographic data | Population shifts create demand changes with 5-20 year lag |
| Job postings | What companies are hiring for reveals where they're investing |

### Evaluating Weak Signals

Not every weak signal becomes a trend. Assess:

1. **Novelty:** Is this genuinely new, or a recycled idea?
2. **Convergence:** Are multiple independent signals pointing in the same direction?
3. **Acceleration:** Is the signal getting stronger over time?
4. **Structural support:** Are there economic, social, or technological tailwinds?
5. **Barrier removal:** Have obstacles to this trend recently been reduced?

If a signal scores high on 3+ criteria, it deserves attention in scenario planning.

## Wind Tunneling

A rapid technique for testing existing plans against driving forces:

1. **List the top 10 driving forces** from PESTEL/STEEP analysis.
2. **For each force, define two poles** (optimistic and pessimistic for your strategy).
3. **Score your current strategy** against each pole: +2 (benefits strongly), +1 (benefits somewhat), 0 (neutral), -1 (harmed somewhat), -2 (harmed strongly).
4. **Sum scores.** High positive total = robust strategy. Negative total = fragile strategy. Mixed results = specific vulnerabilities to address.

Wind tunneling is faster than full scenario construction and useful for quick stress-testing.

## For AI Agents

- **Use PESTEL systematically.** Don't skip categories that seem irrelevant — the most important forces are often in unexpected categories.
- **Distinguish trends from uncertainties.** Trends go into all scenarios. Uncertainties become scenario axes.
- **Track weak signals explicitly.** When doing domain research, flag items that might be weak signals and revisit them during scenario planning.
- **Be honest about uncertainty.** Don't assign probabilities to scenarios. The point is preparation, not prediction.
- **Update scenarios regularly.** Scenarios are living tools, not one-time exercises. Revisit when major signals change.
