# Value-Add Playbook

## Purpose

Common value-add strategies organized by stage and sector. Use to evaluate and articulate what the fund brings beyond capital to a specific deal.

---

## Value-Add by Stage

### Pre-Seed / Seed

**What founders need most:**
1. Product-market fit guidance (from someone who's seen it before)
2. First 10 customer introductions
3. First key hire (usually first engineering hire or first sales hire)
4. Fundraising advice for next round
5. Emotional support and pattern matching from experienced operators

**What investors can provide:**
| Value-Add Type | Concrete Actions |
|---|---|
| PMF guidance | Weekly/biweekly check-ins focused on customer development. Share frameworks for testing hypotheses. Connect to other founders who found PMF in similar markets. |
| Customer introductions | Open your network for design partners and early beta customers. Warm introductions to 5-10 potential customers in the first 60 days. |
| Hiring | Help recruit first critical hires. Share job descriptions. Make introductions to candidates in your network. |
| Fundraising | Introduce to seed/Series A investors when ready. Coach on pitch and narrative. Review materials. |
| Operational guidance | Help set up proper legal structure, cap table management, accounting, HR basics. |

### Series A

**What founders need most:**
1. Go-to-market strategy refinement (scaling what works)
2. VP-level hiring (VP Sales, VP Engineering, VP Product)
3. Board governance (productive board meetings, not reporting sessions)
4. Strategic introductions (enterprise customers, channel partners)
5. Follow-on investor relationships (introduce to Series B investors)

**What investors can provide:**
| Value-Add Type | Concrete Actions |
|---|---|
| GTM (Go-to-Market) scaling | Connect to operators who've scaled similar GTM motions. Help hire VP Sales. Review pricing and packaging strategy. |
| Executive hiring | Introduce VP-level candidates from your network. Help evaluate candidates. Assist with offer structuring. |
| Board effectiveness | Structure board meetings around strategic decisions, not reporting. Prepare board materials templates. Bring specific agenda items. |
| Customer development | Make strategic enterprise introductions. Open doors to logos that validate the product. |
| Operational support | Help implement sales process, CRM, marketing automation, financial planning. |

### Series B+

**What founders need most:**
1. International expansion guidance
2. M&A (Mergers and Acquisitions) strategy (both acquiring and being acquired)
3. CFO and board-level financial expertise
4. IPO readiness preparation
5. Organizational scaling (from 50 to 200+ people)

**What investors can provide:**
| Value-Add Type | Concrete Actions |
|---|---|
| Expansion strategy | Connect to operators who've expanded internationally. Share market-entry playbooks. Introduce to local partners. |
| M&A guidance | Identify acquisition targets. Advise on deal structure. Introduce to corporate development contacts. |
| Financial sophistication | Help hire CFO. Advise on financial planning, forecasting, unit economics optimization. |
| IPO preparation | Connect to investment banks. Advise on readiness criteria. Introduce to public company executives. |
| Organizational design | Help structure teams, implement OKRs (Objectives and Key Results), build management layers. |

## Value-Add by Sector

### B2B SaaS
| Value-Add | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Enterprise customer introductions | Long sales cycles mean each warm introduction has high value |
| Sales process expertise | Hiring VP Sales and building a repeatable sales motion is the #1 challenge |
| Pricing strategy | SaaS pricing is non-obvious and has massive impact on unit economics |
| Integration partnerships | API partnerships and integrations drive distribution |

### Marketplace
| Value-Add | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Solving the cold-start problem | Marketplaces fail or succeed based on initial liquidity |
| Supply-side acquisition expertise | Getting the supply side right is typically harder than demand |
| Regulatory navigation | Many marketplaces face regulatory challenges (gig economy, real estate, healthcare) |
| Category expansion strategy | When to expand to adjacent categories vs. deepen the core |

### Deep Tech
| Value-Add | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Patient capital and realistic timelines | Deep tech takes longer — investors who understand this are rare |
| Technical advisory | Connections to domain experts, research institutions, technical talent |
| Regulatory pathway guidance | FDA, DOE (Department of Energy), DOD (Department of Defense) procurement pathways |
| Strategic partnership introductions | Large companies that could be customers, acquirers, or co-development partners |

### Fintech
| Value-Add | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Regulatory expertise | Navigating banking, securities, and money transmission regulations |
| Banking partnership introductions | BaaS (Banking as a Service) partnerships, sponsor bank relationships |
| Compliance program design | Building compliant operations from the start |
| Enterprise financial institution introductions | Banks and insurance companies as customers or partners |

### Consumer
| Value-Add | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Growth marketing expertise | Paid acquisition, viral mechanics, community building |
| Brand building | Consumer brands require different skills than B2B |
| Distribution partnerships | Retail, platform, and channel partnerships |
| Content and community strategy | Many consumer products are content-led |

## Measuring Value-Add Effectiveness

### Quantifiable Metrics

| Metric | How to Measure | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Customer introductions made | Count of warm intros per quarter | 3-5 per quarter for first year |
| Introductions that converted to revenue | % of intros that became customers | >20% conversion indicates quality intros |
| Executive hires facilitated | Hires where investor sourced or connected the candidate | 1-2 per year |
| Time from intro to board seat | Days from investment to first productive board meeting | <30 days |
| Portfolio company referrals | Intros between portfolio companies that created value | 2-3 per year |

### Qualitative Assessment (from founders)

Annual founder survey questions:
1. "On a scale of 1-10, how valuable has [Fund] been beyond capital?"
2. "What's the single most valuable thing [Fund] has done for your company?"
3. "What should [Fund] do more of? Less of?"
4. "Would you take money from [Fund] again? Would you recommend us to other founders?"
