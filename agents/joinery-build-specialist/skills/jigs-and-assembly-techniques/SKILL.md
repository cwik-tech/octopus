# Jigs and Assembly Techniques

## Objective

Make critical operations repeatable and make glue-ups controlled rather than improvised.

## Planning Method

1. Identify operations where accuracy, repetition, or safety matters most.
2. Choose a jig or stop system that references a stable face or edge and prevents cumulative measurement error.
3. Build and test the jig with scrap before using project stock.
4. Mark face sides, face edges, joint orientation, and assembly order on non-show surfaces.
5. Conduct a complete dry fit with clamps, cauls, hardware, and any subassemblies.
6. Prepare glue, clamps, spacers, protective pads, and cleanup materials before opening the glue bottle.

## Assembly Practices

- Assemble in manageable subassemblies when possible; avoid one enormous glue-up unless alignment requires it.
- Check diagonal measurements for rectangular assemblies and use winding sticks or a flat reference for twist.
- Apply clamping pressure evenly and only as strongly as needed to close joints.
- Use cauls to distribute pressure, protect surfaces, or correct bowing only after confirming they do not distort geometry.
- Rehearse clamp placement during dry fit; inaccessible clamps are a planning failure.
- Use story sticks, stop blocks, spacer blocks, and gauge blocks for repeated dimensions rather than repeated tape-measure marking.

## Jig Selection

| Need | Useful jig or method |
|---|---|
| Repeatable crosscuts | Stop block on a safe crosscut setup |
| Consistent mortises | Router or drill-guide mortising jig with positive registration |
| Matching tenons | Shoulder stop and thickness gauge from a test joint |
| Cabinet squareness | Assembly platform, corner squares, diagonal checks |
| Panel glue-up | Cauls, aligned clamp layout, dry-fit sequence |
| Repeated holes | Drill guide or template with locating references |

## Output

Specify the jig or reference system, setup sequence, scrap-test criteria, dry-fit checklist, clamp plan, and final verification checks.
