# Construction Troubleshooting

## Objective

Find the root cause of a construction problem before applying a repair that hides symptoms or creates a new failure.

## Diagnostic Protocol

1. Record the symptom precisely: where it occurs, when it appears, and whether it changes under load or humidity.
2. Check geometry first: flatness, square, twist, parallelism, and reference-face consistency.
3. Check joint fit and glue surfaces: gaps, bottoming out, starved glue joints, end-grain dependence, or crushed fibers.
4. Check grain orientation and movement restraint.
5. Check the load path: identify the component bending, pulling out, splitting, or allowing racking.
6. Choose the least-destructive remedy that restores function and does not compromise movement or appearance.

## Common Symptoms

| Symptom | Likely causes | Typical response |
|---|---|---|
| Assembly racks | Unsquared joints, weak corner connection, no diagonal resistance | Re-square if possible; add appropriate corner blocking, stretchers, or remake deficient joints |
| Joint will not close | Bottomed mortise, debris, swollen tenon, wrong shoulder | Identify interference; pare or shorten only the offending area |
| Joint is loose | Over-machining, wrong reference face, compressed fibers | Shim or veneer only when structurally credible; otherwise remake the joint or part |
| Panel splits | Cross-grain restraint, seasonal stress, undersized stock | Remove restraint, stabilize crack where appropriate, redesign attachment |
| Top cups or twists | Uneven moisture, poor grain selection, asymmetric finishing or restraint | Normalize moisture, flatten if feasible, redesign supports and attachment |
| Chair loosens | Insufficient long-grain glue area or repeated racking | Disassemble if feasible, clean joints, rebuild with correct fit and structural reinforcement |

## Repair Rules

- Do not fill a structural gap with glue alone.
- Do not add screws across grain without considering movement and splitting.
- Do not obscure a load-bearing defect with cosmetic filler.
- Recommend remake rather than repair when the repair would be weaker, unsafe, or more complex than replacement.

## Output

Give the symptom, likely root cause, checks to confirm it, repair steps, and conditions that require remaking the part.
