# Pattern Matcher — Experience Scratch Pad

### Session: 2026-04-01 — Deep research integration layer for multi-agent pipeline template
- **What worked:** The OS I/O architecture match was the strongest structural contribution of the session. It held at all three isomorphism levels (entity, relationship, dynamics) and directly dissolved the board's central "sync vs async vs pre-execution" debate by showing the OS resolved that same trilemma fifty years ago with layered abstraction. The database materialized-view match was nearly as strong — it reframed the three competing proposals (live call, pre-sprint, knowledge store) as complementary tiers of a standard query execution stack rather than mutually exclusive architectures. Both matches landed because they were verified rigorously rather than presented as metaphors.
- **What didn't:** The MapReduce straggler analogy was useful but broke at the dynamics level — the "use whichever finishes first" policy doesn't transfer cleanly because research results from different services are not equivalent. It was worth including as a strong analogy with an explicit failure note, but it didn't add as much structural insight as the OS and DB matches. The session could have pressed harder on the "thundering herd" failure mode earlier — that insight (request coalescing is the primary cost governance mechanism) deserved more prominent placement given the board's cost concerns.
- **Pattern noticed:** When a board debates "which architecture" among several options, the cross-domain scan frequently reveals that mature systems implement all the options simultaneously as tiers or layers selected by policy — not as competing choices. The OS I/O and database examples both demonstrated this. The pattern: boards prematurely force a binary choice that experienced systems have resolved through layering. The pattern-matcher's highest-value contribution in these situations is showing that the binary framing is structurally false.
- **For next time:** Lead with the deepest match earlier. The OS I/O isomorphism was buried in the output after two other matches. In sessions where there is a very strong single match, surface it first to anchor the board's thinking, then present supporting matches. Also: when the Domain Researcher has already confirmed that "the async problem doesn't exist yet" for current APIs, explicitly use that finding to recalibrate which structural matches are immediately relevant vs. which are future-state design guidance. The board benefited from the structural analysis, but some of the failure mode warnings (straggler mitigation) were premature given the current API landscape.

### Session: 2026-04-04 — Cross-border entry into insular relationship-driven industrial market (Ubivis / Michigan automotive)
- **What worked:** The medieval guild isomorphism was the strongest match of the session and held completely at all three levels. Its power came from capturing *both* the formal and informal gatekeeping layers simultaneously — which no other framing in the session had managed. The guild model also explained why cold-email-to-OEM isn't just a bad strategy; it's an action that actively signals credential illegibility and can foreclose future options. The "failure mode warning" about journeymen who spent years in the admission queue while hedge craftsmen built adjacent-market reputations was the most actionable structural insight: it directly validated the Innovator's adjacent market beachhead as the only HQ-independent track. The three-nested-problems closing frame (credential translation + trust-gate sponsorship + principal-agent commitment) was the highest-value structural contribution — it reframed the board's debate from "which relationship strategy" to "which track is available given current HQ commitment level," which is a materially sharper question.
- **What didn't:** The UN/NATO membership analogy was the weakest of the three matches — the dynamics failed at the power-distribution level, and it should have been flagged as "analogy" earlier rather than developed at length. It added value by naming the HQ commitment problem, but the Client Lens had already named that problem independently, so the structural match was redundant rather than additive. The Foreign Medical Graduate match was strong at entity and relationship levels but the dynamics divergence (formal match process vs. informal automotive gates) deserved more explicit treatment — it was flagged briefly but the implication (completing IATF doesn't guarantee a procurement review the way completing USMLE guarantees a residency match review) is a critical failure mode warning that got under-emphasized.
- **Pattern noticed:** In trust-gated network problems, there is almost always a "hedge craftsman" structural bypass that exists outside the gated ecosystem and is executable without sponsorship. The board consistently defaults to debating which sponsorship path to activate rather than asking what the unguilded adjacent space looks like. The pattern-matcher's recurring contribution in these sessions is identifying that the bypass route exists — and that it becomes the *primary* route (not the fallback) whenever sponsor commitment is uncertain. Relatedly: when a board has a principal-agent alignment problem embedded inside a market access problem, the two problem types have completely different solution spaces. The board spent most of its energy on market access solutions while the Client Lens correctly identified that the binding constraint was principal-agent. Structural analysis can surface this nesting earlier.
- **For next time:** When a principal-agent commitment problem is embedded inside the primary problem, name it explicitly in the Problem Shape section rather than surfacing it at the end. The closing structural question ("does Ubivis have a market entry problem or a principal-agent problem dressed as one?") was the sharpest insight of the session — it should have led, not closed. Also: for cross-domain matches, explicitly connect each match's solution pathway back to the specific team members who proposed analogous solutions (e.g., "the hedge craftsman route IS the Innovator's adjacent market beachhead — these are structurally identical"). This cross-referencing would reduce the appearance that structural analysis is separate from the tactical debate rather than a lens that validates and orders the tactics.
