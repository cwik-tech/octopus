# Domain Mapping Reference

Use this framework to systematically map an unfamiliar domain's structure, players, and constraints.

## Value Chain Analysis

Decompose the domain into production and distribution layers:

1. **Raw inputs.** What raw materials, data, or capabilities serve as the foundation? Who provides them?
2. **Processing/transformation.** Who converts inputs into intermediate products? What value is added?
3. **Integration/packaging.** Who combines intermediate products into final offerings?
4. **Distribution.** How do final products reach customers? Who controls distribution channels?
5. **End customer.** Who buys and uses the product? What problems does it solve?

**For each layer, identify:**
- Key players and their market share
- Typical margins (high/low?)
- Competitive intensity (fragmented or consolidated?)
- Switching costs for players and customers
- Regulatory gatekeepers

**Diagram as:** Inputs → Layer 1 → Layer 2 → ... → Customer

## Competitive Landscape Basics

Map market structure quickly:

- **Market leaders.** Who has the largest share? How did they win it? (First-mover? Technology? Relationships?)
- **Challengers.** Who competes directly? On what basis? (Price? Features? Service?)
- **Niche players.** Who dominates a subsegment? Why can't bigger players compete there?
- **New entrants.** Where are startups and outsiders trying to disrupt?
- **Substitutes.** What alternative solutions exist? How are they gaining share?

**Consolidation question:** Is the domain consolidating (mergers, acquisitions) or fragmenting (new entrants, disruption)? This signals growth, maturity, or disruption.

## Regulatory Environment Scanning

Identify the rules that constrain behavior:

- **Primary regulator(s).** Which government body or authority controls the domain? (FTC for antitrust? FDA for pharma? FAA for aviation?)
- **Licensing requirements.** What credentials, certifications, or permissions are required to operate?
- **Compliance obligations.** What reporting, disclosure, or audit requirements exist?
- **Enforcement history.** How actively does the regulator prosecute violations? What are typical penalties?
- **Recent changes.** What rules changed in the last 2-3 years? Are new regulations on the horizon?

**Shortcut:** Read the most recent regulatory guidance or enforcement action summary. It usually explains why rules exist.

## Technology Stack Identification

For technology-enabled domains, map the tech layer:

- **Foundational tech.** What enabling technologies underlie the domain? (Databases for fintech? Sensors for logistics? APIs for SaaS?)
- **Architectural patterns.** Do most players use similar tech, or do they differ? (Indicates maturity vs. innovation.)
- **Innovation battlegrounds.** Where is technology changing fastest? (Machine learning in credit? Autonomous vehicles in logistics?)
- **Technical moats.** What prevents competitors from copying leading players? (Proprietary algorithms? Network effects? Data advantages?)

**Question:** Is the domain constrained by technology, or enabled by it? Constraint = hard ceiling on growth. Enabler = accelerating change.

## Key Metrics That Matter

Identify domain-specific success metrics:

- **Market size.** Total addressable market (TAM)? Is it growing, flat, or shrinking?
- **Growth drivers.** What expands the market? (Adoption? Regulation? Technology?)
- **Unit economics.** What's the typical transaction? Revenue per user? Gross margin?
- **Customer acquisition cost (CAC) and lifetime value (LTV).** Is the business model profitable at scale?
- **Switching costs and churn.** How sticky are customers? How often do they leave?
- **Competitive dynamics.** Is the domain winner-take-all, duopoly, or fragmented?

**Example:** In cloud infrastructure, key metrics are: total addressable market (TAM) growth, per-instance margins, customer retention, and competitive pricing power.

## Constraint and Bottleneck Mapping

Find what limits growth or change:

- **Physical constraints.** Supply of rare materials? Bandwidth limitations? Physical infrastructure?
- **Regulatory constraints.** Licensing caps? Approval timelines? Export restrictions?
- **Capital constraints.** How much upfront investment is required? Who funds it?
- **Talent constraints.** Are specialized skills scarce? How long to train?
- **Network effects.** Is the domain constrained by network size? (Marketplaces? Standards?)

**Decision rule:** Constraints signal opportunity. Can someone remove the bottleneck? If so, they'll win.

## Ecosystem Dependencies

Map who depends on whom:

- **Supplier dependencies.** If a supplier fails, who breaks?
- **Customer dependencies.** If a customer category disappears, who suffers?
- **Partner dependencies.** Which players are intertwined? Could they decouple?
- **Standard dependencies.** Do all players rely on shared standards? Who sets them?

**Red flag:** Heavy dependency on a single player = fragility. Diversification = resilience.

## Quick Domain-Mapping Template

Use this checklist to ensure complete coverage:

- [ ] Value chain: inputs → transformation → distribution → customer
- [ ] Top 5-10 competitors by market share
- [ ] Primary regulator and recent rule changes
- [ ] Top 3-5 domain-specific metrics
- [ ] 3-5 technical or regulatory constraints
- [ ] 2-3 new entrants or disruptive threats
- [ ] Major supplier or customer dependencies
- [ ] Consolidation vs. fragmentation trend

Complete this in 20-30 minutes of focused research.
