# Finish Defect Diagnosis and Repair

## Diagnostic Discipline

Separate what is observed from what is inferred. Record the substrate, finish system, coat order, elapsed time, application method, temperature, humidity, contamination risks, and whether the issue is local or widespread.

## Defect Patterns

| Symptom | Likely causes | First response |
|---|---|---|
| Runs, sags, curtains | Excess wet film, over-application, poor viscosity or technique | Let cure sufficiently; level locally, then recoat thinly |
| Dust nibs | Dirty air, static, poor cleaning, early handling | Level after cure; improve dust control before next coat |
| Fisheyes or craters | Silicone, wax, oil, polish, or airborne contamination | Stop; isolate contamination source; clean and test before recoating |
| Blotching | Uneven absorption, sanding inconsistency, wood figure, glue residue | Correct preparation; test dye, washcoat, gel stain, or toner strategy |
| Peeling or poor adhesion | Incompatible layers, contamination, insufficient sanding, premature recoating | Perform adhesion assessment; remove failed layers to a sound boundary |
| Cloudiness or blushing | Moisture trapped during application or finish incompatibility | Identify system and conditions; avoid aggressive correction before cure assessment |
| Uneven sheen | Uneven film thickness, sanding, rubbing, or substrate absorption | Allow cure; level and apply a consistent final coat or rub-out method |
| Soft, tacky finish | Incomplete cure, wrong mix, cold conditions, excess film, contamination | Verify product and conditions; extend cure before deciding removal is necessary |

## Repair Sequence

1. Stop applying additional layers until the failure mode is understood.
2. Confirm whether the finish is merely dry, sandable, or fully cured.
3. Test correction in an inconspicuous area.
4. Use the smallest repair area that can blend convincingly.
5. Feather transitions only when the chemistry permits; otherwise take the repair to a natural boundary or refinish the relevant surface.
6. Reassess color, sheen, texture, and adhesion under normal and raking light.

## Red Flags

Escalate or recommend stripping to a sound layer when adhesion failure is widespread, contamination is persistent, a mixed unknown finish system cannot be tested safely, or repeated local fixes create unacceptable witness lines.

## Output

Provide observations, ranked causes, confirmation checks, a least-invasive repair procedure, and conditions that require broader refinishing.
