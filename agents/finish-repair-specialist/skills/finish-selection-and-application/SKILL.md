# Finish Selection and Application

## Selection Framework

Evaluate the finish system against:

1. **Use conditions:** abrasion, water, heat, alcohol, food contact, sunlight, and cleaning.
2. **Appearance:** color, depth, ambering, grain visibility, pore filling, and sheen.
3. **Application constraints:** brush, wipe, pad, spray equipment, ventilation, temperature, humidity, and dust control.
4. **Schedule:** drying, recoating, cure time, and available work windows.
5. **Repairability:** local touch-up needs, film-build requirements, and future refinishing.
6. **Compatibility:** substrate, existing coatings, colorants, sealers, and topcoat chemistry.

## Practical Finish Families

- **Drying oils and hardwax oils:** natural appearance and easier spot repair; generally lower film-build and water/chemical resistance than robust film finishes.
- **Shellac:** fast drying, excellent sealer, warm appearance, and repairable; limited resistance to water, alcohol, and heat without protective topcoat.
- **Oil-based varnish or polyurethane:** durable and forgiving brush application; ambering and longer cure time are tradeoffs.
- **Waterborne finishes:** clear, relatively fast, low odor, and durable; may raise grain and can look less warm without deliberate color strategy.
- **Lacquer:** fast-build and repair-friendly for suitable spray setups; demands appropriate ventilation, technique, and solvent safety.
- **Paint:** hides substrate defects and provides broad color control; surface preparation and edge durability remain critical.

## Application Protocol

1. Confirm the entire system on a test panel.
2. Condition the workspace: suitable temperature, humidity, ventilation, lighting, and dust control.
3. Apply thin, even coats using the product's approved technique and coverage rate.
4. Respect product recoat windows. Do not assume dry-to-touch means ready to sand, assemble, or use.
5. Level lightly only after the coat has hardened sufficiently; remove all sanding dust before the next coat.
6. Build only the number of coats required for protection and desired appearance.
7. Allow full cure before heavy use, packing, stacking, or applying hardware that may trap moisture or imprint the finish.

## Output

State the recommended system, alternatives, why it fits, a coat-by-coat schedule, environmental constraints, cure timeline, and maintenance guidance.
