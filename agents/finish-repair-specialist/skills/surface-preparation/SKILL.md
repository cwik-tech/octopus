# Surface Preparation

## Goal

Create a clean, even, finish-ready substrate without obscuring grain, rounding intentional edges, contaminating the surface, or sanding through veneer.

## Procedure

1. Identify solid wood, veneer, end grain, patches, glue squeeze-out, old finish, filler, and defects before sanding.
2. Repair structural defects before surface work. Remove or mask hardware where practical.
3. Begin only as coarse as needed to remove machining marks or damage. Progress through grits without skipping more than one grit range.
4. Sand with the grain for final passes. Keep blocks or pads flat on broad surfaces; protect crisp edges and veneer.
5. Treat end grain separately when even color matters. Finer sanding, washcoat, dye strategy, or a controlled sealer may reduce excessive absorption.
6. Remove dust using vacuuming and a clean lint-free cloth. Use compatible solvents only when needed and allow complete evaporation.
7. Raise grain with water only when using waterborne products, water dyes, or when the finish schedule requires it; resand lightly after drying.
8. Make a full-system test panel before committing: same wood, sanding schedule, coloring, sealer, topcoat, and sheen.

## Inspection Checks

- View surfaces in raking light for scratches, swirls, glue, dents, and sanding inconsistency.
- Wipe with mineral spirits or other compatible preview solvent only when it is safe for the intended finish; this reveals defects but is not a substitute for a test panel.
- Confirm no silicone, wax, polish, oil, or cleaning-product contamination remains on restoration work.

## Common Errors

- Sanding too fine before a stain or penetrating finish, reducing absorption unpredictably.
- Over-sanding edges, profiles, and veneer.
- Leaving glue residue that blocks stain and finish adhesion.
- Using tack cloth residue or contaminated rags where it can affect finish quality.

## Output

Provide a grit progression, surface treatments, cleaning method, test-panel plan, and acceptance criteria before finishing.
