# Sector Profiles

Sector-specific profiles for deal classification. Each profile covers: business model patterns, key metrics, regulatory landscape, common failure modes, and what to look for in diligence.

---

## SaaS (Software as a Service)

**Business model:** Subscription-based software. Revenue is recurring and predictable. Land-and-expand is the dominant growth motion.

**Key metrics:** ARR, MRR, NRR, gross margin (target 70-85%), CAC payback, LTV/CAC, logo churn, expansion revenue, gross and net churn, customer count by segment.

**Regulatory considerations:** Data privacy (GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation), CCPA (California Consumer Privacy Act)). SOC 2 (Service Organization Control 2) compliance expected for enterprise. Industry-specific compliance (HIPAA for healthcare SaaS, PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard) for payment SaaS).

**Common failure modes:** Customer concentration (top 3 customers = 50%+ of revenue), high churn masked by new logo growth, long sales cycles creating cash flow gaps, premature enterprise pivot, channel dependency on a single platform.

**What to look for:** Cohort-level retention (not blended), expansion revenue quality, pipeline coverage ratio, sales efficiency metrics, competitive differentiation beyond features.

## Marketplace / Platform

**Business model:** Two-sided or multi-sided platform connecting supply and demand. Revenue from take rates, transaction fees, subscriptions, or advertising.

**Key metrics:** GMV (Gross Merchandise Value), take rate, net revenue, supply/demand ratio, liquidity (match rate, time to transaction), repeat rate, CAC by side, contribution margin per transaction.

**Regulatory considerations:** Payment processing regulations, marketplace liability, gig economy labor laws, platform moderation requirements.

**Common failure modes:** Chicken-and-egg (can't attract supply without demand, can't attract demand without supply), disintermediation (parties go off-platform after first transaction), low take rates squeezed by competition, geographic fragmentation requiring city-by-city launch.

**What to look for:** Liquidity metrics in launched markets, organic supply growth, repeat transaction rates, evidence that the platform adds value beyond matching (trust, payments, logistics), path to positive unit economics per market.

## Deep Tech / Hard Tech

**Business model:** Technology-driven with long R&D cycles. Revenue may not exist for years. Often IP-heavy with patent portfolios.

**Key metrics:** Technical milestones achieved, IP portfolio (patents filed/granted, trade secrets), team scientific credentials, partnerships with institutions or corporates, grant funding, prototype performance vs. commercial requirements.

**Regulatory considerations:** Varies by domain — FDA for biotech/medical devices, EPA (Environmental Protection Agency)/DOE (Department of Energy) for energy, FCC (Federal Communications Commission) for communications, export controls for dual-use technologies.

**Common failure modes:** Science risk (the technology doesn't work at scale), valley of death (running out of capital between R&D and commercialization), team fragility (key scientist leaves), market timing (technology ready before market is), capital intensity exceeding investor appetite.

**What to look for:** Separation between science risk and engineering risk. Evidence of technology validation (peer-reviewed results, third-party testing). Corporate partnerships that signal market pull. Team's ability to execute the transition from research to product.

## Fintech (Financial Technology)

**Business model:** Financial services enabled by technology. Revenue from transaction fees, interchange, spread, subscription, lending margin, or insurance premium.

**Key metrics:** TPV (Total Payment Volume), net revenue per transaction, default/loss rates, regulatory approvals obtained, compliance cost as percentage of revenue, customer acquisition cost in regulated channels, NPS in financial services context.

**Regulatory considerations:** Heavy and jurisdictional. Banking licenses, money transmitter licenses (state-by-state in US), AML (Anti-Money Laundering)/KYC (Know Your Customer), PCI DSS, SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission)/FINRA (Financial Industry Regulatory Authority) for securities, state insurance regulators. Regulatory risk is existential, not incremental.

**Common failure modes:** Regulatory shutdown (operating without required licenses), fraud losses exceeding reserves, customer trust collapse, unit economics that only work at scale (losing money per transaction while small), banking partner dependency.

**What to look for:** Regulatory posture (proactive compliance vs. "move fast and ask forgiveness"). Unit economics net of compliance cost. Banking/processing partner relationships and their durability. Fraud and default rates with 6+ months of data.

## Healthtech

**Business model:** Healthcare technology. Revenue from SaaS subscriptions to providers/payers, per-transaction fees, risk-sharing arrangements, or direct-to-consumer subscriptions.

**Key metrics:** Clinical outcomes data, pilot results, sales cycle length, contract value and duration, regulatory approvals, payer coverage decisions, patient/provider adoption rates.

**Regulatory considerations:** FDA approval/clearance for medical devices and digital therapeutics. HIPAA for data handling. CMS (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) reimbursement decisions. State medical practice regulations. IRB (Institutional Review Board) approval for clinical studies.

**Common failure modes:** Brutal sales cycles (12-24 months for health systems), regulatory delays (FDA timelines are unpredictable), clinical validation requirements, payer reluctance to reimburse new modalities, HIPAA violations, buyer/user misalignment (the buyer isn't the user).

**What to look for:** Regulatory pathway clarity. Clinical evidence quality. Sales cycle evidence (not just "pilot" — actual paid contracts). Payer interest signals. Whether the product sells to the person who uses it.

## Consumer

**Business model:** Direct-to-consumer products. Revenue from subscriptions, transactions, advertising, or commerce.

**Key metrics:** DAU/MAU ratio, retention curves (D1, D7, D30), viral coefficient (K-factor), organic vs. paid acquisition mix, ARPU (Average Revenue Per User), session frequency, LTV by cohort.

**Relevant considerations:** App store policies and fees, content moderation requirements, children's privacy (COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act)), advertising regulations for specific categories (alcohol, gambling, health claims).

**Common failure modes:** Retention collapse after novelty wears off, paid acquisition dependency (growth stops when spend stops), fad risk, platform dependency (App Store, Google Play, social algorithm changes), inability to monetize engagement.

**What to look for:** Organic growth rate, D30+ retention (not just D1), evidence of habit formation, monetization per user trending up not down, user acquisition cost relative to LTV, network effects or virality.

## Infrastructure / DevTools

**Business model:** Developer tools, APIs, cloud infrastructure. Revenue from usage-based pricing, subscriptions, or enterprise licenses.

**Key metrics:** Developer adoption (signups, active projects), usage growth, expansion revenue, community health (GitHub stars, contributor count, forum activity), enterprise pipeline, migration/switching costs.

**Regulatory considerations:** Minimal for most, but data residency requirements (EU data sovereignty), SOC 2 for enterprise, FedRAMP (Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program) for government.

**Common failure modes:** Open-source competition (hard to monetize what's available for free), platform risk (AWS/GCP/Azure builds competing feature), developer adoption without enterprise conversion, high support cost for developer-centric products.

**What to look for:** Bottom-up adoption (developers choosing it without top-down mandate), usage-based revenue growing faster than customer count (expansion signal), enterprise conversion pipeline, moat beyond current feature set.

## Climate / Energy

**Business model:** Clean energy, carbon tech, electrification, resource efficiency. Revenue from energy sales, carbon credits, hardware sales, SaaS for energy management, or project development.

**Key metrics:** Unit economics at current scale vs. projected at target scale, policy/subsidy dependency, technology readiness level (TRL), deployment pipeline, offtake agreements, carbon abatement cost per ton.

**Regulatory considerations:** IRA (Inflation Reduction Act) incentives and their durability, state-level renewable standards, carbon pricing regimes, permitting timelines, utility interconnection rules, environmental impact assessments.

**Common failure modes:** Capital intensity requiring repeated large raises, policy dependency (subsidy changes destroy economics), technology that works in lab but not at scale, permitting delays, commodity price sensitivity.

**What to look for:** Unit economics WITHOUT subsidies (what happens if policy changes?). Deployment track record. Customer willingness to pay premium for clean alternative. Technology risk separation (is it science risk or deployment risk?). Capital efficiency relative to sector.
