# Reference Check Protocol

## Purpose

Backchannel reference checks are the most valuable source of founder intelligence. Formal references (founder-provided) are inherently biased. This protocol outlines how to find, approach, and extract signal from independent references.

---

## Finding Backchannel References

### Source Hierarchy (most to least valuable)

1. **Former co-founders or co-executives.** People who have worked alongside the founder under stress — they know the real person.
2. **Former direct reports.** Subordinates reveal leadership quality. Look especially for people who *left* — they have less reason to sugarcoat.
3. **Former managers or board members.** People who managed the founder see strategic thinking, coachability, and growth trajectory.
4. **Other founders in the same space.** Competitors and adjacent founders see market behavior, reputation, and competitive conduct.
5. **Investors who saw the deal and passed.** Ask why they passed. If it was team-related, probe deeply.
6. **Customers (current and former).** Especially valuable for understanding founder's sales ability, follow-through, and product vision.

### Finding These People

- **LinkedIn:** Search mutual connections. Look at the founder's previous companies and find people who overlapped.
- **Crunchbase / PitchBook:** Find previous investors, board members, and advisors.
- **Company alumni networks:** Search "[Previous Company] alumni" on LinkedIn.
- **Conference and event circuits:** People in the same sector who know the founder by reputation.
- **Other portfolio founders:** Ask your existing portfolio companies if anyone knows or has interacted with this founder.

### Approach Script

> "I'm evaluating an investment opportunity and [Founder's name] came up as someone you might know. I'd love to get your honest perspective — this is completely confidential and will only be used to inform our investment decision. Would you have 15 minutes for a quick call?"

**Key principle:** Make it easy, make it confidential, make it short.

## Question Bank

### Tier 1 — Essential Questions (ask every reference)

1. **"How do you know [Founder], and in what context did you work together?"**
   *Purpose:* Establish the depth and nature of the relationship.

2. **"What is [Founder]'s superpower — the thing they do better than almost anyone you've worked with?"**
   *Purpose:* Identify core strengths and whether they match what the startup needs.

3. **"What's the biggest gap or weakness in [Founder]'s capabilities?"**
   *Purpose:* Direct weakness identification. Most references will sugarcoat — listen for the *specific* weakness, not generalities like "works too hard."

4. **"If you were building a company in this space, would you want [Founder] on your team? In what role?"**
   *Purpose:* Tests whether the reference believes the founder is in the right role. If they say "VP of Product" but the founder is CEO, that's informative.

5. **"Would you invest your own money in [Founder]'s company?"**
   *Purpose:* The most honest question. People can praise generously, but money reveals true conviction.

### Tier 2 — Situational Questions (ask 2-3 based on deal context)

6. **"How does [Founder] handle disagreement? Can you give me a specific example?"**
   *Purpose:* Conflict resolution reveals coachability and emotional intelligence.

7. **"Tell me about a time [Founder] faced a major setback. How did they respond?"**
   *Purpose:* Resilience assessment. Startups are a series of setbacks.

8. **"How does [Founder] build and retain teams? Would the best people want to work for them again?"**
   *Purpose:* Recruiting and retention ability is a leading indicator of execution capacity.

9. **"Has [Founder] ever changed their mind about something important based on new evidence?"**
   *Purpose:* Tests intellectual flexibility. Founders who never change their mind are brittle.

10. **"What's [Founder]'s reputation in the industry? How do customers, partners, and competitors view them?"**
    *Purpose:* External reputation assessment.

### Tier 3 — Red Flag Probing (ask if specific concerns have emerged)

11. **"Have you ever seen [Founder] cut corners — ethically, legally, or operationally?"**
    *Purpose:* Integrity check. Probe carefully if any other reference raised concerns.

12. **"How does [Founder] handle money and financial pressure?"**
    *Purpose:* Financial judgment. Some founders are great product people but poor stewards of capital.

13. **"Have you heard of any conflicts with co-founders, investors, or employees?"**
    *Purpose:* Relationship pattern assessment.

## Reading Between the Lines

| What They Say | What It Often Means |
|---|---|
| "They're incredibly smart" (with no follow-up on execution) | Smart but may struggle to operationalize |
| "They're very passionate" | Could be passionate to the point of stubbornness |
| "I'd have to think about that" (on investing their own money) | No — they're being polite |
| "They're a visionary" (from a former employee) | May have poor operational follow-through |
| "They hired some great people" (without mentioning retention) | Great hires may have left |
| Long pause before answering a weakness question | The real weakness is significant and they're deciding whether to share |
| "They're really good at fundraising" | Might be better at selling the story than building the product |

## Post-Reference Synthesis

After completing 3-5 backchannel references, synthesize:

1. **Consistent themes:** What did multiple references agree on (both strengths and weaknesses)?
2. **Contradictions:** Where did references disagree? Why might that be?
3. **Missing information:** What couldn't anyone speak to? (That gap is itself informative.)
4. **Pattern vs. incident:** Is a cited weakness a pattern or a one-time event?
5. **Role-fit verdict:** Is this founder in the right role for this company at this stage?
