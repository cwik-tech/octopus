# Founder Evaluation Skill

## When to Use

On every deal where team quality is material to the investment decision (all stages, but especially Pre-seed through Series A where the team IS the investment).

---

## Evaluation Protocol

### Step 1 — Founder-Market Fit

The single most important assessment: does this founder have an unfair advantage in this specific market?

**Unfair advantages (in order of strength):**

1. **Built and sold a company in this exact space before.** They know the customers, the buyers, the channel, and the pitfalls. Strongest possible signal.
2. **Operated as a senior executive in this domain.** Deep domain knowledge from the buy side — they understand the problem from the inside.
3. **Technical breakthrough creator.** They invented or deeply understand the core technology. Relevant for deep tech and infrastructure plays.
4. **Built the same *type* of company before.** They've scaled a marketplace, or built an enterprise sales motion, or managed a hardware supply chain — even if in a different domain.
5. **Obsessive domain outsider.** No direct experience but demonstrable deep learning and customer obsession. Weakest unfair advantage but sometimes produces the most creative approaches because they're not constrained by "how it's always been done."

**Red flag:** Founder chose this market because it has a large TAM, not because they have a personal connection to the problem. "We analyzed markets and chose healthcare" is a weaker signal than "I spent 10 years as a nurse and this problem made me quit."

### Step 2 — Team Composition Assessment

**Key question:** Does the team have all critical capabilities for the next 18 months?

**Minimum viable team by stage:**

| Stage | Must-Have Capabilities |
|---|---|
| Pre-seed | Technical ability to build the product + domain knowledge to identify the right product to build |
| Seed | Technical (CTO-level), product (someone owns what to build), distribution (someone who can get it to customers) |
| Series A | Above + sales/growth leadership, operational management, ability to recruit senior talent |
| Series B+ | Above + CFO-caliber finance, VP-level functional leaders, board governance experience |

**Complementarity check:** Do co-founders have overlapping or complementary skills? Overlap creates conflict and gaps. Complementarity creates coverage.

**Founder-role fit:** Is each founder in the role that matches their strength? A technical founder as CEO can work if they're an exceptional communicator. A business founder as CTO never works.

### Step 3 — Co-Founder Dynamics

**Healthy signals:**
- Clear role delineation (no ambiguity about who owns what)
- Equitable but not necessarily equal equity split (70/30 is fine if justified; 50/50 with one founder doing more is a time bomb)
- History of working together before (prior company, prior job, long friendship tested under stress)
- Complementary working styles (one detail-oriented, one big-picture; one introverted, one extroverted)
- Ability to disagree constructively in front of the investor

**Warning signals:**
- Founders can't clearly explain who does what
- One founder defers to the other on everything (power imbalance)
- Equity split "hasn't been finalized" (unresolved ownership = unresolved commitment)
- Met recently and decided to start a company together (untested under stress)
- One founder does all the talking; the other seems disengaged

### Step 4 — Execution Track Record

**What counts as evidence:**
- Previous startup experience (founded, scaled, exited — even if the exit was modest)
- Built and shipped products at a company with high standards
- Led and grown teams (managed 10+ people through a scaling phase)
- Navigated a crisis (pivot, near-death experience, market downturn) and survived
- Track record of recruiting strong people (their previous hires went on to do impressive things)

**What doesn't count:**
- Prestigious employers without evidence of specific impact
- Degrees and certifications
- Speaking at conferences or being on podcast circuits
- Advisory roles without operational involvement
- Time spent at a successful company without causal contribution

### Step 5 — Coachability and Self-Awareness

**Coachability indicators:**
- Asks investors and advisors for specific input (not validation)
- Can articulate their own weaknesses and what they're doing about them
- Has changed their mind about something important based on evidence
- Responds to tough questions with curiosity rather than defensiveness
- References specific advice from mentors/investors that changed their approach

**Low coachability indicators:**
- Defensive when challenged
- Cannot name a mistake they've made or something they've changed their mind about
- Dismisses competition or market concerns with "we're better"
- Treats fundraising as the primary measure of success
- Surrounded by yes-people (board, advisors, team all agree with everything)

### Step 6 — Reference Check Protocol

**Formal references (founder-provided):** Useful as baseline but inherently biased. The founder chose these people.

**Backchannel references (independently sourced):** Far more valuable. Find these through:
- LinkedIn connections in common
- Former employees (especially those who left)
- Co-workers from previous jobs
- Other founders in the same space
- Other investors who saw the deal and passed

**Reference check questions:**
1. "What's [Founder]'s superpower — the thing they do better than almost anyone?"
2. "If you were building a company in this space, would you want [Founder] on your team? In what role?"
3. "What's the biggest gap in [Founder]'s capabilities? What would you worry about?"
4. "How does [Founder] handle disagreement? Can you give me a specific example?"
5. "Would you invest your own money in [Founder]'s company?" (This question gets the most honest answers.)

---

## The Pivot Test

The ultimate founder evaluation question: "If the current product fails, do I trust this team to find the right next thing?"

This matters because most successful startups pivoted at least once. The current product is a hypothesis. The team's judgment, resilience, and market instincts are what actually determine the outcome.
