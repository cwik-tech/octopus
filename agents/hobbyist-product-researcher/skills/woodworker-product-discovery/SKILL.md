# Woodworker Product Discovery

## Purpose

Turn assumptions about woodworkers into falsifiable product-learning questions. Use this skill to evaluate whether a room-capture, furniture-concept, CAD, or drawing workflow solves a recurring and valuable user problem.

## Segment Before Synthesizing

Do not generalize across these segments without evidence:

| Segment | Typical context | Key differences |
|---|---|---|
| Novice hobbyist | First projects, limited tools, high uncertainty | Needs guidance, safety confidence, simpler choices |
| Intermediate hobbyist | Repeated projects, home shop or garage | Values planning accuracy, material efficiency, adaptable plans |
| Advanced hobbyist | Strong craft skill, specialized tools | Demands control, precision, editable outputs, credible joinery |
| Semi-professional | Small-batch or commissioned work | Values speed, reliability, client communication, repeatability |

Also note project type, room constraints, available tools, material budget, and whether the user builds alone or with help.

## Research Framework

### 1. Identify the Job

Capture the job in this form:

> When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [desired outcome], despite [constraint].

Distinguish:

- **Functional job:** Plan, design, measure, model, cut, assemble, or communicate a project.
- **Emotional job:** Feel capable, avoid regret, reduce uncertainty, trust the plan.
- **Social job:** Produce a credible result, satisfy a client or household member, share work proudly.

### 2. Map the Current Workflow

For each job, identify:

1. Trigger and project context.
2. Information sources: sketches, tape measures, photos, videos, plans, software, forums, or advice.
3. Key decisions and handoffs.
4. Workarounds and their time, money, uncertainty, or error cost.
5. Failure points and recovery methods.
6. The moment when an inaccurate or unclear output becomes costly.

Prioritize observed behavior and concrete past examples over feature requests.

### 3. Evaluate Product Fit

For each proposed capability, complete this matrix:

| Capability | User problem solved | Existing workaround | Required trust condition | Failure consequence | Evidence needed |

A capability is not validated merely because it is technically possible. It must reduce a meaningful burden for a defined segment without adding unacceptable complexity or risk.

## Prototype Testing Protocol

Test behavior, not praise.

1. Recruit users with a current or recent furniture project matching the target use case.
2. Give them a realistic task, such as capturing a room, selecting a concept, adapting dimensions, or preparing a cut plan.
3. Observe completion, confusion, edits, verification behavior, and points of abandonment.
4. Ask participants to compare the prototype with their actual current method.
5. Test whether they would rely on the output before buying material or making irreversible cuts.
6. Record adoption barriers and the minimum evidence needed to overcome them.

### Essential Measures

- Task completion and time to a usable output.
- Accuracy or confidence thresholds required for the decision.
- Number and type of manual corrections.
- Whether the user understands what to verify.
- Whether the output fits tools, skill, space, and material constraints.
- Stated and behaviorally anchored willingness to pay.

## Willingness-to-Pay Assessment

Anchor payment evidence in alternatives and avoided losses:

- Current spending on plans, software, design help, mistakes, materials, or rework.
- Time saved in measurement, planning, revision, or client communication.
- Increased confidence before purchasing expensive material.
- Value of fewer fitting errors, wasted boards, or abandoned projects.
- Whether value is episodic per project or recurring across a project pipeline.

Classify signals:

| Signal | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Compliment or feature request | Interest only; weak payment evidence |
| Says they would pay | Hypothesis; ask price, alternative, and use case |
| Compares against current paid tool or plan | Medium evidence |
| Accepts a paid test, deposit, or concrete purchase option | Strong evidence |
| Repeatedly uses product in a real project | Strong retention evidence |

## Synthesis Rules

- Separate direct evidence, inferred implications, and untested assumptions.
- State which segment each finding applies to.
- Rank opportunities by pain severity, frequency, current workaround cost, strategic fit, and confidence.
- Convert every major uncertainty into a bounded test with a disconfirming signal.
- Escalate safety, inaccurate measurement, material waste, and unbuildable-plan risks as product-shaping constraints.
