# Category Design: Creating and Owning New Market Categories

## Core Concept

Category design is the discipline of creating, developing, and dominating a new market category. The premise: if you compete in someone else's category, you play by their rules. If you design a new category, you define the rules.

Research from Play Bigger (Al Ramadan, Dave Peterson, Christopher Lochhead, Kevin Maney) shows that in most technology categories, a single "category king" captures approximately 76% of the total market value. Category kings don't just win — they define what winning means.

## The Category Design Process

### Phase 1: Diagnose the Current Category

Before designing a new category, understand the one you're in (or adjacent to):

1. **Name the current category.** What do customers, analysts, and competitors call this market? What mental box do they put you in?
2. **Identify the category's assumptions.** What does everyone in this category believe about:
   - Who the customer is?
   - What the customer wants?
   - How value is delivered?
   - What the business model looks like?
   - What success is measured by?
3. **Find the "ceiling."** What's the maximum outcome possible within this category's assumptions? If that ceiling is lower than the opportunity warrants, the category itself is the constraint.

### Phase 2: Identify the Category's "Missing"

The "missing" is the gap between what the category provides and what people actually need. It's the problem everyone in the category has accepted as unsolvable, irrelevant, or "not our job."

**How to find the "missing":**
- **Listen for workarounds.** What do customers do *around* your category to get the full job done? The workaround points to the missing.
- **Listen for complaints everyone ignores.** What do customers complain about that every player shrugs off as "that's just how it is"?
- **Look at non-consumption.** Who should be using this category but isn't? What's stopping them? The barrier often points to the missing.
- **Look at post-purchase behavior.** What do customers immediately do after buying your product? If they need to buy three more things, integrate multiple systems, or hire consultants — the "missing" is in that gap.

### Phase 3: Design the New Category

1. **Name it.** The category name should:
   - Be instantly understandable (not jargon)
   - Imply the problem it solves, not the technology it uses
   - Be ownable — not a generic descriptor
   - Create a mental contrast with the old category

   Examples: "Cloud Computing" (vs. on-premise servers), "Ride-sharing" (vs. taxi services), "Customer Data Platform" (vs. CRM (Customer Relationship Management)), "No-code" (vs. custom development).

2. **Define its boundaries.** What's in the category and what's not? What adjacent categories does it draw from? What does it explicitly exclude?

3. **Identify the category ecosystem.** What other products, services, or capabilities need to exist for this category to thrive? (Cloud computing needed broadband. Ride-sharing needed smartphones with GPS (Global Positioning System).)

### Phase 4: Develop the Point of View (POV)

The POV is the manifesto for the new category. It reframes the problem so that the new category is the obvious solution.

**POV structure:**

1. **The Old Game.** Describe the current category and its assumptions. "Today, companies rely on [old category] to [job]. This made sense when [original conditions]. But [conditions have changed]."

2. **The Problem With the Old Game.** Name what's broken. "The result is [specific negative outcomes]. Companies are [struggling with X], customers are [experiencing Y], and the market is [stuck at Z]."

3. **The New Game.** Introduce the new category. "[New category name] is [definition]. Instead of [old approach], it [new approach]. The result is [specific positive outcomes]."

4. **Why Now.** Explain the timing. "This is possible now because [technology shift / market change / cultural shift / regulatory change]. The companies that move first will [capture what value]."

5. **The Choice.** Frame the decision. "Companies can continue to [old way] and accept [limitations]. Or they can adopt [new category] and [achieve new outcomes]. The choice is between [past] and [future]."

### Phase 5: Execute Category Design

Category design isn't just strategy — it requires execution:

1. **Lightning Strike.** A coordinated, concentrated launch that forces the market to notice. Product launch + thought leadership + event + media + customer proof — all simultaneously.
2. **Condition the market.** Before selling, teach. Publish the POV widely. Get industry analysts, journalists, and thought leaders to adopt the new category language.
3. **Let competitors validate.** When competitors start positioning against your category (or copying it), the category is real. Welcome them — they expand the market you defined.

## Category King Economics

Why category design matters economically:

- **Category kings capture ~76% of total category value** (Play Bigger research across hundreds of technology categories).
- **Being "better" in an existing category usually isn't enough.** The #2 and #3 players combined often have less value than the king.
- **Creating a new category resets the competitive clock.** In a new category, everyone starts from zero. Incumbency advantage disappears.
- **Category definition is self-reinforcing.** The company that defines the category defines what "good" means — which naturally makes their product the benchmark.

## For AI Agents

- **The "missing" is the most important concept.** Spend the most time here. If you can't find a genuine "missing," category design isn't the right move.
- **Don't confuse naming with designing.** Inventing a category name for an existing product isn't category design. The category must represent a genuinely different frame for the problem.
- **The POV is the deliverable.** A well-crafted POV is the most persuasive argument for reframing. Use Argument Synthesis (Claim-Evidence-Reasoning-Implications) to structure it.
- **Test with non-customers.** The new category should resonate most with people who aren't using the old category. If it only appeals to existing customers, it's repositioning, not category creation.
- **Acknowledge the risk.** Category creation is high-risk, high-reward. Most new categories fail. The agent should be honest about this — enthusiasm for the reframe should be balanced with rigorous testing (the "So What?" / "Who Cares?" / "Why Now?" / "Why Not?" tests from the SKILL.md).
