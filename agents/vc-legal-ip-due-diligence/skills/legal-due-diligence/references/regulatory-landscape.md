# Regulatory Landscape by Sector

## Purpose

Sector-specific regulatory requirements, compliance considerations, and regulatory risk factors for startup due diligence.

---

## Fintech

### Key Regulations

| Regulation | What It Covers | Who It Applies To |
|---|---|---|
| **State money transmitter licenses** | Any entity that transfers money on behalf of others | Payment processors, money transfer, crypto exchanges |
| **Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) / AML** | Anti-money laundering, suspicious activity reporting | All financial services companies |
| **Know Your Customer (KYC)** | Identity verification requirements | Any company onboarding financial customers |
| **SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission) regulations** | Securities offerings, investment advice, broker-dealer activities | Investment platforms, robo-advisors, tokenized assets |
| **FINRA** | Broker-dealer registration and conduct | Any company executing securities transactions |
| **CFPB (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau)** | Consumer protection in financial products | Lending, credit, payments |
| **State usury laws** | Maximum interest rates by state | Lending companies |
| **PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard)** | Payment card data security | Anyone processing credit card transactions |

### Regulatory Risk Factors

- Unlicensed money transmission is a federal crime — verify all required licenses
- Regulatory sandbox programs exist in some states but don't provide permanent protection
- Crypto/DeFi (Decentralized Finance) regulation is rapidly evolving and enforcement is increasing
- Lending companies face both federal and state-by-state regulation
- "Banking-as-a-Service" partnerships with chartered banks shift some but not all regulatory burden

## Healthtech

### Key Regulations

| Regulation | What It Covers | Who It Applies To |
|---|---|---|
| **HIPAA** | Protected Health Information (PHI) privacy and security | Any entity that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI |
| **FDA (Food and Drug Administration)** | Medical devices, diagnostics, digital health software | Software that diagnoses, treats, or manages medical conditions |
| **HITECH (Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health) Act** | Electronic health records, breach notification | Healthcare technology companies |
| **State medical practice laws** | Licensure requirements for providing medical advice | Telemedicine, clinical decision support |
| **Stark Law / Anti-Kickback** | Prohibitions on referral fees and kickbacks | Companies working with healthcare providers |
| **21st Century Cures Act** | Information blocking, interoperability | Health IT companies, EHR (Electronic Health Record) vendors |

### FDA Software Classification

| Category | Example | Regulatory Pathway | Timeline/Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wellness (not regulated)** | Fitness tracking, general wellness | None required | N/A |
| **Class I (low risk)** | Simple diagnostic support | 510(k) exempt or general controls | 3-6 months |
| **Class II (moderate risk)** | Clinical decision support, diagnostic algorithms | 510(k) clearance | 6-12 months, $50K-$500K |
| **Class III (high risk)** | Life-supporting software, novel diagnostics | PMA (Pre-Market Approval) | 1-3 years, $1M-$5M+ |
| **SaMD (Software as a Medical Device)** | AI-powered diagnostics | De Novo, 510(k), or PMA depending on risk | Variable |

### Regulatory Risk Factors

- FDA classification can change if the product adds clinical features
- HIPAA violations carry penalties up to $1.5M per violation category per year
- State telehealth laws vary significantly — multi-state compliance is complex
- AI in healthcare faces emerging regulation around bias, explainability, and liability

## Data / AI

### Key Regulations

| Regulation | What It Covers | Jurisdiction |
|---|---|---|
| **GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)** | Personal data processing, consent, data subject rights | EU/EEA (European Economic Area) + any company processing EU resident data |
| **CCPA / CPRA (California Privacy Rights Act)** | Consumer data rights, opt-out, data sale restrictions | California (de facto national standard) |
| **State privacy laws** | Various consumer privacy protections | Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, Utah, and growing |
| **EU AI Act** | Risk-based AI regulation, transparency, prohibited uses | EU + any company deploying AI for EU users |
| **Biometric data laws** | Biometric data collection and use (BIPA (Biometric Information Privacy Act) in Illinois) | Illinois, Texas, Washington, and growing |
| **COPPA** | Children's data (under 13) | US federal |
| **FERPA** | Student educational records | US federal |

### AI-Specific Regulatory Considerations

| Area | Current State | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| AI transparency / explainability | Emerging requirements (EU AI Act) | Tightening globally |
| AI bias and discrimination | Employment, lending, housing (existing civil rights laws apply) | Expanding to more domains |
| Deepfakes and synthetic content | Emerging legislation in several states | Rapid expansion expected |
| AI liability | Unclear — product liability, negligence, malpractice all being tested | Major area of legal uncertainty |
| Copyright and training data | Active litigation, uncertain outcomes | Could fundamentally change AI business models |

## Regulatory Due Diligence Summary

For any deal in a regulated sector, verify:

1. **What regulations apply?** — Map all applicable regulations
2. **Is the company currently compliant?** — Evidence of compliance programs, audits, certifications
3. **What's the cost of compliance?** — Impact on unit economics and growth speed
4. **What's the regulatory trajectory?** — Tightening or loosening?
5. **What's the enforcement risk?** — Active enforcement, fines, shutdown risk
6. **What's the competitive impact?** — Does regulation create barriers to entry (positive) or slow the company (negative)?
