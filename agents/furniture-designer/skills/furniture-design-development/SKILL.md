# Furniture Design Development

## Purpose

Convert a furniture brief into a practical design concept that balances function, appearance, structure, material behavior, and the maker's shop capabilities.

## Process

1. **Capture the brief.** Identify furniture type, intended user, location, contents or load, style, site limits, budget, timeline, preferred material, tools, and maker experience.
2. **Separate constraints.** Label requirements as fixed, preferred, assumed, or unknown. Do not bury assumptions in dimensions.
3. **Choose a construction family.** Select an appropriate approach: casework, frame-and-panel, table base and top, post-and-rail, slab construction, or a hybrid.
4. **Map structural responsibilities.** Identify components that carry vertical load, resist racking, stiffen spans, locate drawers or doors, and permit wood movement.
5. **Choose materials intentionally.** Match species, sheet goods, edge treatment, and thickness to durability, appearance, movement, budget, and available stock.
6. **Select joinery.** Prefer joints that meet strength and alignment needs while staying within the maker's tools and capability.
7. **Check buildability.** Confirm components can be milled, cut, clamped, assembled, and finished in a feasible sequence.

## Design Rules

- Use solid wood where its grain, durability, repairability, or visual character matters; use stable sheet goods where broad flat panels and efficiency matter.
- Account for wood movement across grain on solid tops, panels, and wide boards.
- Size rails, legs, stretchers, and panels according to span, load, and visual mass.
- Avoid combining unrelated visual languages unless the contrast is intentional and controlled.
- Favor modular subassemblies that can be dry-fit before final glue-up.

## Output

Provide a concise design basis containing:

- Purpose, user, site, and style direction
- Overall dimensions and governing constraints
- Construction family and structural concept
- Material and joinery choices with rationale
- Key assumptions and decisions to confirm
- Buildability risks before detailed drawings begin
