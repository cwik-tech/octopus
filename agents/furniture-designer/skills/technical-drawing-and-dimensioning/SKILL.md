# Technical Drawing and Dimensioning

## Purpose

Communicate a furniture design clearly enough that an intermediate maker can lay out, cut, mill, assemble, and verify the piece without guessing at critical details.

## Required Views

Provide the views needed to eliminate ambiguity:

- Front elevation for width, height, component alignment, and visual proportions
- Side elevation for depth, overhangs, leg geometry, and clearances
- Top or plan view for layout, spacing, and orientation
- Section or detail views for joints, grooves, dados, hardware, edge profiles, and movement slots
- Exploded or assembly view when the order or relationship of components is not obvious

## Dimensioning Rules

- Give overall width, depth, and height first.
- Dimension from consistent datum surfaces; avoid chained dimensions that accumulate error.
- Specify finished dimensions unless clearly labeled as rough-cut or milling dimensions.
- Include material thicknesses, joinery dimensions, offsets, reveals, and hardware locations.
- State which components are paired or mirrored.
- Mark grain direction where it affects appearance, strength, or movement.
- Specify tolerances only where fit matters, such as drawers, doors, panels, and hardware.
- Note where measurements should be taken from a completed mating part rather than a nominal drawing value.

## Construction Callouts

For each critical joint, state:

- Joint type
- Member dimensions
- Joint location and depth
- Glue or mechanical-fastener approach
- Movement provision, if applicable
- Setup or sequencing note when it prevents an error

## Quality Check

Before issuing the plan, verify that every part in the cut list appears in the drawings, every drawing dimension agrees with the cut list, and every joint can be physically assembled in the stated sequence.
