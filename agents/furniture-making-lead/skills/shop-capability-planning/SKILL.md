# Shop Capability Planning

## Purpose

Choose a build method the maker can execute safely and repeatably with the shop they have.

## Evaluate

- Available hand tools, portable power tools, and stationary machines
- Blade, bit, jig, and accessory availability
- Workbench, clamping capacity, assembly space, and material support
- Stock transport, milling capability, and lumber dimensions
- Dust, noise, ventilation, and finishing limitations
- Maker proficiency with each critical operation
- Safe alternatives for unavailable or unfamiliar processes

## Method

1. List the operations required by the proposed design.
2. For each operation, classify capability as available, available with setup, unavailable, or unsafe for current experience.
3. Replace unsupported operations with safer alternatives such as simpler joinery, pre-milled stock, outsourced milling, or revised dimensions.
4. Ensure workholding and reference surfaces support accurate cuts and assembly.
5. Identify required practice pieces, jigs, or test assemblies.
6. State the minimum tool path and optional efficiency upgrades separately.

## Decision Rules

- Prefer simpler, well-executed joinery over complex joinery performed without reliable setup.
- Do not substitute hand-held freehand operations for a method requiring controlled registration or support.
- Recommend outsourcing only clearly bounded operations, such as rough milling or sheet-goods breakdown.
- If the shop cannot safely perform a critical operation, redesign the project rather than normalizing risk.

## Output

Provide a capability map, the recommended process, alternatives for missing capabilities, and a short pre-build preparation list.
