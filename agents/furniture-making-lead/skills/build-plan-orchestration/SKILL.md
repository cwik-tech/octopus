# Build Plan Orchestration

## Purpose

Produce a build sequence that protects accuracy, accommodates wood movement, and avoids irreversible mistakes.

## Planning Sequence

1. **Confirm design:** finalize overall dimensions, critical clearances, hardware, grain direction, and wood-movement strategy.
2. **Prepare materials:** acquire extra stock for defects and test pieces; acclimate and inspect lumber.
3. **Make a cut and part plan:** identify finished dimensions, rough dimensions, grain orientation, matched parts, and reference faces.
4. **Mill and machine in dependency order:** square stock, produce repeatable parts, cut joinery, and avoid final sizing until mating parts are verified.
5. **Test before commitment:** make samples for critical joints, hardware, profiles, and finish schedules.
6. **Dry fit:** verify square, flatness, reveal consistency, moving-part clearance, and clamp strategy.
7. **Assemble:** stage clamps, cauls, glue, alignment aids, and cleanup tools before glue-up.
8. **Prepare and finish:** complete sanding and edge treatment; use finish samples; respect cure time before hardware and use.
9. **Inspect:** check function, stability, surfaces, hardware, and fit in the intended environment.

## Coordination Rules

- Surface design decisions that alter other steps, especially hardware, drawer sizing, panel construction, and finish selection.
- Put high-risk or hard-to-reverse tasks behind a measurement, mockup, or dry-fit gate.
- Separate mandatory steps from optional aesthetic upgrades.
- Include hold points where the maker should stop and reassess rather than push forward.

## Output Format

For each phase provide: objective, inputs, key operations, quality check, risks, and the next decision gate.
