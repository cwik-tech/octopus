# Project Risk Review

## Purpose

Find the failures most likely to waste material, compromise safety, or prevent the piece from functioning before they occur.

## Review Areas

### Safety

Check workholding, blade and bit suitability, machine setup, kickback exposure, dust and respiratory controls, chemical ventilation, lifting, and clamp pressure. Stop any plan that depends on unsafe improvisation.

### Structure and Movement

Check load paths, racking resistance, joint orientation, fastener choice, panel construction, grain direction, seasonal wood movement, and attachment methods that permit movement where needed.

### Fit and Function

Check room access, final dimensions, level and floor variation, door or drawer clearance, hardware templates, mating parts, ergonomics, and installation conditions.

### Sequence and Recovery

Check reference-face discipline, part labeling, duplicate-part consistency, order of milling, glue-up staging, masking needs, and whether a test piece or dry fit is required.

### Finish

Check species and grain behavior, glue contamination, sanding progression, stain or dye tests, topcoat compatibility, cure time, ventilation, and handling protection.

## Method

1. List the five to ten highest-impact project risks.
2. Rate each by likelihood and consequence.
3. Specify one preventive control and one recovery path where feasible.
4. Escalate any risk that could cause injury, structural failure, or an unusable piece.
5. Convert the result into hold points in the build plan.

## Output

Use a table with risk, trigger, consequence, prevention, recovery, and decision owner. End with the conditions that must be checked before cutting, glue-up, and finishing.
