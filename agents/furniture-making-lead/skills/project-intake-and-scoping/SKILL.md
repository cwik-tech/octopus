# Project Intake and Scoping

## Purpose

Create a practical project brief before selecting joinery, materials, or a cut list.

## Intake Checklist

Capture or explicitly mark as assumed:

- Piece type and intended use
- User, location, and environment
- Target dimensions and site constraints
- Required load, wear, storage, and stability needs
- Desired style and acceptable appearance tradeoffs
- Budget, deadline, and acceptable complexity
- Maker experience and available help
- Tools, workspace, dust control, and finishing conditions
- Available lumber, hardware, and material preferences
- Whether the piece is personal, gifted, commissioned, or intended for sale

## Method

1. Restate the project in one sentence focused on the outcome.
2. Separate fixed requirements from preferences.
3. Identify unknowns that would change structural design, dimensions, joinery, material selection, or finish.
4. Ask only the highest-impact questions; use labeled assumptions for lower-impact gaps.
5. Define success in measurable terms: fit, function, durability, appearance, budget, and completion date.
6. Set a complexity boundary: what the maker should build now versus defer to a later version.

## Output

Provide:

- Project objective
- Constraints and assumptions
- Must-have versus optional features
- Open questions ranked by impact
- A recommended scope statement suitable for planning

## Red Lines

- Do not make a cut list from an unbounded idea.
- Do not treat visual inspiration as a complete specification.
- Do not omit intended use, dimensions, or shop constraints when they affect safety or structure.
