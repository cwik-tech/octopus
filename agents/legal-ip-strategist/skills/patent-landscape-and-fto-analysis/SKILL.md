## Patent Landscape and FTO Analysis

### Purpose

Identify existing patents in modular wall, interior partition, and building system design space. Search US and international databases (USPTO, EPO, WIPO) for patents that overlap with your design. Map the competitive IP landscape and flag freedom-to-operate risks before design locks in.

### When to Use

- Before design decisions commit to a specific interface, material, or assembly method
- Before entering a new market segment (e.g., healthcare, education, hospitality)
- When assessing competitor products for potential infringement or design-around opportunities
- Quarterly or annually to monitor new patent filings in the space

### Process

**1. Define Search Scope**

- Identify the core innovation in your product: interface/connector design, assembly method, material composition, load-bearing mechanism, modular unit configuration, fire rating approach, or acoustic treatment.
- Define the geographic markets you care about: US (primary), EU (EPO), or global (WIPO).
- Set a time window: last 10 years (most relevant) or last 20 years (comprehensive).

**2. Build Search Queries**

Search USPTO, EPO, and WIPO databases using combinations of:

- **Technology keywords:** modular wall, movable partition, interior partition system, demountable wall, panel system, interface connector, assembly joint, quick-release, magnetic connector, interlocking mechanism
- **Market keywords:** office partition, healthcare facility, modular construction, prefabricated building component, interior fit-out
- **Competitor names:** Steelcase, Herman Miller, Knoll, Haworth, Interface, Clestra, Partitions, Dirtt, Expansion
- **IPC/CPC classification codes:** A47B88 (partitions, screens), E04B1 (building construction), E06B (shutters, awnings, partitions)

Run searches on:
- USPTO.gov (full-text patent search, classification browse)
- EPO espacenet.com (free international patent search)
- WIPO patentscope.wipo.int (global patent search)

**3. Review High-Relevance Patents**

For each patent that appears relevant:

- **Read the abstract and claims** first. Understand what the patent actually covers (broad concept vs specific implementation).
- **Map claims to your design:** Does your design use the same method, material, configuration, or interface? Or is it substantively different?
- **Check claim expiration date:** Active patents (not yet expired) present infringement risk. Expired patents define prior art but don't block your use.
- **Assess claim breadth:** Narrow claims (e.g., "magnetic connector with spring retention") are easier to design around. Broad claims (e.g., "modular partition system") are harder.

**4. Classify Freedom-to-Operate Risk**

For patents that seem close to your design, rate the risk:

- **HIGH RISK:** Claims directly describe your design method, interface, material, or assembly approach. Infringement appears likely.
- **MEDIUM RISK:** Claims are adjacent to your design; design-around possible but requires material or method changes.
- **LOW RISK:** Claims are in a different technology area or cover a different problem. Unlikely to block your design.
- **EXPIRED/INVALID:** Patent has expired, been invalidated, or abandoned. Prior art but not an infringement risk.

**5. Map Competitive Landscape**

- List the top 10-15 modular wall/partition companies and their patent portfolios.
- Summarize their core patents (what they protect, claim dates, expiration dates).
- Identify design clusters: Are most competitors using similar interfaces, materials, or methods? Or is the space fragmented?
- Note any gaps: Is there a design approach no one has patented?

**6. Summarize Findings**

Deliverable: FTO Analysis Report

- **Patent Summary Table:** Patent ID | Title | Assignee | Issue Date | Expiration | Core Claim | Relevance to Your Design | Risk Level
- **High-Risk Infringement Vectors:** Specific patents and claims that directly describe elements of your design.
- **Design-Around Opportunities:** Patents where a material change or method variation would avoid infringement.
- **Competitive Landscape Map:** Summary of top competitors' IP strategies and coverage gaps.
- **Recommended Actions:** Patent counsel review, potential design modifications, patent licensing opportunities, or design-freedom assertions.

### Evidence Quality

**Strong analysis includes:**
- Actual patent titles and claim language (not paraphrased)
- Published patent numbers and issue dates
- Citations to specific claims that conflict or align
- Competitor patent portfolio summary (5+ patents per competitor)
- Expiration dates for all active patents

**Weak analysis:**
- Paraphrased claims without citation
- No competitor patent data
- Opinions about infringement risk without claim mapping
- Outdated patent search (older than 2015)

### Tools and Resources

- **USPTO:** www.uspto.gov (full-text search, classification browse, patent term calculator)
- **EPO Espacenet:** espacenet.com (global patent search, CPC classification)
- **WIPO PatentScope:** patentscope.wipo.int (international filings, PCT search)
- **Google Patents:** patents.google.com (user-friendly interface, citation tracking)
- **LexisNexis:** Patent search with legal analysis (paid, but more sophisticated filtering)

### Typical Deliverable Length

500–1500 words for a single product design. 2000–4000 words for a comprehensive competitor landscape review.
