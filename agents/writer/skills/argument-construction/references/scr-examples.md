# SCR Examples — Reference

Annotated examples of the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) framework applied in business consulting documents.

---

## What Good SCR Looks Like

### Example 1 — Strategic Recommendation

**Situation:**
> Acme Corporation has grown its mid-market customer base by 34% over the past three years, establishing a strong position in the £50K-£200K annual contract value segment. The sales team of 22 account executives consistently exceeds quota, and net revenue retention has held above 110% for six consecutive quarters.

**Complication:**
> However, three factors are compressing future growth. First, mid-market saturation — the total addressable market (TAM) in the UK mid-market is estimated at 4,200 companies, of which Acme has penetrated 31%, approaching a natural ceiling. Second, increasing competitive pressure from two well-funded entrants who have undercut on price by 20-25%, eroding new logo conversion rates from 28% to 19% over 18 months. Third, the highest-value customers — those with contracts above £150K — are consistently requesting enterprise-grade features the current product does not support, resulting in 12% annual churn in that cohort.

**Resolution:**
> Acme should initiate a deliberate move upmarket into the enterprise segment (£500K+ Annual Contract Value (ACV)), targeting the top 800 UK enterprises in three core verticals. This requires a focused product investment of approximately £1.8M over 12 months to close four critical feature gaps, alongside hiring a dedicated enterprise sales team of 6 account executives with specific enterprise experience. The modelled outcome is a £4.2M increase in Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) within 24 months, with improved gross margin due to the higher ACV.

**Why this works:**
- Situation is 3 sentences — establishes the baseline without overstaying
- Complication is specific: three named pressures with data for each
- Resolution directly addresses all three pressures: product investment (feature gap), sales team (enterprise motion), with a quantified outcome
- No hedge language — states the recommendation directly

### Example 2 — Project Proposal

**Situation:**
> The operations team currently processes approximately 1,200 supplier invoices per month using a manual review and approval workflow. Each invoice requires an average of 47 minutes of staff time, distributed across three approval stages. The current process costs an estimated £340K annually in staff time and generates a 4.2-day average payment cycle.

**Complication:**
> Two developments make the current system untenable. The finance director has committed to reducing the payment cycle to under 24 hours to qualify for early payment discounts worth an estimated £180K annually. Simultaneously, the operations team is being asked to absorb a 30% increase in invoice volume following the acquisition of Vantage Ltd, which would push current processing capacity beyond its limit within 6 months.

**Resolution:**
> We propose implementing an automated invoice processing solution over a 10-week engagement. The project covers system selection (weeks 1-3), integration with the existing Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) system (weeks 4-7), and staff training and parallel running (weeks 8-10). The expected outcome is a reduction in processing time from 47 minutes to under 8 minutes per invoice, a payment cycle of under 20 hours, and total annual savings of £290K against a project investment of £85K — a 3.4x return in year one.

## Nested SCR

SCR works at both the document level (macro) and within individual sections (micro). A section-level SCR keeps section arguments self-contained.

**Section-level SCR example (inside a "Market Context" section of a larger document):**

> **Situation:** The UK logistics software market has grown at 18% compound annual growth rate (CAGR) for four years, reaching £2.1B in 2024.
>
> **Complication:** Growth is concentrating in the middle tier — solutions priced between £15K-£60K annually — where three new entrants captured 41% of new contract value in 2024. The premium segment above £100K has seen flat growth, with legacy vendors losing share to cloud-native alternatives.
>
> **Resolution for this section:** The opportunity for new entrants lies in the middle tier, where switching costs are lower and buyer sophistication is increasing faster than incumbent vendors are innovating.

## Common SCR Failures

**Failure 1 — The long situation:**
Writing three pages of background before getting to the complication. The reader already knows the context — they live in it. If the situation takes more than 3-4 paragraphs, cut it.

**Failure 2 — The vague complication:**
"The market is changing and there is increasing pressure on margins." This is not a complication — it's noise. A good complication is specific, quantified where possible, and urgent. What changed? By how much? Since when? What happens if nothing is done?

**Failure 3 — The disconnected resolution:**
The resolution proposes something that doesn't address the complication. If the complication is "we're losing mid-market customers to cheaper competitors" and the resolution is "we should expand internationally," the SCR is broken. The reader will feel it even if they can't name it.

**Failure 4 — The buried resolution:**
Presenting background and analysis for 8 pages before getting to the recommendation. Lead with the answer, then support it. Use the Pyramid Principle if the audience is senior.
