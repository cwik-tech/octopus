# Register Examples — Reference

The same content rewritten in four different style profiles. Use to calibrate tone before drafting.

---

## Source Content (neutral facts)

> Company revenue grew 31% year-over-year. The growth was driven primarily by enterprise contracts, which now represent 58% of ARR. However, customer acquisition cost (CAC) has increased by 40% over the same period, and the sales cycle has lengthened from an average of 3.1 months to 5.4 months.

---

## Profile: executive-concise

**Settings:** Authoritative tone | High formality | Short-to-medium sentences | Active voice | Third person | Minimal hedging

> Revenue grew 31% year-over-year, driven by enterprise. Enterprise now accounts for 58% of Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR). The trade-off is significant: customer acquisition cost (CAC) rose 40% and the average sales cycle lengthened from 3.1 to 5.4 months. Growth is real. Efficiency is deteriorating. The two trends require separate responses.

**What changed:** Short sentences. No qualifiers. Final two sentences name the tension directly without softening it.

---

## Profile: analytical-detailed

**Settings:** Analytical tone | Medium-high formality | Medium sentences | Active preferred | Third person | Moderate hedging

> Revenue growth of 31% year-over-year represents strong top-line performance, with enterprise contracts emerging as the primary growth driver — now representing 58% of Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR), up from an estimated 39% in the prior year. However, this shift in customer mix appears to be contributing to efficiency headwinds. Customer acquisition cost (CAC) has increased 40% over the same period, and the average sales cycle has extended from 3.1 months to 5.4 months — a 74% increase. These trends suggest that while enterprise expansion is generating revenue growth, the cost structure and sales motion have not yet been optimised for the longer, more complex enterprise buying process. Further analysis of CAC by segment and deal stage would clarify the extent and reversibility of this trend.

**What changed:** More context provided. "Appears to be" acknowledges uncertainty. Final sentence suggests further investigation rather than stating a conclusion. Longer sentences with subordinate clauses.

---

## Profile: collaborative-warm

**Settings:** Collaborative tone | Medium formality | Mixed sentence length | Active | First person plural | Moderate hedging

> We've seen strong revenue growth — 31% year-over-year — and enterprise has been a big part of that story. Enterprise contracts now make up 58% of our Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR), which reflects the team's effort in shifting upmarket. That said, we're also seeing some real cost pressures. Our customer acquisition cost (CAC) is up 40%, and deals are taking longer to close — the average sales cycle has gone from 3.1 months to 5.4 months. We need to have an honest conversation about whether our current go-to-market motion is calibrated for this shift, and where we can make it more efficient together.

**What changed:** "We" language throughout. Acknowledges effort ("reflects the team's work"). Softer framing of problems ("real cost pressures"). Ends with a question that invites collaboration rather than a directive.

---

## Profile: proposal-persuasive

**Settings:** Persuasive tone | Medium formality | Varied sentence length | Active | Mixed person | Minimal hedging

> The numbers confirm what the market already knows: enterprise is the growth engine. Revenue is up 31% year-over-year, and enterprise contracts now represent 58% of Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR). But there's a cost that isn't showing up in the headline figure. Customer acquisition cost (CAC) has risen 40%. Sales cycles have stretched from 3.1 months to 5.4 months — nearly double. This is not a red flag. It's a transition phase that every company moving upmarket experiences. The question is whether you address it now, before efficiency erodes the margins that make enterprise worth pursuing, or wait until the problem becomes harder and more expensive to solve. We have a clear view of what needs to happen.

**What changed:** Opens with a strong, confident framing. Uses "But" to pivot to the problem. Reframes the problem as normal and solvable. Ends with forward momentum and a hook for the recommendation. Sentence length varies for rhythm and emphasis.

---

## Calibration Test

After drafting your first paragraph, read it aloud and ask:

1. Does it feel like the profile? (Authoritative / Analytical / Collaborative / Persuasive)
2. Would a busy executive feel comfortable with the tone, or would it feel wrong for the relationship?
3. Are the sentences the right length for the profile?
4. Is the hedging level correct? (Count hedging phrases: "might," "could," "perhaps," "appears to")

If any answer is "no," adjust before continuing. Tone drift is easier to prevent than fix.
