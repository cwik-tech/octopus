# Occasional Poem Drafting

A method for producing a first draft that fits the occasion, the room, and the form contract on the first pass.

## Inputs

- **Occasion brief** (from the lead) covers the occasion, audience, delivery mode, target length, and tone.
- **Detail brief** (from the interviewer) covers names, relationships, stories, specifics, and the avoid list.
- **Form specification** (from the form specialist) covers meter, rhyme scheme, stanza shape, length, and read-aloud rhythm.

If any input is missing, draft only from what exists. List what is missing as open questions. Never fill gaps with invented facts.

## Step 1 — Set the tone register

Pick one primary register and at most one secondary register. Each has its own rules:

| Register | Typical occasions | Rules |
|----------|-------------------|-------|
| Tender | Anniversaries, gifts, weddings | Quiet images and domestic specifics. Understatement over declaration. |
| Celebratory | Birthdays, weddings, retirements | Momentum and bright verbs. Lists of vivid specifics. End with a lift toward the future. |
| Humorous | Toasts, milestone birthdays, retirements | Affectionate teasing about habits, never about body, age or failure. Every joke should resolve into warmth. |
| Consoling | Eulogies, memorials, sympathy gifts | Honor the life through ordinary specifics. Acknowledge loss plainly. Do not rush to comfort or explain death. |

A secondary register is placed deliberately. Examples are a single laugh in a eulogy or one tender stanza in a roast-style toast. The poem must return from it.

## Step 2 — Plan the arc

Write one line each for the three beats:

1. **Opening.** Ground the listener in a specific place, object or moment. Do not open with the occasion's abstract name.
2. **Turn.** The shift that gives the poem meaning. Examples: from past to present, from habit to what it revealed, from loss to what remains.
3. **Landing.** The final line or couplet the room will remember. Draft it now, before the middle.

## Step 3 — Draft to the form contract

- Build stanza by stanza. Each stanza must carry at least one concrete detail from the detail brief.
- Honor the meter and rhyme scheme. If the form specification forces inverted syntax, filler words or a false rhyme, rewrite the line instead.
- If no rewrite works, propose a minimal deviation and note it.
- Hold the length. For spoken poems, estimate read-aloud time at roughly 130 to 150 words per minute. Slow to about 110 for eulogies.

## Step 4 — Tune for delivery mode

**Spoken (toast, reading, eulogy):**
- End-stop most lines so the speaker can breathe.
- Put the key word at the end of the line.
- Avoid consonant pile-ups and tongue-twisting names in stressed positions.
- Use rhyme and refrain as memory anchors for the audience.

**Read on a card or page:**
- Enjambment and quieter internal sound are available.
- White space can carry pauses.

**Framed or keepsake:**
- Favor a compact shape that looks balanced on the page.
- Every line must bear rereading for years.

## Step 5 — Self-check before submitting

- [ ] Every line passes the only-these-people test (it could not belong to another subject).
- [ ] No stock phrases: journey, forever and always, soulmate, angels, golden years, two hearts as one, gone too soon.
- [ ] Tone register holds, and any secondary turn is deliberate and resolved.
- [ ] Form specification is met, or each deviation is noted with its reason.
- [ ] Length and read-aloud time are within target.
- [ ] Nothing from the avoid list appears, directly or obliquely.
- [ ] Names are spelled exactly as in the detail brief.
