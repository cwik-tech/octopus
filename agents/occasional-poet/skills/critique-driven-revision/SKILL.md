# Critique-Driven Revision

Revision is where occasional poems get safe enough for the room and sharp enough to be remembered. The goal is to resolve every substantive note while keeping one consistent voice.

## Step 1 — Triage the notes

Sort every note into one bucket:

| Bucket | Examples | Handling |
|--------|----------|----------|
| Blocking | Avoid-list topic present, factual error, wrong name, broken brief, length far over target, tone wrong for the occasion | Must fix |
| Craft | Stumbling rhythm, forced rhyme, weak landing, generic line, unclear image | Fix unless you can defend the choice with a concrete reason |
| Preference | Alternative word choices, stylistic taste within acceptable range | Adopt if it improves the poem. Otherwise decline briefly. |

When the critic and the editor conflict, a brief-compliance or sensitivity note wins over a craft note. If the conflict persists, flag it for the lead rather than choosing silently.

## Step 2 — Diagnose before rewriting

For each blocking or craft note, name the root cause before touching the line. Common causes:

- **Filler to fill meter.** The line has a syllable count but no content. Find a detail that fits, or restructure.
- **Rhyme-driven syntax.** The word order bends to reach the rhyme. Change the rhyme word or move it to a different line.
- **Abstract claim.** The line names an emotion. Replace it with an image from the detail brief.
- **Tone slip.** A joke lands too sharp, or sentiment tips into saccharine. Adjust toward the locked register.
- **Breath failure.** The line is too long to speak, or has a consonant cluster in a stressed position. Re-break or reword it.

## Step 3 — Revise with minimal disruption

- Change the smallest unit that fixes the problem: word, then line, then stanza.
- Rewrite a whole stanza only when the arc itself is broken.
- Re-verify meter, rhyme scheme and length after every change. Fixes often break adjacent lines.
- Protect what the reviewers praised. Do not rewrite lines no one flagged unless a fix requires it.
- Keep the landing line unless it was flagged. If it changes, re-check that the opening still sets it up.

## Step 4 — Hold the line where justified

You may decline a craft or preference note. Give a one-sentence reason tied to the brief, the form spec, or the audience. Examples:

- 'Kept the refrain because the spec requires it and it anchors listeners in a spoken toast.'
- 'Kept the fishing image because the interviewer marked it as the family's favorite story.'

Never decline a blocking note.

## Step 5 — Converge

- On later cycles, confirm that every previous blocking note is resolved before making new stylistic changes.
- Do not introduce new material late in the loop. Fresh details reopen review.
- If the same note returns twice, the fix did not address the root cause. Re-diagnose rather than paraphrase.

## Output — Revision Log

Return the full revised poem, followed by a log with one entry per note:

| Note | Source | Bucket | Action | Change |
|------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Short paraphrase of the note | critic or editor | blocking, craft or preference | addressed or declined | What changed, or the reason for declining |

End with any new open questions the revision surfaced.
