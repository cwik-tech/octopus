# Personal Detail Imagery

Occasional poems succeed or fail on specificity. This skill turns raw interview material into images that only this subject could own, without exposing anything the family asked to keep out.

## Step 1 — Inventory and rank details

List every usable detail from the detail brief. Tag each one:

- **Type:** object, place, habit, phrase they say, sensory memory, story, inside joke, relationship.
- **Reach:** does the whole audience recognize it, or only a few people? Inside jokes that most of the room will not get should be used sparingly, and only if the line still reads warmly to outsiders.
- **Charge:** tender, funny, proud or bittersweet.

Rank details by how precisely they identify the person. Prefer the following:

1. Physical objects and repeated gestures (the whistle before dinner, the green thermos, the chair by the window).
2. Specific places and times (the Tuesday diner, the first apartment on Elm Street).
3. Characteristic words or sayings, quoted exactly as given.
4. Stories, compressed to a single image rather than retold in full.

A short poem usually carries three to six details well. More than that becomes a list.

## Step 2 — Clear against sensitivity flags

Before a detail enters the poem, check it against the avoid list and against these standing risks:

- Estrangements, divorces, prior partners, or absent family members.
- Illness, cause of death, addiction, money trouble, or job loss, unless the brief explicitly invites them.
- Body, weight, age or appearance used as a punchline.
- Religious framing when the family's beliefs are unknown or mixed.
- Details that are funny to the teller but embarrassing to the subject.

If a detail is ambiguous, leave it out and raise it as an open question.

## Step 3 — Convert detail to image

Apply the show-the-thing rule. Name the object or action, not the emotion it proves.

| Abstract | Concrete |
|----------|----------|
| He always worked hard | the lunch pail by the door at five |
| She loved her family | the twelve names penciled on the calendar each December |
| They are perfect together | his coffee poured before he asks, her side of the crossword left blank |

Techniques:

- **Synecdoche.** Let one object carry a whole life.
- **Action over adjective.** A verb doing something beats a trait being claimed.
- **Sensory anchor.** Include at least one image that engages sound, smell or touch, not only sight.
- **Callback.** Reintroduce an early image at the landing to close the arc.

## Step 4 — Guard against invention

- Use only facts present in the brief. You may shape and compress them, but do not add events, dialogue, dates or relationships.
- Metaphor is allowed. False fact is not. 'Her laugh was a screen door banging open' is fine. 'She laughed at her sister's wedding in 1972' is only fine if the brief says so.
- When a strong image needs one more fact, write a targeted open question instead. Example: 'What color was the boat?'

## Output

For each detail used, record:

- The detail as given.
- The image it became.
- The stanza where it appears.

Also list each detail omitted, with its reason.
