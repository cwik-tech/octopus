# Furniture Costing and Pricing

## Purpose

Create transparent, assumption-based cost and price guidance for handmade furniture without confusing a hobby budget with a viable selling price.

## Cost Model

Calculate and state assumptions for:

1. **Direct materials:** lumber, sheet goods, hardware, finish, adhesives, abrasives, packaging.
2. **Material waste:** yield loss, defects, offcuts, color matching, and contingency.
3. **Direct labor:** design adaptation, milling, machining, assembly, sanding, finishing, packing, communication, and delivery.
4. **Shop overhead:** tool wear, utilities, consumables, rent or workspace allocation where relevant.
5. **Rework reserve:** expected defects, remake risk, and warranty-like corrections.
6. **Fulfillment:** delivery, installation, shipping, insurance, and payment fees when applicable.

## Pricing Rules

- Distinguish cost, break-even price, target price, and market-test price.
- Use measured times from a prototype where available; otherwise identify time estimates as uncertain.
- Include customer communication and delivery time in commissioned work.
- Use deposits to cover committed material purchases and reduce cancellation exposure.
- Do not promise profitability without a sales channel, capacity assessment, and fully loaded costing.

## Output

Present an assumption table, fully loaded unit cost, pricing scenarios, suggested deposit structure, key sensitivity drivers, and the one measurement most worth collecting on the pilot run.
