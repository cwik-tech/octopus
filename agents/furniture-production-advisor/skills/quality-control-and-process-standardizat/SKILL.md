# Quality Control and Process Standardization

## Purpose

Make quality repeatable by defining what good looks like, where defects are caught, and how the build process is documented.

## Method

1. Define critical-to-quality characteristics: final dimensions, squareness, stability, joint fit, surface quality, finish appearance, hardware function, and safety.
2. Classify each criterion as critical, major, or cosmetic.
3. Set an inspection point before a defect becomes expensive to correct.
4. Create a simple build record: material batch, part labels, jig settings, finish system, inspection result, and rework notes.
5. Establish rework rules: repair, remake, accept with disclosure, or reject.
6. Feed recurring defects back into design changes, jigs, work instructions, or material selection.

## Checkpoint Principles

- Inspect stock quality before cutting.
- Verify dimensions and joinery before glue-up.
- Test stability and hardware before finishing.
- Inspect surfaces under appropriate light before final finish coats.
- Conduct final functional and cosmetic inspection before packing or handoff.

## Output

Produce a concise quality plan listing criterion, acceptable standard, inspection stage, method, owner, and corrective action. Highlight the three failures most likely to reach the customer.
