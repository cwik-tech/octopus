# Business Model Canvas

## Purpose

Decompose any venture or business idea into the nine standard building blocks of a business model. This skill surfaces gaps, contradictions, and unstated assumptions across all dimensions of the model, not just revenue or product.

## When to Use

Use this skill at the start of every business model engagement. It is your primary structuring tool. Use it again if the venture pivots, enters a new market, or changes its revenue model.

## Process

### Step 1: Define the Canvas Blocks

Take the venture brief and populate each of the nine blocks. For each block, write a 1-3 sentence description. If the brief does not provide enough information for a block, mark it as **Assumption (A)** and state the assumption explicitly.

| # | Block | Description | Status (E/A) |
|---|-------|-------------|--------------|
| 1 | **Customer Segments** | Who are the customers? Mass market, niche, segmented, diversified, multi-sided? | |
| 2 | **Value Proposition** | What problem do you solve or need do you fulfill? What bundle of products/services creates value? | |
| 3 | **Channels** | How do you reach customers? Awareness, evaluation, purchase, delivery, after-sales? | |
| 4 | **Customer Relationships** | How do you acquire, retain, and grow customers? Personal assistance, self-service, automated, communities, co-creation? | |
| 5 | **Revenue Streams** | What are customers paying for? Asset sale, subscription, usage fee, licensing, advertising, brokerage? Fixed or dynamic pricing? | |
| 6 | **Key Resources** | What physical, intellectual, human, or financial assets are essential? | |
| 7 | **Key Activities** | What does the venture do to deliver the value proposition — production, problem-solving, platform/network? | |
| 8 | **Key Partnerships** | Who are the suppliers, partners, or strategic alliances needed? Buyer-supplier, strategic alliance, joint venture? | |
| 9 | **Cost Structure** | What are the most important costs? Fixed vs variable, economies of scale vs scope? | |

### Step 2: Identify Interdependencies

Mark the arrows between blocks. The most critical linkages are:

- **Value Proposition ↔ Customer Segments:** Does this value proposition actually matter to this segment?
- **Revenue Streams ↔ Value Proposition:** Are customers willing to pay for this specific value?
- **Key Resources ↔ Key Activities:** Does the venture have (or can it get) the resources to perform the key activities?
- **Cost Structure ↔ Revenue Streams:** Do the unit economics work?

### Step 3: Assess Completeness per Block

For each block, rate the venture's clarity on a scale:

- **Clear (C):** The brief and available evidence support a specific description. Low risk of misunderstanding.
- **Assumption (A):** The description is inferred, guessed, or based on founder belief. High risk. Must be surfaced as an assumption.
- **Empty (E):** No information available to populate this block. Critical gap.

### Step 4: Output

Produce a filled nine-block table with status markings and a summary of the three weakest blocks (those with the most assumptions or empty cells).
